# GBP Creation Strategy

**Purpose:** Google Business Profile (GBP) creation methods for building local presence when a business has no existing profile, or when setting up R&R microsites.

**Caveat:** These methods work because Google treats business identity primarily through NAP (name, address, phone) matching. Abuse carries risk — use responsibly.

---

## Method 1: Create via Google Maps UI (Your Own Account)

**How it works:**
1. Go to Google Maps
2. Sign in with a Google account that has a good "Local Person" rating/trust
3. Use the "Add a place" or "Add business" option
4. Fill in the business name, address (yours), phone number, category
5. Verify the business via postcard mail or phone (Google's standard verification flow)

**When to use:**
- Adding genuinely **local, existing businesses that are truly not on the map** (the business is real, operates locally, has a physical location, but somehow has no GBP profile)
- Creating GBP profiles for **your own microsites that cannot get a profile any other way** (e.g., service-area-only businesses, newly built microsites where Method 2 isn't viable)
- Only when you have direct authorization from the business owner (for real businesses) or when you own the microsite

**Important caveat:** Use this **sparingly**. Your "Local Person" rating/trust on Google Maps is valuable currency. Each profile you create affects your reputation standing. Use it strategically for microsites you can't get profiles for otherwise, but don't over-use it or you risk degrading your trust rating with Google.

**Advantages:**
- Low risk when used responsibly
- Builds a profile from scratch with full control
- Works for local service businesses, retail, offices, microsites
- Only method available for some microsite types

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires verification (postcard or phone), which takes time (7–14 days for mail)
- Requires valid phone number at the verified address
- Only one profile per business per location
- Overuse damages your Local Person reputation

**Risk if abused:**
- Mass-creating profiles across dozens of microsites or fake addresses → flagged as spam/PBN footprint → Local Person rating suspension
- Creating unverified profiles for non-existent businesses or without authorization → suspension
- Overusing the method to rapidly scale microsite networks → Google detects pattern, suspends all profiles and bans your account

**Community guidance (Blair):** Use this for real businesses missing from Maps AND for your microsites, but use it **sparingly**. Your Local Person trust is your asset — don't burn it by mass-creating profiles. Reserve it for microsites that genuinely need it and can't get a profile through other means. Think of it as a precious resource, not a shortcut for rapid scaling.

## Method 2: Claim an Existing Profile (Ownership Transfer / Name + Address Swap)

**How it works:**
1. Find a business with **good existing reviews and a GBP profile**
2. Create a new GBP profile using the **same business name but with a different address** (yours)
3. **Within 2 minutes** of creation, go back and change the phone number
4. Google's matching system sees the same business name, notes the address/phone difference, and often treats it as a location update for the same business rather than a duplicate

**When to use:**
- You're building an R&R microsite and want to leverage an existing business's review authority without creating a profile from scratch
- The original business is in a nearby area but not directly servicing the new location (so the address/phone difference makes sense)
- You want to accelerate the process (no verification wait time)

**How Google sees it:**
- If the phone is the only thing that changes, Google may merge the profiles or treat it as a location with a new phone number
- If address + phone both differ, Google may flag it as a potential duplicate or separate location

**Advantages:**
- Faster than Method 1 (no verification mail wait)
- Inherits review authority from the original business
- No need to wait for postcard verification

**Disadvantages:**
- High risk of duplicate/suspension if Google catches the pattern
- Works only because of a parsing gap; relies on 2-minute window before locking
- If both profiles stay active, Google may merge them or penalize one

**Risk if abused:**
- Mass-creating profiles by cloning successful businesses → obvious PBN footprint
- Google detects the pattern (same name, systematic address swaps) → suspension of all cloned profiles
- One report from the real business owner → immediate takedown

**Community guidance (Blair):** Don't abuse this method. It works in specific, legitimate cases (e.g., a plumbing business expanding to a new service area and adding a second location profile), but it's not a shortcut for building microsite networks. Use Method 1 when possible.

## Comparison

| Factor | Method 1 (Maps UI) | Method 2 (Clone + Swap) |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Time to Active** | 7–14 days (verification) | Seconds (no verification) |
| **Risk** | Low (if legitimate) | Medium–High (pattern-detectable) |
| **Best for** | Real local businesses missing from Maps | Strategic location expansion (rare) |
| **Review Authority** | Starts at zero | Inherits from original |
| **Sustainability** | High (lasting) | Medium (Google may merge/detect) |

## Strategic Use Cases

### Legitimate: Add Real Local Business to Maps (Method 1)
- A plumbing company in Verdun operates from a real address but has no GBP profile
- You create one via Google Maps UI with their real address, verify it, and build their GBP optimization strategy
- Risk: Low | Outcome: Lasting, Google-approved presence | Local Person impact: Minimal (one legitimate business)

### Legitimate: Create GBP for Your Own Microsite (Method 1, Used Sparingly)
- You're building a "mold remediation services" microsite in Verdun
- You can't use Method 2 (no existing high-review business to clone)
- You create a GBP via Google Maps UI for the microsite, use your address + microsite phone number, verify it
- Risk: Low if used **sparingly** | Outcome: Lasting, verified microsite GBP | Local Person impact: Moderate (uses up some of your trust budget)
- **Only do this occasionally.** Reserve your Local Person rating for microsites you truly need GBPs for.

### Legitimate: Multi-Location Expansion (Method 2, Rare)
- Your client is a roofing company that operates across 3 cities
- They have a strong GBP in City A; you create a new profile for City B (different address, phone)
- Google recognizes it as a new location for the same business
- Risk: Low if it's truly a new service area | Outcome: Legitimate multi-location presence

### **⚠️ Not Recommended: Mass R&R Microsite Network (Method 1, Scaled)**
- You're building 20 "mold remediation" microsites across different cities
- You use Method 1 to rapidly create GBPs for all 20 via Google Maps UI
- Google detects the pattern (same service type, systematic microsite creation, all under one Local Person account)
- Risk: **High** — all 20 profiles suspended, Local Person rating permanently damaged | Outcome: Loss of all profiles and your account's trusted standing
- **Don't do this.** Your Local Person rating isn't a unlimited resource.

### **⚠️ Not Recommended: Mass R&R Microsite Network (Method 2, Scaled)**
- You're building 20 "mold remediation" microsites across different cities
- You clone the same successful "mold remediation" business 20 times, changing only the address
- Google's algorithm detects the pattern (same name, systematic address swaps across unrelated locations)
- Risk: **High** — all 20 profiles suspended, possible account ban | Outcome: Loss of all profiles, reputation damage
- **Don't do this.** Pattern is easily detected.

## Summary

**Method 1** is the recommended default for legitimate GBP creation — both for real local businesses missing from Maps AND for your own microsites that can't get profiles otherwise. It's slower but sustainable and carries minimal risk when used responsibly. **Key limitation:** Use it **sparingly**. Your Local Person rating is a finite asset. Each profile you create consumes some of that trust. Use it strategically for microsites that genuinely need it, not as a scaling shortcut.

**Method 2** works in specific, narrow cases (genuine location expansion) but is easily detected at scale. Don't use it as a shortcut for microsite networks.

**Golden rule:** Your Local Person reputation on Google Maps is currency. Spend it wisely. Use Method 1 for real business gaps AND for critical microsites, but do it sparingly. If you're asking "Is this abusive?" the answer is probably yes. Don't mass-create profiles — that's how you burn your trust and get suspended.
