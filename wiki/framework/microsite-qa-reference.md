# Microsite Q&A Reference

**Purpose:** Quick reference for 30+ common questions about microsite building, ranking, and local SEO strategy, plus real-time case studies. Compiled from community Q&A.

**Format:** Questions organized by topic; each answer is practical and field-tested. Answers are sourced from Sep 8, 2026 Q&A batch (Sep batch) and Oct 22, 2026 Q&A batch (Oct batch), plus ongoing community contests.

## Keyword Research & Niche Validation

### Q1: My keyword shows zero search volume. Do I skip it?

**A:** No. Every tool pulls the same Google Ads data and then extrapolates, so in a town of 15,000 to 30,000 people a "zero" is a guess, not a fact. Shawn has pages on zero volume terms doing 3,000 views a month. Because every SEO reads the same dashboard, those terms are off everybody's radar at the same time—which is your advantage.

**Key insight:** Tool silence = low competition = opportunity.

### Q2: The tool says 40 searches a month but the site gets way more than that. Which number is real?

**A:** The site is real; the tool is an estimate. One member watched a query his tool priced at 40 pull hundreds of actual users on a client site. Small queries are the hardest thing for these tools to see, which is exactly why they undercount the ones nobody else is bidding on.

**Implication:** Don't kill a microsite because the keyword tool shows low volume. Let Search Console tell you the real numbers.

### Q6: I want to test a product niche before I go chasing distributors. Cheapest way?

**A:** Build about five microsites for roughly $50 total on the product term, link to Amazon as an affiliate, and let Amazon's own data tell you whether you rank and whether anyone clicks. That is a $50 answer to a question most people spend three months and a lot of phone calls on. If it works, then go find distributors with proof in your hand.

### Q7: My research says there is barely any volume for web design in my area. Different service, or different plan?

**A:** Both, and you already found it. "Web design" in a town is a thin term; "web design for contractors" or "web design for HVAC" is where the searches and the money are. Pick the vertical rather than the geography. Remember the back end is where this pays, because automations and AI voice agents sell to the same client after the site.

## Domain & Microsite Setup

### Q5: Do exact match domains work for e-commerce?

**A:** They work everywhere, including e-com. You can run a Shopify buy button or a full cart on an EMD off the same Shopify backend, so you get the domain match and the conversion. The catch is not the domain, it is who you are up against: e-com is an ad arbitrage game and organic sits under the ads, so check how competitive the term is before you build.

### Q9: The domain I want is taken. What do I use?

**A:** In order:
1. Flip the service and location around
2. `[serviceinlocation.com](http://serviceinlocation.com)` — works even if it's not lovely
3. Brand it: `elitetowingcrystallake.com` or `towingcrystallakepros.com`

None of these is a reason to abandon the niche.

### Q10: Do all my microsites need to look different from each other?

**A:** No. One layout that works is fine at scale, because any single site is a drop in the ocean and the person searching in one town is never going to see the one in the next. If it bothers you, run five variants and cycle them.

**What does matter:** Not leaving a footprint. Don't use the same "site built by us" footer on 40 sites, and vary the naming conventions across your portfolio.

### Q14: Can I build microsites in GoHighLevel, and will they rank?

**A:** They rank. GoHighLevel hosts on Cloudflare natively and supports meta tags, schema, sitemaps, and proper headings—which is all the site needs from the platform.

## Google Business Profile & Local SEO

### Q8: Can I buy a Google Business Profile instead of building one up?

**A:** Yes, and it flips the usual order. People sell them in niche Facebook groups for about $200–$300, and abandoned ones can be claimed, so you can choose the niche based on what profile you can actually get. It is a gamble. Shawn has been burned a couple of times and more often they hold. Keep it isolated on its own Gmail and do not go wild with it.

### Q15: A business with 180 five-star reviews and a better site still loses the map pack. How?

**A:** Categories and proximity, usually, not quality. Pull the map competitors with GMB Everywhere or Pleper, match your primary category to what the top rankers share, put that category in the title, H1, and meta, link your money services off the homepage to real service pages, match the profile categories to the site, run a heat map scan to see where you are invisible, then write for those cities and neighborhoods. Reviews are not the lever when everybody has them.

### Q16: How much does the business name on the Google profile matter?

**A:** More than it should. Reviews stay the top priority, but a keyword in the name is heavy—same reason exact match domains keep working. People do legally change the registered business name to match what gets searched. A keyword name will outrank a branded one. Worth knowing what you are up against even if you never do it.

## Backlinks & Citations

### Q11: Do backlinks and citations still matter, and which ones?

**A:** Yes, quality rather than the old volume game. Point local links at your own assets: Yelp, a free BBB listing, Yellow Pages, and the Right Local top 100 list (which you can work through without paying for the subscription). Don't sleep on LinkedIn—it carries enormous authority with Google. One member rewrote his profile around a phrase and ranked for it inside an hour. If running accounts is too much, you only need one persona writing home service articles that link out.

### Q12: Is the realtor niche worth going after?

**A:** I would not. Redfin, Zillow, and Trulia own those results with in-house SEO teams and 90+ domain ratings. Property search is effectively required to be visible, and even a strong exact match name sits at position five. A "we buy houses" microsite ranked and took a few calls, but the volume was not there. There are easier rooms to be the best in.

## Getting Started & Execution

### Q13: I am on day one and I do not know what I am actually offering. Where do I start?

**A:** Build first. If you cannot name your skill yet, the Upwork route is going to hurt because it asks you to sell something specific before you have one. Put up a microsite, learn the thing, and you will have an answer by the end of the week.

**Honest sequence:** Build → learn → then position → then outreach (not the other way around).

### Q20: I am not technical. Where do I begin?

**A:** Any AI will do the tweaking. Hand it the site folder and tell it what you want changed in plain English. Your first site will not be your best one and it is not supposed to be. A site costs a couple of dollars, so build one, get it live, and let it teach you. Build twenty over time, keep the winners, prune the ones that only cost you money.

## Tools & Cost Optimization

### Q4: Is there a cheaper way to get Ahrefs or SEMrush style data?

**A:** DataForSEO is a good solution. There is also an open tool called OpenSEO that you run off Claude Code or self-host, plugging in your own DataForSEO and Claude keys. People are reporting something close to a SEMrush equivalent for about $10 a month. (Treat that as a lead to check, not a recommendation yet.)

### Q19: Is there a tool that tells me if a niche is worth building in?

**A:** The Niche Validator in the classroom gives you a score, and the standing caveat holds: the score is the shortcut, the live search result is the final word. One member built his own on top of DataForSEO that scans a niche and returns "build," "review," or "skip." He ran about 450 niches for around $5 and built on the best gap, ranking in two days. Don't trust any niche validator unhedged—have Claude audit the build after.

## Content & Design

### Q17: Does the site builder leave a footprint Google can spot?

**A:** Less than people fear. Image hosting has changed and forms carry unique codes, so the obvious tells are not there. The footprints that get people are self-inflicted: the same footer credit on 40 sites, the same naming pattern across a portfolio. Vary those deliberately.

### Q18: Is a resources section worth adding to a microsite?

**A:** One member added a resources section that answers what searchers actually ask and is seeing over a minute average time on page a few weeks after launch. That is the whole argument. It also feeds the service pages, because the questions people read are the pages you build next.

## October 2026 Q&A Batch

### Q1-Oct: I'm a month in. Five sites live, zero leads, one click. Is that normal?

**A:** Yes. Five sites live in a month is the hard part done. Check which pages are indexed using `site:` in Google Search Console—leads always lag behind indexing. Deacon Nick got his first leads at three weeks; Ruth Gray got hers at six weeks. It's normal, and you're on track.

**Key insight:** Track indexing velocity first, leads second. Both are lagging indicators.

### Q2-Oct: Can I use Search Console without tying all my sites together?

**A:** Fine for your first 2 sites; after that, keep them separate. Google knows which account created them, and grouping 100+ sites in one Search Console account can trigger a ban. Use ProRankTracker, DataForSEO, or Ahrefs' free traffic tracker for larger portfolios.

### Q3-Oct: Should my first site cover one service or several?

**A:** Optimize for one primary service + 2–4 related ones to add context. The tighter the site at launch, the better its chances of early ranking. Add more services after the site ranks.

### Q4-Oct: I already run the business. How do I use microsites to feed my own crew?

**A:** Keep your brand off the microsites (except the logo) and don't link back to your main site. Use smaller towns, alternate word orders, or .net if EMD is taken. If the head term is brutal (e.g., "plumber"), go niche: "main water line replacement" is a different fight with less competition.

**Key insight:** You own the business relationship—that's your biggest advantage over builders with no existing client base.

### Q5-Oct: My English isn't great and I don't want to cold call. How do I sell the leads?

**A:** Email + free leads. Send 2 free leads by email, ask if they want more, give a price. The relationship with the contractor becomes the real asset. Shawn has never cold-called a business. Relationship > English proficiency.

### Q6-Oct: Kinsta or Cloudflare for the sites Claude builds?

**A:** Cloudflare. Kinsta is expensive for what you need. Cloudflare + Claude Code is the standard now.

### Q7-Oct: Can I share one gallery of my real work across all my sites?

**A:** No. Don't create a footprint. Ask: if Google deindexed every site sharing that gallery, would it hurt? If yes, keep one original gallery per site.

### Q8-Oct: One business, twenty sites. Same Google Business Profile and phone number on all of them?

**A:** Same phone number is typically a no-go. Same GBP is OK if all sites are the same niche and brand (franchise model), but if they're different businesses, don't link sites to the profile or vice versa.

### Q9-Oct: Claude says neighborhood pages are doorway pages. Is it right?

**A:** Claude is giving general advice; the proven builder template is the specific version. Start from the template and let Claude build on top. A real doorway page is the same page with a few swapped words. Neighborhood pages are fine if each has its own content, images, and headings, and actually helps someone who finds it.

### Q10-Oct: The service happens in one country and the buyers search from another. Does a microsite still work?

**A:** It can. Microsites work for anything location-based. Position it as the business that connects buyer with provider. First check: is anyone searching for it? Zero volume = zero calls.

### Q11-Oct: Is Google's new anti-spam update going to hit microsites?

**A:** Not from what's being observed. The update targets mass-builds that swap town names across thousands of near-identical pages. A site that looks and works like a real local business is a different pattern. Early data shows microsite rankings unchanged.

### Q12-Oct: WhatConverts put my account in manual review. Did I burn it?

**A:** No. Add the tracking script (you should be doing this anyway) and email support. Manual review is routine. One member was approved within 2 minutes.

### Q13-Oct: Where's the Content Generator GPT from the Builder video?

**A:** It's inside the Builder flow (Step 4). Not a separate download—it's built into the workflow.

### Q14-Oct: I have zero experience. Will people laugh at my Upwork profile?

**A:** No. Everyone here started at zero. Verify your account, add portfolio pieces (your own microsites count), add 1–2 credentials. Write for the business owner, not for SEO: "I will help you make more money" beats any technical bullet point.

### Why the Leader Is Winning

The #1 contestant is using a strategy that looks like a **real, well-established business**, not a microsite:

1. **Two identical sites:** Same template, same schema, same setup. One for mold; one for radon.
2. **Unified business identity:** One business name, one address, one phone number—copied to ~15 profiles across trusted platforms:
   - Facebook, YouTube, Houzz, Clutch, Manta, Crunchbase, Pinterest, and more
   - All listed in the site code
   - Google sees the same business described identically on 15 domains it already trusts

3. **Supporting listings rank around the main site:**
   - **Mold:** Facebook #6, site #10, MapQuest #11, Clutch #12 (9 total top results)
   - **Radon:** Houzz #8, Crunchbase #17, site #22 (3 top results)
   - No other contestant has more than 2 top results on either query

4. **Stable homepage ranking:** Homepage held positions #10–#13 every day this week
   - Deep, comprehensive homepage covering all services
   - Other contestants' homepages swap with FAQ/contact pages; rankings swing daily

5. **What they did NOT do:**
   - No press releases
   - No daily republishing
   - No 100-page site

6. **What they DID do:**
   - ~50 pages, built once
   - Surrounded by 15 matching profiles
   - Left alone

### The Takeaway

**A fresh domain cannot hold a ranking by itself.** The winning strategy works because:
- The site looks like a real local business (unified NAP everywhere)
- Supporting listings provide authority signals
- Consistency (homepage locked in) signals stability to Google
- Profile diversity (15 platforms) builds legitimacy

**Lesson for builders:** Don't count on the microsite alone. Build the site + the profiles + match them exactly + stop touching it. This is different from the high-volume, constantly-optimized approach—it's the "build once, leave alone" strategy that works.

## Summary: The Recurring Theme

Across 30+ questions and ongoing contests, one pattern emerges: **Build first, optimize second.** The tools are rough estimates. Your site will teach you more than any tool. Small moves compound. Start cheap, measure real results, then scale what works.

**Build-and-leave-alone beats constant optimization.** The winning microsite in the contest stayed at #10 by being stable and surrounded by matching profiles, while sites being constantly tweaked swapped positions daily.
