# Pricing and Deal Structures

**Core Principle:** Most operators leave money on the table. Underpricing is the quietest way to stay broke. So is a messy deal structure that leaves you chasing payments or arguing over lead quality.

The operators who do well aren't always the best at SEO. They're the ones who price with confidence and structure deals so they get paid cleanly and the client has every reason to stay.

---

## Play #1: The Fastest Raise Is Your Existing Clients

**Principle:** Raising existing rates is faster and cheaper than finding new ones.

**The Play:**
- Run a price adjustment across your entire client book
- Raise every single client simultaneously
- Some will balk, a few will leave (and often come back later)

**Real Example (Blair):**
- Raised all clients at once
- Result: Average landed near $1,600/month
- Range: $500 clients on one end, $5,000 on the other
- Most clients didn't leave

**Why This Works:**
- Relationship inertia is real
- Switching costs are high (finding new vendor, ramp-up time)
- You're improving, which justifies the raise
- Growth compounds from existing revenue faster than new client hunting

## Play #2: Per-Lead, Percentage, or Flat Retainer — When to Use Each

**Principle:** Start per-lead to prove value, move to flat retainer once volume is steady and trust is built.

**The Three Models:**

### Per-Lead Model
- **How:** Client pays for each lead you deliver (e.g., $150 for garage-door lead)
- **Pros:** Simple, easy to pitch, you prove value immediately
- **Cons:** Risk of non-payment; members have been burned for $1,000+ in unpaid leads
- **Best for:** New clients, high-ticket work, when you want proof of ROI

### Percentage Model
- **How:** You take a percentage of the job booked/revenue
- **Pros:** Incentive-aligned, scales with client success
- **Cons:** Requires CRM access to verify what closed (most clients resist)
- **Best for:** Established relationships where trust exists

### Flat Retainer Model
- **How:** Fixed monthly fee once lead volume is steady
- **Pros:** Predictable revenue, no counting individual leads, cleanest long-term
- **Cons:** Requires volume history to estimate
- **Best for:** Proven volume, long-term partnerships

**Pitch Angle:** You deliver *exclusive* leads, unlike Angie's List, which sells the same lead to five businesses at once.

**Transition Path:** Per-lead → build trust & volume data → Flat retainer

## Play #3: The 12-Microsite Deal Structure

**Principle:** Package the offer with a simple monthly number, a small bonus, and a buyout path.

**The Package:**
- **12 sites for $1,500/month on a 12-month engagement**
- Structure: 10 paid + 2 bonus sites (feels generous but operationally simple)
- **Effective:** $150/site/month
- **Buyout:** $3,500/site to own outright after year 1

**Why It Works:**
- Client gets clear monthly number (easy to budget)
- Bonus sites feel generous (psychological win)
- Defined path to ownership (addresses "do I own this after?")
- You keep recurring revenue for 12 months minimum
- Client is less likely to leave mid-term because of sunk cost + bonus

**Closes Better Than:** Pricing sites individually

## Play #4: Take the Cheap "Prove-Out" Deal on Purpose

**Principle:** A discounted deal that proves your system on a live client can be worth more than a full-price one-off. Play the long game when the upside is real.

**The Play:**
1. Client wants flexibility or lower price
2. You accept it deliberately, as a test case
3. Get a year of runway to show results on a real client
4. Client who negotiated you down usually upgrades after you deliver

**Why This Works:**
- Clients who start cheap often become your best long-term revenue
- You get a real test case (which you can reference with future clients)
- Low risk to test a new niche or model
- Trust compounds — they see results over time

**Caution:** Don't discount to everyone. Only take this deal when the upside (relationship, reference, future revenue) is real.

## Play #5: Stack Offers So the Client Can't Afford to Leave

**Principle:** One service is easy to cancel. Three intertwined services are not. Stack them and retention takes care of itself.

**The Stack:**
1. Microsites (entry point)
2. Main-site SEO (expansion)
3. Web hosting / CRM / automation (integration)

**Effect:** With two or three lines of business tied together, leaving becomes painful. Each added service deepens the relationship and raises the cost of switching.

**Example:** Site + CRM + automation + email management all connected means if they leave, they lose all of it, not just one service.

## Play #6: The Dedicated-Hosting and SaaS Markup

**Principle:** The build fee is one-time. The hosting and automation layers are recurring. Bundle real ongoing value.

**The Structure:**
- **Build fee:** One-time (e.g., $1,500)
- **Hosting layer:** Monthly recurring (e.g., $20 real cost, billed as "dedicated hosting")
- **Automation layer:** Monthly recurring (e.g., white-labeled GHL for SMS, email, scheduling)

**Real Example (Bryan):**
- Build: $1,500 upfront
- Hosting + automation: ~$197/month
- Total Year 1: $1,500 + ($197 × 12) = $3,864

**Why This Works:**
- Clients expect hosting to cost something (it's normalized)
- Automation layer is real value they use (CRM, SMS campaigns, scheduling)
- Recurring revenue compounds
- Hard to leave because the CRM lives there

## Play #7: Charge Upfront. Always.

**Principle:** Getting paid is a system, not a hope. Upfront, on subscription, in dollars, and one concession maximum.

**The Mechanics:**
- **Stripe subscription** for monthly fees (simple, removes the chase)
- **Price in US dollars** for cross-border work (currency swing is not your problem; Wise handles it)
- **Anchor and hold** — you ask $X, they counter with $Y, you negotiate to your walk-away price, then hold that

**Red Flag:** Never bill after work is done. "I don't work until I get paid."

**Negotiation Rule:** One concession maximum. Ask $2,500 → negotiate down to $1,500 → that's where it stays for the term.

## Play #8: The Prepaid Prove-Out (Learn on Their Dime)

**Principle:** When payoff is slow, get paid to build, keep what you build, and add upside later.

**The Play:**
1. Sector has long close cycle (e.g., commercial real estate: 6 months–2 years)
2. Don't negotiate rev-share (you'd wait forever)
3. Have partner prepay ~$1,000 for 3 microsites
4. Partner gets leads for year 1
5. You keep site ownership
6. Result: You build, learn, and get cash flow while earning upside later

**Hybrid Version (Shawn):**
- Get first sites out for cash flow
- Then lease the sites
- Layer rev-share later to offset long close cycle

**Real Example (Commercial RE):**
- Partner asks for leads but deals take 1–2 years
- Instead of rev-share (which starves you), prepay for 3 sites
- You learn the niche, get paid, and keep assets
- Layer percentage deal later once you have data

**Caution:** Revenue sharing tied to closed loans is largely prohibited in the US (mortgage, real estate, insurance). Treat regulated niches as flat-fee territory unless you've checked the rules.

## Play #9: The Three-Hour Client ($500/Hour by Design)

**Principle:** Your hourly rate is a design decision. Cap the hours first, then automate until the cap holds.

**The Target:**
- Maximum 3 hours/month per client
- At $1,500/month retainer = $500/hour

**The System (Jesse's Build):**
1. Give Claude automated access to Google Search Console, FTP, and private GitHub
2. Claude accumulates knowledge in GitHub
3. Claude writes new content, revises pages, runs automated schedules
4. Claude emails the client every update it makes (value visibility)
5. Result: Perceived value climbs while you sleep; you stay under 3 hours/month

**The Math:**
- 3 hours/month × $500/hour = $1,500 (or whatever your retainer is)
- 10 clients × $1,500 = $15,000/month with 30 billable hours
- "You'll be making $10K a month before you know it." (Jesse)

**Why This Works:**
- Automation removes scope creep
- Client sees every update (high perceived value)
- You can take more clients at this rate
- Frees you for sales, strategy, new client work

## Play #10: Sell Low or Sell High. Never the Middle.

**Principle:** Two offers work: premium ($20K+) and commodity volume (~$100/month). The middle limps because mid-priced clients expect premium service without premium money.

**Commodity Volume Proof (Teja):**
- "Netflix for websites" — ~40 clients
- $100/month per site
- Fully automated, no upfront cost
- Retention is so high clients forget they're paying (it just posts every month)
- Positioning: You're not selling a website, you're selling infrastructure (site, CRM, profile, posts, leads)

**Premium Proof:**
- $20,000+ entry fee
- Full-service positioning
- Your time is limited; clients expect premium access

**The Middle (The Trap):**
- $500–2,000/month retainers with high-touch expectations
- Clients expect premium service
- You can't deliver premium service profitably
- Deals get renegotiated

**Where to Position:**
- Small/startup → Commodity ($100–300/month, fully automated)
- Mid-market/established → Premium ($2,000–5,000+/month, your involvement)
- Never camp in the $500–1,500 middle unless it's a stepping stone

## Play #11: A Contract Where Every Clause Has a Scar Behind It

**Principle:** A contract is a record of everything that ever went wrong, priced in advance. Make the scary clauses initialed, not buried.

**Blair's Contract (15 years, 2.5 pages):**
Every clause came from a real incident.

**High-ROI Clauses:**
- **No refund policy** — initialed
- **One additional payment due no matter when client cancels** — initialed
- **Arbitration clause** — non-negotiable
- **Not responsible for ADA compliance or privacy policy accuracy** — initialed (client consults their own attorney)

**Why This Works:**
- Blair won his first chargeback in 15 years because terms were explicit and initialed
- "You never win against American Express" — unless the contract is airtight

**How to Build Yours:**
- Don't invent clauses speculatively
- Add them as real situations teach you
- AI can draft the language; don't use generic templates
- Initial the scary ones; bury nothing

## Quick Reference: Which Deal for Which Situation?

| Situation | Deal Type |
|-----------|-----------|
| New client, proving value | Per-lead |
| Multiple microsites, 12-month commitment | 12-site package |
| Client negotiating down but good long-term fit | Cheap prove-out |
| Established client, predictable volume | Flat retainer |
| Long close cycle (real estate, mortgage) | Prepay + percentage layer |
| Want passive income, minimal touch | Commodity volume ($100–300/mo) |
| Premium positioning, your direct involvement | $2,000–5,000+ retainer |

## Implementation Checklist

- ☐ **Audit current clients** — What are you charging vs. what should you charge?
- ☐ **Draft a rate increase email** — to your lowest-priced client
- ☐ **Pick a payment system** — Stripe, Wave, or similar (never invoice post-hoc)
- ☐ **Build a contract template** — with clauses based on your real incidents
- ☐ **Stack an offer** — What second service can you add to your best client?
- ☐ **Create a "three-hour client" system** — Automation + visibility

## The Bottom Line

**Price with confidence.** The operators making the most money aren't the best at SEO—they're the ones who structured deals so they get paid cleanly, moved to recurring revenue, and raised their existing clients without losing them.

Underpricing doesn't make you accessible. It makes you broke.
