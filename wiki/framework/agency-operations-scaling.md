# Agency Operations: Scaling Past Solo

**Core Principle:** The solo ceiling is real. You cap out around 15–20 clients. Every operator who pushed past that did the same uncomfortable thing: they stopped being the person who does the work and became the person who runs the system that does the work.

---

## The Solo Ceiling

**Why 15–20 clients is the hard cap:**
- You're the bottleneck on every decision
- Client relationships depend on you personally
- New work requires you to learn it yourself
- No leverage from systems or delegation

**The path past 15–20 requires a mindset shift:**
From → "I am the person who does the work"  
To → "I run the system that does the work"

---

## Shift #1: Loom Everything Now, Hire Before You're Ready

**Principle:** You can't delegate what only exists in your head. Record it once, and every future hire starts trained.

**The Play:**
1. **Record a Loom video of every task you do** — starting today
2. **Build a training library** as you go
3. **Hire before you're ready** — Filipino VAs (junior-to-senior skill at ~1/10th Western cost)
4. **Train with the library** — don't train the person, the videos train them

**Key Insight:** VAs come and go (churn is normal). The training library is the asset, not the person.

**One Critical Caveat:** AI taken as gospel will embarrass you in front of a client. Keep a human review in the loop. Don't automate the judgment.

**Goal State (Blair's words):** "Become the person who talks to the client and manages the work, not the person who does the work."

---

## Shift #2: Put Boundaries on the Service, Not Just the Price

**Principle:** Scale dies from unlimited scope faster than from low prices. Define what the money buys, out loud.

**The Play:**
- Set a clear policy on what's included (and what's not)
- Communicate boundaries upfront
- Let the boundary make the cheap price profitable

**Example (Teja's $100/month websites):**
- "We do a quarterly review of the website."
- "We're not changing it until then."
- "That's why it's a hundred dollars a month."
- VA handles change requests only; everything else is automated

**Why This Works:**
- Clients accept the boundary because it's stated upfront
- Scope creep kills profitability, not low prices
- Boundaries make automation sustainable

---

## Shift #3: Train the System, Then Let Anyone (or Anything) Run It

**Principle:** Systems scale, heroics don't. If the work only happens when you remember how, you're the bottleneck by design.

**The Play:**
1. **Log every project step** to a central source of truth:
   - GitHub (Kate's approach)
   - Google Sheets (master checklist)
   - ClickUp (Shlomi's approach)
   - Notion or similar
2. **Update the system after every cycle**
3. **Make it AI-compatible** — Claude agent can pick it up mid-stream
4. **Remove handover meetings** — anyone/anything can read the source and continue

**What This Enables:**
- A VA can pick up a project mid-stream
- A new hire starts trained, not confused
- Claude agents can execute repetitive work without human context-switching
- Your absence doesn't slow the client work

**Comparison:** Same move as the Claude writing tool for VAs in the AI Stacks lesson. Encode the standard once. People and agents plug into it.

---

## Implementation Checklist

- ☐ **Start recording** — Pick one task you do weekly and Loom it this week
- ☐ **Build the library** — Record 5 core processes before hiring
- ☐ **Define boundaries** — Write a 1-page service agreement with what's included/excluded
- ☐ **Create a source of truth** — GitHub, Sheets, ClickUp, or Notion
- ☐ **Document one project end-to-end** — Log every step for this month
- ☐ **Audit your current clients** — Which ones would survive your absence for a week?

---

## The Transition Timeline

**Months 1–3: Record & Document**
- Loom 5–10 core processes
- Document your current client workflow
- Set boundaries with 1–2 pilot clients

**Months 3–6: Hire & Train**
- Hire first VA (part-time if cash is tight)
- Use Looms to onboard
- Adjust documentation based on VA feedback

**Months 6–12: Systematize & Delegate**
- Hand off routine tasks
- Move non-judgement work to automation
- Rebuild your hours as client management

**Month 12+: Scale**
- You're now the operator managing systems and people
- New clients can be onboarded without your direct involvement
- Growth is no longer capped by your time

---

## Key Metrics

Track the shift from "person who does" to "person who runs the system":

- % of billable time spent managing vs. doing
- Number of clients onboarded without your hands-on work
- VA/team utilization rate (how much of their time is billable)
- Client satisfaction (do they feel the difference when you step back?)

---

## The Hard Part

This is uncomfortable because:
- You lose direct control
- Mistakes will happen in systems you didn't execute
- You have to trust the process and the people
- Your identity shifts from "expert doer" to "system builder"

**But this discomfort is the gate.** Everyone past 20 clients walked through it. The ones who didn't stayed stuck at 15–20.
