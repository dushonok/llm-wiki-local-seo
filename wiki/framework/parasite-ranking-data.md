# Parasite Platform Ranking Data (Real Contest Results)

**Purpose:** Real-world proof of which parasite/citation platforms actually rank in search results vs. which don't. Based on Oct 12, 2026 microsite contest data where contestants built the same service across dozens of platforms and tracked which ones appeared in top-50 rankings.

**Key insight:** Most parasite platforms contestants built never appeared in rankings. Only 27 platforms out of 50+ built made it to top-50 positions. There's a clear Tier 1 (ranks) vs. Tier 2 (doesn't rank) division.

## Common Ranking Factors (What Tier 1 Winners Did)

**Rules observed across all ranking profiles:**

1. **Business name identical everywhere** — Same name on site and every profile (no variations)
2. **Same street address and phone number** — NAP consistency across all platforms
3. **Same one-paragraph description** — Usually the site's meta description, copied verbatim
4. **Listed in sameAs schema** — Every profile URL included in `<script type="application/ld+json">` sameAs array on the site
5. **Built early, then left alone** — Created in the first week, no updates/modifications after that

**Note:** These are the *common* factors across winners, not necessarily the *causative* factors. See [[citations-strategy.md]] for deeper causal analysis.

## Practical Implications

### For Citation Building:
- **Prioritize Tier 1 platforms.** They're the only ones that move the ranking needle.
- Don't waste time on Tier 2 platforms expecting ranking rewards. Use them only if they serve a secondary purpose (brand presence, traffic, authority building at scale — not local ranking).

### For NAP Consistency:
- This contest reinforces the NAP rule: identical business name, address, phone everywhere.
- The `sameAs` schema linking matters — contestants who linked all profiles in schema saw them rank together.

### For Microsite Strategy:
- Focus on the 27 Tier 1 platforms, not the 100+ in the larger parasite list.
- The "built early, left alone" pattern suggests that overoptimization or constant updates actually hurt rankings. Set it and forget it.

## Questions This Raises

1. **Why didn't Yelp rank?** Yelp was in Tier 2 (didn't appear in top-50 for this contest). This is surprising given Yelp's authority. Possible reasons: (a) new profiles take longer to rank on Yelp, (b) Yelp's algorithm doesn't surface small/new businesses easily, (c) the keywords tested weren't "Yelp review" queries where Yelp itself dominates.

2. **Why did press releases rank?** Paid press distribution (e.g., PR Newswire) appeared in top 50. This suggests newswire syndication is being picked up by secondary news outlets and indexed.

3. **Why did Pinterest rank?** Pinterest is not typically a citation source, but it ranked. Possibly because contestants linked it in `sameAs` and created actual pins linking back to the site.

4. **LinkedIn company pages didn't rank?** Despite high DA and trust, LinkedIn company pages are gated/profile-focused, not indexable for public search queries.

---

## Recommendations

- **Use Tier 1 exclusively** for local microsite ranking strategy.
- **Tier 2 can be used** for brand presence/authority building, but don't expect ranking returns.
- **NAP + sameAs linking** is critical — contestants who maintained consistency and linked everything ranked higher.
- **Set and forget** — constant updates/optimization didn't help; stability did.
