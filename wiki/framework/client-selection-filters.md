# Client Selection Filters

**Core Principle:** Not all clients are created equal. The wrong ones churn fast, drain your time, second-guess your work, or make you nervous every time your phone rings.

Most operators learn this the hard way — they say yes to everyone, then spend their energy on the clients least likely to stay. These filters help you pick clients that stick and spot the ones that won't before you sign them.

---

## Filter #1: Avoid the Client Who Knows Just Enough SEO

**Red Flag:** Clients from YouTube, podcasts, or AI content who know *a little* about SEO.

**Why They're Painful:**
- Send emails at 2am with walls of ChatGPT output telling you how to do your job
- Never fully trust your expertise
- Constant second-guessing and "why didn't you do X?"
- You're fighting them instead of helping them

**The Client You Actually Want:**
- Business owner who says: *"You're the expert — you handle it."*
- Values the outcome, not the tactics
- Lets you do the work you're good at
- Trusts the process

**The Principle:** The best client respects your expertise. Someone who wants to co-pilot every decision will cost you more than they pay.

**How to Spot Them Early:**
- Ask in the sales call: "How involved do you want to be in the SEO work?"
- If they start talking about specific tactics, that's a warning sign
- If they want weekly calls to discuss strategy, they're likely high-maintenance

## Filter #2: Pre-Qualify Lead Buyers by Review Count

**Red Flag:** Businesses with either extreme:
- Thousands of reviews (they're established, don't need you)
- Very few reviews (they don't care about reputation or growth)

**The Goldilocks Zone:** Around 50 reviews

**Why This Works (Naomi's Data):**
- Company with thousands of reviews will ignore you
- Company with ~50 reviews is hungry
  - They pick up the phone
  - They listen
  - They say yes
  - Near 100% hit rate on these calls

**The Play:** Target the business sitting *just below* the leader in the map pack.
- Enough proof they want to grow
- Not so established they don't need you
- Sweet spot for response rate and deal quality

**The Principle:** The hungriest partners make the stickiest clients. Pick the ones who actually need what you bring.

---

## Filter #3: Only Rev-Share with Partners Who Can Swallow the Volume

**Red Flag:** Contractors who can't handle 100+ leads per month.

**Why Volume Matters (Jesse's Team Data):**
- Small contractors choke on the volume
- They ghost when things get busy
- Deal dies because they can't execute
- You generate leads they waste

**The Two Filters:**

### Filter 3a: Volume Capacity
- Ask: "How many leads can you handle per month?"
- If they hesitate or say <50, move on
- Minimum: 100+ leads/month absorption

### Filter 3b: Service Radius
- Small contractors: drive 30–40 minutes max
- Bigger operators: drive up to 90 minutes for large jobs
- **Match your site's geographic coverage to what they'll actually service**
- Otherwise, you're generating leads they won't take

**The Principle:** Rev-share only works when the partner's capacity matches your output. Qualify for appetite before you qualify for anything else.

**What to Ask:**
- "What's your geographic service area?"
- "How many jobs per month can you handle?"
- "What's your average job size and timeline?"

## Filter #4: Pick Niches Where Your Work Is Actually the Lever

**Red Flag:** You do great work but the niche doesn't respond.

**Real Example (Shlomi):**
- Spent months on restaurant local SEO
- More keywords, better content, solid strategy
- Rankings moved minimally
- Reason: In restaurants, the review score is the ranking parameter, not content
- His best work couldn't win because reviews, not SEO, move the needle

**The Question to Ask Before Signing:**
*"What actually ranks in this niche? Is it what I sell?"*

**Common Niche Failures:**
- **Restaurants:** Reviews are the lever, not SEO
- **Real estate:** Agent brand/reputation dominates
- **Mortgage/lending:** Compliance and brand trust dominate
- **Medical:** Credentials and E-E-A-T dominate over rankings

**Where SEO Actually Wins:**
- **Local services:** Plumbing, HVAC, locksmith (reviews + keywords both matter)
- **Niche contractors:** Specific types of repair, installation
- **Specialty retail:** When people search specifically for the service
- **Professional services:** Where authority + rankings compound

**The Principle:** Before you take the client, ask what actually ranks in their niche. If the answer isn't the thing you sell, the client can't stick no matter how good you are.

**Shlomi's Wisdom:** "It was a nice ride, a nice attempt. It was a lesson. You have to walk the path to learn things." (Sometimes you find out by doing it, not by asking.)

## Quick Reference: Client Evaluation Scorecard

Use this before signing a client:

| Factor | Green Flag | Red Flag |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **SEO Knowledge** | "You're the expert, handle it" | Talks about tactics, wants to co-pilot |
| **Growth Hunger** | ~50 reviews, wants to grow | 1,000+ reviews or <10 reviews |
| **Lead Absorption** (rev-share) | 100+ leads/month capacity | <50 leads/month, vague on volume |
| **Service Radius** (rev-share) | Covers your geographic area | Won't travel for jobs |
| **Niche Viability** | SEO is the ranking lever | Reviews/reputation/credentials dominate |
| **Trust Level** | References previous vendors | Difficult on sales calls |
| **Payment** | Upfront, no negotiation | Wants terms, payment later |

## Implementation: The Pre-Sales Filter

Before you pitch or demo, ask these questions:

1. **"How involved do you want to be in the SEO work?"**
   - *Good answer:* "Just tell me when results come in"
   - *Bad answer:* Detailed questions about tactics

2. **"What results have you seen from [previous vendor]?"**
   - *Good answer:* "Nothing, that's why we're looking"
   - *Bad answer:* "We did it ourselves" (they'll do it again)

3. **"How many [leads/jobs/customers] could you handle per month?"**
   - *Good answer:* Specific number, confident
   - *Bad answer:* Vague, hesitant, low number

4. **"What's your service area?"**
   - *Good answer:* Matches your site coverage
   - *Bad answer:* Tiny radius or nationwide (hard to manage)

5. **"Why did you reach out to me specifically?"**
   - *Good answer:* Saw your results, referral, specific problem
   - *Bad answer:* Generic outreach, price shopping, "trying everyone"

## The Bottom Line

**Your best clients are the ones you screen ruthlessly before they become clients.**

You cannot convert a bad-fit client into a good one. But you can spend 20 minutes on the phone and spot them before they become your problem. Do the work early; it saves months of frustration.

The operators with the best margins and lowest churn aren't lucky. They're selective.
