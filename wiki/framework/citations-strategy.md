# Citations Strategy

**Core Principle:** Citations are a factor, not the factor. You can rank a website without them if you cannot get an address and phone number — or if you simply don't want to go down that rabbit hole.

But when citations are available, they matter for local ranking signals and trust building.

---

## The Citations Spectrum

### Ideal: Client's Real Business + Full NAP

**Setup:**
- Real business address
- Verified phone number
- Google Business Profile (ideally verified)
- Build citations across major directories (Yelp, BBB, Google, etc.)

**Why It Works:**
- Full entity validation across the web
- AI systems can link the business across citations
- Strongest local ranking signal

**Who Does This:** Traditional local SEO for established clients with real locations.

---

### Middle Ground: Created Address + Citations

**Setup:**
- Use a business park or co-working space address (real, verifiable location)
- Register the address as the business location
- Build citations to that address
- Launch GBP after site is established

**Pros:**
- Less legal risk than fabricated addresses
- Slightly better ranking potential than no citations
- Buildable at scale

**Cons:**
- Still carries some risk if the address is too generic or shared
- Some citation platforms are strict about business address verification

**Who Does This:** R&R microsite operators who want citation benefits without fully fabricating identity.

**Notes from Call Discussion:**
- One member: "We have experimented with business park addresses and building citations to those."
- Shawn: "We prefer less legal risk, even if we get a slightly worse hit ratio."

---

### Lean: No Citations, No GBP

**Setup:**
- No physical address (or don't publish one)
- No GBP
- No structured citations
- Rank on content + backlinks alone

**Pros:**
- Zero legal/verification risk
- Easier to scale (no address hunting)
- Works for certain niches

**Cons:**
- Lower ranking ceiling in some local niches
- Missing ranking signal

**Who Does This:** Operators ranking in niches where citations don't move the needle; service-area only businesses.

**Notes from Calls:**
- "You can rank a website without [citations] if you cannot get an address and phone # (or just don't want to go down that rabbit hole)." (Jesse)
- "I know Ippei SEO has his R&R and they don't even seem to have very many citations whatsoever." (Zach)

## Strategy by Situation

### Scenario 1: Building R&R Microsites (No Specific Client)

**Problem:** You're building sites in a network; listing multiple fake addresses looks like a footprint.

**Solution Options:**

**Option A: Skip Citations Entirely**
- Rank on content quality and backlinks
- Build one site per niche to test market
- If it works, then explore citations
- Fastest to market

**Option B: Use Business Park Addresses**
- Find an actual business park or co-working space address
- Use that address for 1–2 sites in the area
- Build citations to that address
- GBP is optional until site is established

**Option C: City-Only NAP (No Street Address)**
- List: City, State, Country, Phone only
- Many citation platforms allow this for "service area" businesses
- Reduces footprint risk
- Still builds some citation authority

**Recommended:** Option A (skip citations initially) to avoid legal risk. Add them once the site has proven ranking potential.

---

### Scenario 2: Building for an Actual Client

**Process:**
1. Get their real address and phone
2. Create/claim GBP
3. Build citations systematically across major platforms (Yelp, BBB, Google, industry-specific)
4. Maintain NAP consistency across all citations

**Citation Priority Tiers:**

**Tier 1 (Must Have):**
- Google Business Profile (verified)
- Google
- Yelp
- Better Business Bureau (BBB)

**Tier 2 (High Value):**
- Industry-specific directories (roofing guilds, plumbing associations, etc.)
- Local chambers of commerce
- Local directories relevant to the niche

**Tier 3 (Volume):**
- General business directories (Yellowpages, Apple Maps, etc.)
- Review aggregators

## The Legal Consideration

**Fabricated Addresses:**
- Carries legal risk (false business claims)
- Can trigger investigation if noticed
- Some members avoid it; others use business park addresses

**Safer Alternatives:**
- Use real addresses (co-working, business park)
- Use city-only NAP (no street address)
- Skip citations entirely if ranking without them

**Consensus from Calls:** When in doubt, prefer less legal risk, even if hit ratio is slightly worse.

## Case Study: Hunter Lord's #1 Ranking Citation Build

**Context:** Hunter built a rank-and-rent site for the Gold Coast that now ranks #1 in its niche. His citation strategy is a real-world example of the commitment required.

**His Process:**
- **Time commitment:** 2 hours per day for approximately 3 weeks
- **Citation count:** 115 manually built citations
- **Pace/throttling:** Only pinged 5 citations per day to avoid triggering Google's spam filters
- **Total investment:** ~42 hours of manual work

**Key Takeaway:** Manual citations work, but they require discipline. Don't blast all citations at once; pace them to look natural.

**Validation Step:** After building citations, use a free bulk index checker (e.g., https://www.duplichecker.com/google-index-checker.php) to verify which ones are actually indexed by Google. **Not all citations get indexed**, so focus on the ones that do.

**The Real Win:** Google indexes and keeps only the most trustworthy citations. Hunter's strategy of careful pacing and then validating which ones stuck is the refined approach.

## Web 2.0 & Foundational Links (Tier 2 Strategy)

**What are Web 2.0 links?**

Web 2.0 links are links from user-generated content platforms (free blog networks, profile sites, social platforms) that point *to your citations* (not directly to your main site). They "power up" your citation authority.

**Strategy:**
- Build citations on Tier 1–2 platforms (Yelp, BBB, YellowPages, etc.)
- Buy Web 2.0/foundational links pointing to those citations
- This indirect link-building approach protects your main domain from overoptimization while boosting your citation authority

**Where to Find Web 2.0 Vendors:**

**Fiverr (Recommended):**
- Search category: **Off-page SEO → Top Rated Sellers**
- Search term: **"foundational links"** (more results than "web 2.0 backlinks")
- Look for: Vendors with 4.8+ ratings and track records on home-services projects
- Cost: $20–$100 per order depending on link quantity/quality

**Vetting Vendors:**
- Check reviews for real clients mentioning their niche (home services, local services)
- Ask for sample links before ordering
- Verify links post-delivery using Ahrefs or SEMrush (some vendors deliver PBN/spam)
- Warning: One member tried what looked like high-quality links from icopify.com; Semrush later identified them as a PBN link farm

**Caution:** Don't over-rely on Web 2.0 links. They're supplementary to real citations and social proof. Quality sources on Fiverr vary widely — always verify.

## Brave Browser Citations (AI Visibility Boost)

**Why Brave Matters:**

Claude (Anthropic's AI) uses Brave Search. If you want your citations indexed by Brave and discoverable to Claude-based queries, submit them to:

**Brave Search Submission:** [https://search.brave.com/submit-url](https://search.brave.com/submit-url)

**Process:**
- One URL submission at a time
- Submit each of your top citations individually
- Takes 1–2 minutes per citation but ensures Brave crawler finds them
- No guaranteed ranking, but improves discoverability in Brave results (which Claude references)

**Added Value:**
- As AI citations become more important for visibility, being indexed on Brave (which Claude uses) is a practical hedge
- Zero cost; only time investment

## Implementation Checklist

**For Client Sites (Real Business):**
- ☐ Verify real address and phone
- ☐ Claim/create GBP, get verified
- ☐ Build Tier 1 citations (Google, Yelp, BBB)
- ☐ Build Tier 2 (industry-specific)
- ☐ Audit quarterly for consistency
- ☐ Update citations if NAP changes

**For R&R Microsites:**
- ☐ Decide: Citations or no citations?
- ☐ If citations: secure business park address or city-only NAP
- ☐ Build Tier 1 citations only (cost-benefit)
- ☐ Monitor for ranking; if no movement, de-prioritize

**For Wikipedia Opportunities:**
- ☐ Install Wikipedia Citation Opportunity Engine workflow
- ☐ Run on 5–10 articles in your niche
- ☐ Create content to fill 2–3 identified gaps
- ☐ Reach out to Wikipedia editors with sources

---

## The Bottom Line

**Citations matter, but they're not mandatory.** You can rank without them if your content is good and your backlinks are solid. When you do use citations, keep NAP consistent and prioritize the platforms where your customers actually search. For R&R microsites, weigh the legal risk against the ranking gain—often skipping citations is the right move until you've proven the site works.
