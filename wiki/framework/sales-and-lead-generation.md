# Sales and Lead Generation

**Core Principle:** Cold email, voicemail drops, automated funnels, "AI-powered" everything. Prospects have seen all of it and learned to tune it out. The harder you lean on automated tactics, the more invisible you become.

The fix isn't a better automation. It's standing out in the ways automation can't.

---

## Approach #1: Be the Human, Not the Bot

**The Play:** Open cold calls with unmistakable humanity.

**The Script (Blair's Approach):**
- "I'm a real person in South Carolina, not AI."
- Or: Mention something specific about their business (not generic)
- Acknowledge you're a real person reaching out

**Why It Works:**
- Prospects are braced for bots and automation
- A real human is now the surprise
- It stops them (positive surprise = they listen)
- In a market drowning in automation, the human touch is the edge

**The Principle:** When everyone automates, the human touch becomes the edge. Lead with it.

**Implementation:**
- Use your real name, not a brand name
- Call during business hours (not automated times)
- Mention something specific from their website or local business
- Be conversational, not scripted

## Approach #2: Give Them a Reason to Pick Up

**The Play:** Arrive with something useful about their business, not a generic offer.

**Blair's System:**
1. Write a Claude prompt that runs an instant "AI visibility" check
2. Grade the prospect's site on the 7 things AI looks for (AI Overviews readiness)
3. Open the call with that data: "I'm on your About page right now, and here's what I'm seeing..."

**Why It Works:**
- The call has a specific hook (not "want better SEO?")
- You've done homework (shows you care)
- It's about them, not your offer
- Opens a real conversation instead of a pitch

**The Principle:** The call works when you arrive with something useful about their business, not a generic offer.

**What You Could Check:**
- NAP consistency (check citations)
- LocalBusiness schema presence
- Review count vs. competitors
- GBP optimization score
- On-page structure (title, H1, H2s)
- Citation diversity

**The Email/Call:**
- "I pulled your site and analyzed [X]. Here's what I found: [specific insight]"
- Not: "I help businesses rank better"

## Approach #3: Stack Lead Channels (Facebook Ads + Cold Outreach + Email)

**The Play:** Don't rely on one lead source. When one channel goes quiet, another fills the pipeline.

**The Stack (in order):**
1. **Rank your own city sites** (organic, no ad spend)
2. **Cold and warm email** (your list, referrals)
3. **Facebook and Instagram ads** (targeted, ~$49 per lead)

**Why It Works:**
- Single-channel dependency is dangerous
- When cold email dries up, ads keep the pipeline full
- Owned sites (rank your own) = no ad spend, pure margin
- Ads are cheap at volume if you're selective

**Budget Approach (One Member):**
- Spend ~$49 per lead via Facebook/Instagram
- Keep the pipeline full while cold outreach cooks
- Use owned sites as the base (always on)

**The Principle:** Multiple channels compound. When one slows, the others keep you moving.

**Metrics to Track:**
- Cost per lead (cold outreach)
- Cost per lead (paid ads)
- Conversion rate by channel
- Which channel produces the highest-quality leads?

## The Reality of Cold Outreach

**What's broken:**
- Everyone automates the same way
- Templates are obviously templates
- Prospects are tired of pitches
- Volume of noise is too high

**What still works:**
- Real human contact
- Specific, useful information about their business
- Reaching them at the right time
- Following up without being annoying

## Approach #4: Find Your Ideal Client Before They Post a Job (Google Maps Prospecting)

**The Play:** Instead of waiting for prospects to post jobs on Upwork or request services, find them on Google Maps where they're already ranked and already have customers. Target businesses showing SEO/marketing gaps.

**Why It Works:**
- Upwork has high competition; 100+ bidders per job
- Prospects on Google Maps are actively in business, not just exploring
- Gaps you find (poor website, weak reviews, no local SEO) are pain points they *don't know they have yet* — you get there first

**The Process:**

### 1. Pick Your Service Niche
Choose a niche you understand or want to specialize in (plumbing, electrical, HVAC, pest control, divorce law, tax prep, etc.).

### 2. Search Google Maps for That Niche
Search "[Service] [City]" on Google Maps.

**Critical:** Don't focus on the top 3. Those are the established players with strong SEO and dedicated marketing.

Look deeper in the Maps results. Skim past the first few pages and target **businesses ranked #4–#15**.

### 3. Identify Gaps & Pain Points
Once you've scanned 20–30 businesses in your niche/city, look for patterns:

**Review Gaps:**
- ❌ Competitors have 150+ reviews; this business has 8
- ❌ Competitors' average is 4.8 stars; this business is 3.9
- ❌ Competitors get 2–3 reviews/month; this business gets 1 every 6 months

**Website Gaps:**
- ❌ Outdated website (looks like 2015)
- ❌ No service-specific pages (just a homepage)
- ❌ No "best service" pages (they could be ranking for high-ticket keywords they don't target)
- ❌ Website and GBP don't align (GBP says one thing, site says another)
- ❌ No schema markup (you can spot this by comparing their Google search appearance to competitors')

**Local SEO Gaps:**
- ❌ Not showing up for "[Service] [Nearby Town]" searches (weak location targeting)
- ❌ GBP photos are outdated or low quality
- ❌ GBP service area is not defined
- ❌ No special hours, no holiday notes, no call-to-action

**Marketing Gaps:**
- ❌ No mention of reviews in their copy ("See our 150+ 5-star reviews")
- ❌ No case studies or before/after examples
- ❌ No guarantee or warranty mentioned
- ❌ No proof (certifications, years in business, client logos)

### 4. Find the Owner's Direct Contact
Look for signals that you can reach the owner directly:

- **Personal mobile number on GBP** — Use Google Maps; look at the phone number. Call it. If it's a personal line (not a switchboard), you have the owner.
- **Email on website** — Check About page, footer, contact form
- **LinkedIn** — Search "[Business Name] owner" on LinkedIn
- **Facebook** — Personal Facebook account may show current projects

### 5. Craft Your Outreach
Use the human-touch principles from Approach #1, but add specificity:

**Opening:**
> "Hey [Name], is this [First Name], the owner of [Business]? I was looking at [Business] on Google Maps and came across your profile. I noticed something I thought might be worth a quick chat—mind if I share?"

**Specific Finding (Use What You Found):**
> "Your Google listing is ranking solid for '[Main Keyword],' but I noticed you're not showing up for '[Nearby Town] + [Service]' searches. I found two other [Service] businesses in [City] ranking for that term. If you're doing work in those areas, there's probably some low-hanging fruit there."

OR:

> "I was on your website and saw you don't have a dedicated page for [Specific Service]. I checked Google trends for that keyword—it's getting 500+ searches/month locally. You're probably losing leads there."

OR:

> "Your Google reviews are at 3.9 stars with 12 reviews. Your top competitor has 150+ at 4.8. The difference is they ask every client for a review and you don't see that happening. I could help you systematize that."

**The Ask (Not a Sales Pitch):**
> "I work with [Service] businesses on stuff like this. Would you be open to a 15-minute call to see if there's something worth exploring?"

### 6. Follow-Up System
- **Call/text:** If you get voicemail, text 2 hours later with same message (copy/paste is fine)
- **Email:** If you find email, send within 1 hour of calling
- **LinkedIn:** If you find them, connect with a note referencing your call
- **Persistence:** Reach out 3 times over 2 weeks. If no response, move to next prospect

**Reality:** You'll get ~5–10% response rate on cold outreach. That's normal. It scales.

### 7. Monetize the Conversation
Once you get them on the call:

**Option A: Services (Upfront or Hourly)**
> "I can build you a dedicated service page optimized for that term, set up a review collection system, and get some press coverage. That's a $3K project."

**Option B: Monthly Retainer (Long-term)**
> "I could manage your online presence—review follow-up, content updates, local SEO monitoring—at $500/month for the next 6 months."

**Option C: Revenue Share (Lead Gen)**
> "I'll set up landing pages for '[Service]' in the nearby towns. For every qualified lead, I take a commission ($50–$100 depending on your close rate)."

**Option D: Done-for-You Microsite**
> "I can build you a dedicated microsite for '[Service] in [Nearby Town]' and rank it. $2K upfront, or $300/month for 12 months."

## Implementation Checklist

**For Phone Outreach:**
- ☐ Write 3 opening lines (one human, one with a hook, one with a question)
- ☐ Research 5 prospects deeply (NAP consistency, reviews, schema, GBP)
- ☐ Make 10 calls this week (with real research, not templates)
- ☐ Track which opening line gets callbacks

**For Email Outreach:**
- ☐ Build a Claude prompt for "AI visibility check" on their site
- ☐ Run it on 20 prospects
- ☐ Write 20 emails with specific findings (not templates)
- ☐ Track open and reply rates

**For Paid Ads:**
- ☐ Create 1 video ad (testimonial, before/after, or case study)
- ☐ Launch with $100 budget on Facebook/Instagram
- ☐ Target: Local service businesses in your niche + geography
- ☐ Track cost per lead, quality of leads

## The Bottom Line

**Cold outreach is dead.** Cold outreach that shows you've done homework and respects the prospect's time is very much alive.

Stand out by being the opposite of automation: specific, human, and useful. That's all it takes.
