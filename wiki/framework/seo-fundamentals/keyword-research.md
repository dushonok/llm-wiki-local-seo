# Keyword Research Fundamentals

**Purpose:** Conceptual foundation for understanding keyword research — how to find, evaluate, and prioritize search terms. This is theoretical knowledge supporting tactical guides like [[niche-selection-strategy.md]] and [[getting-cited-by-ai.md]].

**Audience:** Anyone building content or optimizing a site who needs to understand how keyword research drives SEO strategy.

---

## What Is Keyword Research?

Keyword research is the process of identifying search terms (keywords) that people actually type into Google, Bing, and other search engines. It's the foundation of SEO because:

1. **It reveals what your audience searches for** — Not what you think they should search for
2. **It uncovers content gaps** — Topics competitors don't cover
3. **It guides content prioritization** — Which pages to build first
4. **It informs strategy** — Whether a niche is worth pursuing

**Core insight:** Successful SEO starts with understanding search demand, not with guessing what to write.

## Key Concepts

### Search Volume
**Definition:** The number of times a keyword is searched in a given period (usually monthly).

- **High volume** (1K+ searches/month) — More traffic potential, but usually more competition
- **Medium volume** (100–1K searches/month) — Sweet spot for many niches
- **Low volume** (10–100 searches/month) — Less traffic, but often easier to rank
- **Zero volume** — Tools show no data, but real traffic exists (especially in small towns or niche services)

**Caveat:** Search volume tools (Google Ads Planner, Ahrefs, SEMrush) estimate based on partial data. Actual volume varies.

### Search Intent
**Definition:** Why someone is searching — what they're trying to accomplish.

The four main intent types:

1. **Informational** — "How to fix a leaky faucet" — user wants to learn
2. **Commercial** — "Best plumber in San Diego" — user wants to decide between options
3. **Transactional** — "Emergency plumber near me" — user wants to buy/hire NOW
4. **Navigational** — "Google Maps" — user wants to navigate to a specific tool/site

**Local SEO insight:** Local intent keywords ("plumber near me," "[city] + service") are highly transactional and convert best.

### Keyword Difficulty (KD)
**Definition:** How hard it is to rank for a keyword based on the competition.

- **Low KD (0–30)** — Easier to rank; often lower search volume
- **Medium KD (30–60)** — Moderate competition; good balance of traffic + rankability
- **High KD (60–100)** — Highly competitive; established brands dominate

**For microsites:** Target low-to-medium KD keywords in your niche. Avoid high-KD brand keywords (plumbing vs. "plumber San Diego" vs. "emergency plumber zip code 12345").

### Cost Per Click (CPC)
**Definition:** What advertisers pay per click on Google Ads for that keyword.

- **High CPC ($2+)** — Commercial keywords; people spend money (good for monetization or lead gen)
- **Medium CPC ($0.50–2)** — Mixed commercial/informational
- **Low CPC (<$0.50)** — Mostly informational; lower monetization potential

**Insight:** High CPC keywords often correlate with high-ticket services (legal, medical, financial). Low CPC doesn't mean low value — local service keywords can have low CPC but high conversion rates.

## Common Keyword Research Tools

### Free Tools
- **Google Ads Keyword Planner** — Official data from Google (registration required)
- **Google Trends** — See search volume trends over time
- **Bing Webmaster Tools** — Keyword research from Bing's index
- **AlsoAsked** — Shows question variations people ask
- **Answer The Public** — Visualizes questions and prepositions people use
- **Reddit/Quora** — Real user questions (no volume data, but reveals actual pain points)

### Paid Tools
- **Ahrefs** — Comprehensive keyword data, backlink research, rank tracking
- **SEMrush** — Keyword research, competitor analysis, audit tools
- **Semrush Keyword Tool** — Detailed keyword metrics
- **Moz Keyword Explorer** — User-friendly interface, good difficulty estimates
- **Topic Mojo** — Affordable alternative to premium tools

**For local SEO:** Free tools often lack geo-specific search volume (e.g., "plumber near me" in Zip 12345). Paid tools give better local breakdown.

## Keyword Research Process

### Step 1: Seed Keywords
Start with broad terms related to your niche. Examples:
- Niche: Plumbing → "plumber," "plumbing," "emergency plumbing"
- Niche: Weight loss → "weight loss," "diet," "workout"

Use Google autocomplete or free tools to brainstorm variations.

### Step 2: Expand & Segment
Use a keyword tool to expand seed keywords:
- Add modifiers: location ("San Diego plumber"), intent ("best," "near me"), long-tail ("emergency plumber for gas leak")
- Look for question formats: "how to fix," "why is," "what is"
- Identify local intent keywords

Segment keywords by:
- **Search intent** (transactional, commercial, informational)
- **Search volume** (high, medium, low)
- **Difficulty** (easy, medium, hard)

### Step 3: Analyze Competitor Keywords
See which keywords competitors rank for:
- Tools: Ahrefs, SEMrush, Moz
- Look for gaps: keywords they rank for that you don't (opportunity to target)
- Look for overlaps: keywords you both target (head-to-head competition)

### Step 4: Prioritize
Create a prioritization matrix:

| Keyword | Volume | Difficulty | Intent | Relevance | Priority |
|---------|--------|------------|--------|-----------|----------|
| plumber | 5K | High | Mixed | N/A (too broad) | Low |
| emergency plumber near me | 50 | Low | Transactional | Yes | High |
| how to fix leaky faucet | 200 | Low | Informational | Maybe | Medium |

Prioritize based on:
- **Transactional intent + low difficulty** = best ROI
- **High volume + low difficulty** = secondary targets
- **Informational + low difficulty** = support content for topical authority

### Step 5: Organize Into Content Clusters
Group related keywords into topics:

- **Hub:** "Emergency plumbing services" (main page)
  - Spoke 1: "Emergency plumber near me"
  - Spoke 2: "24/7 plumbing"
  - Spoke 3: "Pipe burst repair"

This helps with topical authority and internal linking strategy.

## Keyword Research for Local SEO

### Geo-Modifying Keywords
Add location to every keyword:
- "plumber" → "plumber San Diego" → "emergency plumber San Diego" → "emergency plumber zip 92101"
- "divorce lawyer" → "divorce lawyer Dallas" → "divorce lawyer downtown Dallas"

**Benefit:** Lower difficulty, higher local intent, better conversion.

### Keyword-Location Matrix
Create a tracking matrix for your target locations + keywords:

| Keyword | San Diego | Phoenix | Los Angeles |
|---------|-----------|---------|-------------|
| Emergency Plumber | Rank: #3 | Rank: #8 | Not ranking |
| 24/7 Plumber | Rank: #1 | Not tracking | Rank: #5 |

This shows where to focus optimization effort.

### "Near Me" Keywords
"Near me" is now one of the biggest search modifiers in local SEO. Examples:
- "plumber near me" (highest intent, most competitive)
- "mechanic near me"
- "coffee near me"

These keywords trigger Google Maps and Local Pack results.

## The Reality of Search Volume

### What Tools Don't Tell You

1. **"Zero volume" keywords have real traffic** — Especially in small markets or niche services. A town of 10K people might search "locksmith [town] PA" only 5 times/month, but tools show "0."

2. **Search volume varies by season** — Tax keywords spike in March/April; holiday shopping peaks Oct-Dec

3. **Search volume estimates are educated guesses** — All tools estimate; no one has access to 100% of Google search data

4. **Long-tail keywords compound** — Fifty 50-search keywords add up to more traffic than one 2K keyword, often with less competition

**Implication for microsites:** Don't dismiss "zero volume" keywords in small geographic markets. Do your own market research (check Google Trends, local Facebook groups, etc.).
