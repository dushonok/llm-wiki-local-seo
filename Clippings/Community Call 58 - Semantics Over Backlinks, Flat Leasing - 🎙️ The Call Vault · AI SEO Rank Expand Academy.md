## What we covered

Backlinks are a function of competition, not a default. If there were one roofer in a state, that site would need zero links. The target is a sub-niche with real volume and light competition, where structure and semantic relevance do the work. Two members backed this up live: a Jacksonville carpenter site is showing up across AI Overviews from content alone, and another member sees AI Overview impressions in Search Console from semantic structuring, with link building only starting now. The closing point: dial in technicals and internal linking first. Backlinks are fuel on a fire that is already burning, and if the homepage does not link properly to the hub pages, that equity never reaches the pages you want ranked.

On YMYL niches like lawyers and financial services, the group mostly stays away, or works under an existing license (a "powered by" sponsoring bank, for example). For attorney niches the advice was firmer: work under an actual attorney. One route is to build a relationship with the largest firm in the niche, run a test site powered by them at a discounted per-lead rate, and cover the smaller towns they do not reach yet.

Jay asked about a site Gmail account that got disabled for being linked to other accounts. The answer: do not use Google products for microsite email at all. Mailgun and Cloudflare forwarding came up as clean options. Since the domain is most of a microsite's upfront cost, a site gets a full year before the renewal call, and lead flow is the only metric that counts. Dead sites get dropped, and reselling them was described as rarely worth the effort.

On seasonal pricing, Brandon leases at one flat rate all year and keeps it fair. He has had clients with him for 25 plus years, and each new location adds a couple of pages and about $500 a month. For bigger B2B deals Jesse leans toward revenue share.

Engagement came up next: multi-step quiz forms that ask one question at a time keep visitors longer and stop bot fills, with one member routing those leads into GoHighLevel. Visuals and on-page tables carry more weight now that AI text is cheap. One member pairs OpenArt with Claude Code on a client's real photos to make shorts that do not look AI generated, which landed $600 a month for three shorts a week from a home builder.
