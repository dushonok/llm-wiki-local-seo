FULL ANSWERS

Q1: Do microsites show up in AI Mode, or does AI Mode just pull the Google Business Profile?

A: Both. It's a mixed bag. What we bank on is the old-school results page: two LSAs or two ads up top, and we rank organically right below them. That's bread and butter, and it's what you're seeing in the contest. AI is a cherry on top. We'll catch it when we catch it, but it's not the focus. Shawn added that Google has been pulling phone numbers off business profiles in the results lately, and our sites rank fine with a map pack above or below.

[@Justin Lofton](/@0dbcf831f5d341afae6b62328c0f52de?g=rank-expand-academy)

made a fair point that AI Overviews now show on most searches, so Q2 covers how members are getting cited there.

Q2: Are sites ranking with no backlinks, just from content?

A: Yes. Backlinks are always a function of competition. You need them if you need them, and you don't if you don't. If there was only one roofer in Maryland, that roofer would show up for every roofing term. Thousands of roofers is a different story. A sub niche with real volume and not much competition is the sweet spot, and often all it takes is slightly better structure than the next site. The same goes for AI Overviews. Two members on the call are getting cited there from structure, internal linking and topical coverage alone, and one can see the AI Overview impressions in Search Console. When links do come in, they're fuel, not the engine. If the homepage doesn't link to the hubs you want ranked, the link juice stops at the homepage and the money is wasted.

Q3: What's your kill rule for a site that isn't working?

A: The domain is most of what a microsite costs, so you've already bought a year of data. Let it roll for the year, then decide whether to renew. It's that simple. Shawn's rule is lead flow, nothing else. Rankings and impressions are fun to watch, but the question is whether the phone rings. One lead a month and it stays. Nothing after a year and it goes. We just dropped about 25. Redirects and migrations aren't worth it, and Shawn has tried selling old sites on marketplaces many times without it panning out.

Q4: Google disabled one of my site Gmail accounts. How do I set up email for lots of sites?

A: We don't use Gmail or any Google product on the sites, exactly to avoid that kind of footprint. A few options from the call. Cloudflare does free email forwarding. Formspree handles contact forms. We run Mailgun, about $30 a month with unlimited sending domains. Each domain needs its DNS set up, and that can be automated through their API. Shawn is also looking into Cloudflare Workers as a cheap route.

Q5: Do renters pay less for a form lead than a phone call?

A: Not for us. A VA qualifies every lead by hand, so everything that gets through is a good lead and it's all one rate. Nobody on the call prices lead types differently. Fair warning on forms: in two years we've had maybe 10 good form leads against about 3,000 spam submissions. What helps is a quiz-style form, one question at a time. Bots have a harder time with it. One member runs a five-question quiz on the homepage and pipes every lead into GHL for the client.

Q6: Can I add quizzes and other engagement stuff to a site later with Claude Code?

A: Yes, after a site starts working or right from launch. It's a soft skill. Ask what makes sense on the homepage for that niche, test it, and watch Search Console. One member's tips, from a quiz that converts better than any form they've run: the first question is the key one, ask for contact details only in the last one or two (they're invested by then), and keep it one question at a time. Claude is good at building a custom quiz and walking you through how many questions to ask.

[@John R](/@9b615af5673e4611b65ac53d65a74158?g=rank-expand-academy)

added that visuals and tables carry more weight on the page now that AI text is everywhere. Infographics and tables built on your own data add expertise and time on page.

Q7: Google is fighting the crawlers. Can I still trust DataForSEO for keyword research?

A: Google has been poisoning the results for crawlers for years, and they keep getting better at it. It's cat and mouse, and DataForSEO has fixed it every time Google rolled out a change so far. Justin's flag from the call is worth knowing, though. Justin still uses DataForSEO for a lot, but finds the backlink data limited.

Q8: Seasonal and commercial niches. Per lead, per job, or a flat lease?

A: Whatever you like most. We've done all of them.

[@Brandon M](/@2c2970f1d6a042faa531eccb71b613bd?g=rank-expand-academy)

leases at one price all year, slow months included, and skips per lead because of seasonality. Brandon gives up some upside for longevity: clients have stayed 25+ years, and every new location they open adds about $500 a month for a couple of extra pages. For commercial and B2B, I like rev share. It's bigger money, and the bigger the ticket, the less hassle you get. They make $100k, they peel off $10k to you, and that's cool.

Q9: Lawyers and other licensed niches. Any trouble with licenses when renting leads?

A: We stay away from licensed industries now. Brandon wrote a 30-page breakdown of the rules for financial services, insurance and debt leads, and it's the post to read before you build in that space: [https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/before-you-build-another-financial-services-microsite-read-this](https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/before-you-build-another-financial-services-microsite-read-this?utm_campaign=skool_link_post&utm_content=e0c04038afe74a28a97dccc4b03256df "https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/before-you-build-another-financial-services-microsite-read-this") On a national level, one route is running the site "powered by" a licensed sponsor and working under their license. Attorney leads are very strict, so the room's answer was to work under an attorney. One member's play: build a relationship with the biggest attorney in the niche, run a test powered by their firm in the smaller towns their own site doesn't cover, and price per lead under the $300 to $500+ they already spend. None of this is legal advice, so check the rules for your state.

Q10: How do you upsell a business owner into a directory?

A:

[@Blair Witkowski](/@ab346ce88d4e48cb932c684753540b53?g=rank-expand-academy)

runs it the other way and uses the directory to get clients. The pitch is a $100 verified listing, and verifying means an interview and a reference check. That due diligence shows what the business is missing. On the follow-up call it's low key: "I asked ChatGPT who the best company in town is, and 300 others showed up, but not you." The bells go off, and the conversation turns to SEO on a monthly fee.

Q12: To test a niche, do I build three sites in one niche or three different niches?

A: One niche. We build three to five sites at a time in one industry, in the same region, because statistically about one in five takes off. At $10 to $15 a site, $1,000 builds a lot of tests. Build a cluster, collect the data, find the niche that works, then scale. This is the time, get on it.
