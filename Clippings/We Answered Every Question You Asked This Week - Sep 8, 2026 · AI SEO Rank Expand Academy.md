Q1: My keyword shows zero search volume. Do I skip it?

A: No. Every tool pulls the same Google Ads data and then extrapolates, so in a town of 15,000 to 30,000 people a zero is a guess, not a fact. Shawn has pages on zero volume terms doing 3,000 views a month. And because every SEO reads the same dashboard, those terms are off everybody's radar at the same time.

Q2: The tool says 40 searches a month but the site gets way more than that. Which number is real?

A: The site is real, the tool is an estimate. One member watched a query his tool priced at 40 pull hundreds of actual users on a client site. Small queries are the hardest thing for these tools to see, which is exactly why they undercount the ones nobody else is bidding on.

Q3: I have sites on page one for odd terms and the phone is not ringing. What did I do wrong?

A: Nothing. A new site goes through a growing phase where Google does not know what it is about yet, so whatever surfaces first is what you see, and at a month old that is normal. Put Search Console on one of them and read the real queries, because the terms you actually want are probably sitting at 20 to 40 where a third party tool will not lift them. Then pick the one site that is closest to money and add service pages to it.

Q4: Is there a cheaper way to get Ahrefs or SEMrush style data?

A: DataForSEO is a good solution. There is also an open tool called OpenSEO that you run off Claude Code or self host, plugging in your own DataForSEO and Claude keys, and people are reporting something close to a SEMrush equivalent for about $10 a month. We are testing it. Treat that as a lead to check, not a recommendation yet.

Q5: Do exact match domains work for e-commerce?

A: They work everywhere, including e-com. You can run a Shopify buy button or a full cart on an EMD off the same Shopify backend, so you get the domain match and the conversion. The catch is not the domain, it is who you are up against: e-com is an ad arbitrage game and organic sits under the ads, so check how competitive the term is before you build.

Q6: I want to test a product niche before I go chasing distributors. Cheapest way?

A: Build about five microsites for roughly $50 on the product term, link to Amazon as an affiliate, and let Amazon's own data tell you whether you rank and whether anyone clicks. That is a $50 answer to a question most people spend three months and a lot of phone calls on. If it works, then go find distributors with proof in your hand.

Q7: My research says there is barely any volume for web design in my area. Different service, or different plan?

A: Both, and you already found it. Web design in a town is a thin term; web design for contractors, or for HVAC, is where the searches and the money are. Pick the vertical rather than the geography, and remember the back end is where this pays, because automations and AI voice agents sell to the same client after the site.

Q8: Can I buy a Google Business Profile instead of building one up?

A: Yes, and it flips the usual order. People sell them in niche Facebook groups for about $200 to $300, and abandoned ones can be claimed, so you can choose the niche based on what profile you can actually get. It is a gamble. Shawn has been burned a couple of times and more often they hold. Keep it isolated on its own Gmail and do not go wild with it.

Q9: The domain I want is taken. What do I use?

A: In order: flip the service and location around, then [serviceinlocation.com](http://serviceinlocation.com "http://serviceinlocation.com"), which works even if it is not lovely, then brand it. Something like [elitetowingcrystallake.com](http://elitetowingcrystallake.com "http://elitetowingcrystallake.com") or [towingcrystallakepros.com](http://towingcrystallakepros.com "http://towingcrystallakepros.com") does the job. None of these is a reason to abandon the niche.

Q10: Do all my microsites need to look different from each other?

A: No. One layout that works is fine at scale, because any single site is a drop in the ocean and the person searching in one town is never going to see the one in the next. If it bothers you, run five variants and cycle them. What does matter is not leaving a footprint: no repeated "site built by us" footer, and vary the naming conventions.

Q11: Do backlinks and citations still matter, and which ones?

A: Yes, quality rather than the old volume game. Point local links at your own assets: Yelp, a free BBB listing, Yellow Pages, and the Right Local top 100 list, which you can work through without paying for the subscription. And do not sleep on LinkedIn, it carries enormous authority with Google. One member rewrote his profile around a phrase and ranked for it inside an hour, and if running accounts is too much you only need one persona writing home service articles that link out.

Q12: Is the realtor niche worth going after?

A: I would not. Redfin, Zillow and Trulia own those results with in house SEO teams and 90 plus domain ratings, property search is effectively required to be visible, and even a strong exact match name sits at position five. A "we buy houses" microsite ranked and took a few calls, but the volume was not there. There are easier rooms to be the best in.

Q13: I am on day one and I do not know what I am actually offering. Where do I start?

A: Build first. If you cannot name your skill yet, the Upwork route is going to hurt, because it asks you to sell something specific before you have one. Put up a microsite, learn the thing, and you will have an answer by the end of the week. Two of you said this week that the playbook needs a positioning step before the outreach step, and you are right, we are building it. Until it is in there, that is the honest sequence.

Q14: Can I build microsites in GoHighLevel, and will they rank?

A: They rank. It hosts on Cloudflare natively and supports meta, schema, sitemaps and proper headings, which is all the site needs from the platform.

[@Deacon Nick](/@bddf5d504c53481fa0835fad5c408a7d?g=rank-expand-academy)

on Nick posted three of his own live ones and then wrote out his whole sequence: research the areas and services, buy the domain, copy the JSON out of the builder, spin up a sub account, paste the JSON into AI Studio with a hold instruction, put the extras in additional inputs, then build, and wire the number, form and voice AI in GHL while it cooks.

Q15: A business with 180 five star reviews and a better site still loses the map pack. How?

A: Categories and proximity, usually, not quality. @John R's answer on this got pinned: pull the map competitors with GMB Everywhere or Pleper, match your primary category to what the top rankers share, put that category in the title, H1 and meta, link your money services off the homepage to real service pages, match the profile categories to the site, run a heat map scan to see where you are invisible, then write for those cities and neighborhoods. Reviews are not the lever when everybody has them.

Q16: How much does the business name on the Google profile matter?

A: More than it should. Reviews stay the top priority, but a keyword in the name is heavy, which is the same reason exact match domains keep working. People do legally change the registered name to match what gets searched, and a keyword name will out rank a branded one. Worth knowing what you are up against even if you never do it.

Q17: Does the site builder leave a footprint Google can spot?

A: Less than people fear.

[@Deacon Nick](/@bddf5d504c53481fa0835fad5c408a7d?g=rank-expand-academy)

went and did the research rather than guessing: image hosting has changed and the forms carry unique codes, so the obvious tells are not there. The footprints that get people are the self inflicted ones, the same footer credit on 40 sites and the same naming pattern across a portfolio.

Q18: Is a resources section worth adding to a microsite?

A:

[@Andrew Perez](/@1e165515aaef44149c88a77695df6864?g=rank-expand-academy)

added one that answers what searchers actually ask and is seeing over a minute average time on page a few weeks after launch. That is the whole argument. It also feeds the service pages, because the questions people read are the pages you build next.

Q19: Is there a tool that tells me if a niche is worth building in?

A: The Niche Validator in the classroom gives you a score, and the standing caveat holds: the score is the shortcut, the live search result is the final word.

[@Alex Zubarev](/@d8d5216433f14a45b21efc0438ee3697?g=rank-expand-academy)

built his own on top of DataForSEO that scans a niche and returns build, review or skip, and he ran about 450 niches for around $5, built on the best gap and ranked in two days. It is pre beta and not shared yet. Do not trust any of them with the numbers unhedged, and do what

[@Joel Efosa](/@b041ab98e8a0490b9941972f48895c3f?g=rank-expand-academy)

said and have Claude audit the build after.

Q20: I am not technical. Where do I begin?

A: Any AI will do the tweaking. Hand it the site folder and tell it what you want changed in plain English, and Shawn recorded a walk through of exactly that. Your first site will not be your best one and it is not supposed to be. A site costs a couple of dollars, so build one, get it live, and let it teach you. Build twenty over time, keep the winners, prune the ones that only cost you money.
