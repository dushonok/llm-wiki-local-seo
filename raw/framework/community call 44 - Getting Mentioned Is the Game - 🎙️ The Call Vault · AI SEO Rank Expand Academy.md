## What we covered

The WikiLLM memory system is becoming the default way people work with Claude. Shlomi walked through why: instead of dumping PDFs and notes in one messy folder, you give Claude a [CLAUDE.md](http://claude.md/ "http://CLAUDE.md"), a wiki folder, and a raw folder, then say "ingest." Claude files everything for you and the context stays clean over every project. Teja and Shlomi both run a version of it per client so Claude always knows the whole business. Shawn is going to add the starting prompt and a blank skeleton to the classroom.

Claude Fable is the new favorite. It is fast, it saves on tokens, and it is very good at planning and coordinating big jobs. Sandra built the One-Click Microsite Builder with it and went to lunch while it finished. It spun up a 3,000 page directory plan in under a minute. The catch is nobody knows how long it stays this cheap, so use it while you can.

Getting cited is the real AI SEO game right now. Mentions in local Facebook groups, on YouTube (the single biggest factor, and the number two cited source behind Reddit), and from influencers all feed into what ChatGPT and Gemini repeat back. One member shared the gold: take your top queries, turn them into questions, use DataForSEO to see who gets cited in Gemini and ChatGPT, then go get links on the URLs that keep showing up. Another member turned a three year old Bradley Benner backlink idea into a profile page that links out to your citations and gets them indexed.

Moving a Google Business Profile without losing everything came up through a real client case. The group agreed: proximity to the address is the number one factor, so brand it even when locations are close, do not 301 the old site, and keep it live with a "we are now part of" banner. Whitespark will fix your citations by hand for about $395, and if a listing gets suspended you can get it back with the right documents (a couple of unsuspend vendors were shared). Fun detail from Whitespark's 45 factors video: NAP consistency ranked low.

On the build side, directories were the pain point. Pages built in JetEngine do not rank because they turn into long ugly URLs, so the move is clean HTML with Astro on Replit, building in Claude first to keep the agent costs down. And the reframe of the week came from Teja's model: he runs 35 client sites at $100 a month on autopilot, with Claude even scheduling 50 days of social posts per client. You are not selling a website, you are selling the whole system, the site, the CRM, the profile, the leads, which is why nobody leaves.

Then everyone shared what they built. Jesse made a hail-trace tool in two days with Fable. Braden is refreshing stale content on financial sites and one builder sent 640 leads in a month with no ad spend. Kate logs every project to GitHub and a 25 tab Google Sheet so any agent can pick up where she left off. And John De Silva got 4,400 AI Overview impressions in 28 days using ChatGPT.
