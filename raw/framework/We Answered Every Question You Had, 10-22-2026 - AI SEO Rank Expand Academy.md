Q1: I'm a month in. Five sites live, zero leads, one click. Is that normal?

A: Yes. Five sites live in a month is the hard part done. The first thing I check after a sitemap goes in is the site: operator on Google, to see which pages are actually indexed. Leads lag behind that.

[@Deacon Nick](/@bddf5d504c53481fa0835fad5c408a7d?g=rank-expand-academy)

was about three weeks into building when his first ones came in, and

[@Ruth Gray](/@15298ce8dd01498cbff1f04c752a8e0c?g=rank-expand-academy)

s first four arrived when her sites were around six weeks old.

[@Captain Jérôme](/@225c574876134c279534ce61fd9c2ef0?g=rank-expand-academy)

who asked, is tracking every number on the way there, and that's a good habit to have when they start.

Q2: Can I use Search Console without tying all my sites together?

A: For your first couple of sites, Search Console is fine, and it's honestly the best way to learn how these sites get seen. Past that I keep them out. I've seen people with 100+ sites on one Search Console account get them all banned.

[@Brandon M](/@2c2970f1d6a042faa531eccb71b613bd?g=rank-expand-academy)

's point on the workarounds: Google still knows where the account was signed up from. For a bigger portfolio, @Shawn Buckles points to ProRankTracker or something set up on DataForSEO, and

[@Nadya Ershova](/@88816c0a359c4a5eb73166ce87f5ba39?g=rank-expand-academy)

uses Ahrefs' free traffic tracker.

Q3: Should my first site cover one service or several?

A: We optimize for one primary service and add a few related ones so Google gets more context on the main one. @Deacon Nick lets the search data decide which services make the cut, and the builder asks for six.

[@Blair Witkowski](/@ab346ce88d4e48cb932c684753540b53?g=rank-expand-academy)

starts with four to eight and adds more once the site is ranking, because the tighter the site is at launch, the better its chances.

Q4: I already run the business. How do I use microsites to feed my own crew?

A: You've got the biggest opportunity of anyone here, because you can run your own leads. What I'd do: keep your brand off the sites except inside each site's logo image, and no links back to your main website. If the obvious service plus city domains are taken, @Deacon Nick tries smaller towns and other word orders before falling back to .net, and @Blair Witkowski is happy with a brandable name that has the service and the town in it. And if the head term is brutal, go a level down. You can't go head to head with "plumber", but a specific job like main water line replacement is a different fight.

Q5: My English isn't great and I don't want to cold call. How do I sell the leads?

A: You don't need the phone. Break the ice by email, then send a free lead or two. Once the leads are coming in, how good your English is stops mattering. You're the rainmaker. James did exactly this: two free leads by email, asked if the owner wanted more, gave a price, and got a yes. Shawn has never cold-called a business either. And even in a high-ticket niche I'd still give the first lead away. The relationship with the contractor is worth a lot. Q6: Kinsta or Cloudflare for the sites Claude builds?

A: Kinsta is super pricey. I'd go straight to Claude plus Cloudflare now. Shawn agrees, there are cheaper options that do the same job.

Q7: Can I share one gallery of my real work across all my sites?

A: I wouldn't link them across your sites. Ask yourself: if Google found that footprint and deindexed every site sharing those photos, would that hurt? If yes, don't share them. @Brandon M keeps one original gallery per site. The footprint is the one area he won't dance around.

Q8: One business, twenty sites. Same Google Business Profile and phone number on all of them?

A: The same phone number is typically a no-go for me. On the profile, @Blair Witkowski's rule: if every site is the same niche and the same brand, it's treated a lot like a franchise and is somewhat safe. If they're different businesses, don't link the sites to the profile or the profile to the sites.

Q9: Claude says neighborhood pages are doorway pages. Is it right?

A: Claude is giving you general advice. The builder is the specific version that's been proven to work hundreds of times, so Shawn and @James Wrest both say start from it and let Claude build on top. @Blair Witkowski builds neighborhood pages on a lot of his sites, not just microsites. A doorway page is the same page with a few words swapped. Give each one its own content, images and headings, and ask yourself: does this page help anybody if they found it?

Q10: The service happens in one country and the buyers search from another. Does a microsite still work?

A: It can. Microsites are mostly for local business, but the structure works for anything location-based. @Blair Witkowski searched the example and found weak sites on the first two pages, and he'd position it as the business that connects you with the provider. @Brandon M's check comes first, though: is anyone searching for it? Zero searches means zero calls.

Q11: Is Google's new anti-spam update going to hit microsites?

A: Not from what I'm seeing. It has been running for a few months and microsite rankings haven't moved. The big changes are on conventional, non-local sites. @Abel F and @Brandon M read it the same way: the target is the mass builds that swap the town name across thousands of near-identical pages. A site that looks and works like a real local business is a different thing.

Q12: WhatConverts put my account in manual review. Did I burn it?

A: No. Add the tracking script to the site, which we always do anyway. @Alan Thomas did, sent them an email, and was approved two minutes later.
