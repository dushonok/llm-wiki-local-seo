## What we covered

A contest result opened the call: one site's number-one ranking crashed after a big rebuild, a reminder not to overhaul a page that's already working. The video-heavy contest entries pushed Blair Witkowski to automate instead of leaning on 22 VAs: running Claude Desktop through Novamira across several WordPress sites at once, he matched a month of VA output in about five hours. That led into scaling compute past one machine, VPS options like Hostinger or DigitalOcean droplets for running more Claude instances in parallel.

On Google Business Profiles, Steve Alexander has built 51 using a T-Mobile multi-line plan, cheap Android phones, and location-spoofing apps: swap the phone number (not the SIM) once a Gmail hits its two-profile cap, and most verify within about 24 hours, with roughly 5% triggering video verification. On service-area businesses, the group leaned toward showing the address on both the profile and the site, since hiding it makes the map pack much harder to reach in competitive niches. Reviews matter less than raw count suggests: recency, frequency, a fully filled-out listing, fresh photos and posts, and an exact-match name carry real weight, and most top earners run 40 to 100 profiles rather than piles of reviews. To protect a profile that's making money, the group discussed a real LLC or the cheaper route, a DBA, so the business has a legal right to the name Google sees, which also helps a suspended profile get reinstated.

On monetizing leads, one host walks clients from commission to a flat monthly fee using a simple formula: for every dollar a client sends, the goal is four dollars back in net profit for them, tracked over three to six months before offering a discounted flat fee. Others bill per lead directly through Stripe. Commission carries real risk of getting stood up without a personal relationship, while flat fee avoids that but adds churn risk. An existing rank-and-rent competitor in a niche was read as proof of demand rather than a reason to stay out, especially now that AI makes a fifty-page site realistic instead of the old one-pager. Niche dependence on the map pack tracks with urgency: towing and locksmith lean hard on ads and the map pack, while higher-consideration niches leave more room to compete without one.

With Google reportedly serving inaccurate data to rank trackers, the group weighed spreading Search Console access across separate or aged Gmail accounts, or tools like Multilogin and AdsPower, against the fact that large, legitimate Search Console portfolios on one account haven't caused members problems. For behavior data beyond Search Console, Microsoft Clarity and Bing Webmaster Tools came up, along with a member's own conversion-audit skill. The call closed on community notes: a jargon glossary kicked off live, new members introduced themselves as the group nears 1,000 members, and the hosts confirmed another microsite contest, this time archiving the top sites as static HTML snapshots.
