#### Your data is ready. This step generates the site.

Every directory built with the Blueprint has the same bones: the page set, the URLs, the navigation, the order of the sections on every page. That is the master template, and it is the result of a real state build reviewed until it was right. What changes from one directory to the next is the look (colors, type, imagery, a pattern or two) and the facts. Claude takes the look from the website you named in Step 4 and the facts from your data. It does not invent a new layout.

## Step 1. Gate 4, Plan

In the same Claude Code session, type: `Run Gate 4.` Claude shows you the plan: which cities get a page (any city with at least three real businesses; smaller towns that are far from any city get a short page of their own), how many pages in total, which pages go into the sitemap at launch and which wait, where the featured slots sit, and the colors and type it derived from your inspired site, with the contrast measured and any fix recorded. The plan ends with a numbered list of decisions that are yours, each with Claude's recommendation: what to do with the other-trade businesses that came through, whether to sort confirmed businesses first, and anything else the data raised. Every listing you drop or change goes into one file Claude keeps, so a rerun keeps your decisions. Read it. If a city is missing that you know has businesses, say so. Approve.

## Step 2. Gate 5, Build

Type: `Run Gate 5.` Claude copies the template, fills it with your data, and writes the copy that is not data: a fact sheet first (every claim about law, licensing, permits or weather with its source, and what the copy may not say), then 500 to 800 words and four to six FAQs per city page, written about the place in relation to the trade, never a list of what the businesses say. A checker rejects any number that is not in your data and any sentence that repeats across cities, and a linter runs on every block. The buyer's guide on the home page is written with the seo-content-engine skill when DataForSEO is connected, by hand when it is not. Then it runs the build and tells you when it passes.

City copy runs on your Claude plan like the enrichment did, about 18,000 tokens a city. Claude tells you the size before it starts.

This needs Node installed on your machine. If the build fails on the first run with a message about `npm` or `node`, install Node from [nodejs.org](http://nodejs.org/ "http://nodejs.org") and run Gate 5 again.

## Step 3. Look at it

In the `site` folder Claude names, double-click `preview-site.command` (Mac) or `preview-site.bat` (Windows). That serves the site exactly as it will be deployed. Do not open the files directly; they show unstyled with dead links that way. Open the home page, one city page, one business page, the claim page. Check five things:

1. The city page reads like it was written about that city, not swapped in.
2. The businesses on it show real numbers: rating, review count, attributes.
3. Nothing says "true" where the data said "not enough info."
4. The look came from the site you named. The structure did not, and should not: the page set and the sections are the template on purpose. A small line above a heading or icons on the home page are fine when your inspired site has them and each one says something specific. The tells are the generic version: the same label above every heading, a row of stock icons with a sentence that says nothing, buttons you cannot read.
5. Your own microsites, if you have any, look exactly like every other listing.

## Step 4. Fix in the data, not the page

If a business is wrong, fix the row and run Gate 5 again. If the look is off, tell Claude what to change and run Gate 5 again. Never edit a generated page by hand. The next rebuild would erase it.

## Why rebuild instead of edit

A directory changes constantly: a business claims its listing, someone buys a spot, a company closes. On WordPress that is a login and a plugin and a VA. Here it is one row in a file and one command. That is the whole reason the site is static.
