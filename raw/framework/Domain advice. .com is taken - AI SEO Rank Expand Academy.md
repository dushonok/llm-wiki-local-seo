Hey Daniel, back in the day I'd say if .COM isn't available move on. But I think there's more nuisance now.

I'm getting into the habit of consulting the Claude Code microsite contest session (because it has daily real data on what's working). Here's what it is saying:

The .com being taken isn’t a reason to move on. Take a variant and build.

What the contest showed about domains: the sites holding points right now are a hyphenated .com, a .org, a .net and a .us. The two sites with the exact keyword .com and .org on the mold side sit next to each other in the 20s.

Nobody gained a position because of the extension. Every position gained came from the business entity behind the domain, meaning the brand name matching the keyword, a phone and address that show up the same way everywhere, and ten to fifteen profiles that all point at the site.

So the question isn’t .com or .org. It’s whether you can make the brand name the keyword. You can with any of these:

- A .ca variant with the words reordered or a modifier added, like kitchenrenos\[city\].ca or \[city\][kitchenrenopros.ca](http://kitchenrenopros.ca "http://kitchenrenopros.ca"). In Canada a .ca beats a .org for local trust every time.
- A hyphenated .com. The mold leader is one.
- The .org only if nothing else works. It ranks fine, it just reads like a nonprofit.

On the two taken domains: one bad site and one coming soon page is what a winnable market looks like. If both were strong, that’s when you’d worry. The coming soon site tells you someone else saw the same numbers you did. The site that gets the profiles and pages built first takes it, not the one that registered first.
