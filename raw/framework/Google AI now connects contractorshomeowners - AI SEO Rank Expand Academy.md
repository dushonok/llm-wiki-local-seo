Google AI now connects contractors/homeowners...

Contractors can get a call from Google's AI this week, asking for an open time and a price for a homeowner.

On Nov 16, Google started rolling out an Ask Maps update in the U.S.. Someone can ask Google Maps to find a home repair appointment, and Google's AI calls local businesses to check times and prices for them. What your CSR says on that call is what goes back to the homeowner.

The setting behind it:

Open Google Business Profile and go to More, then Business Profile settings, then Advanced settings. Under "Google automated calls and text messages," make sure "Bookings and inquiries from customers" is turned on.

Google's announcement doesn't say whether Ask Maps follows that setting, but it's the one Google's help page gives for automated calls made on a customer's behalf. That page says:

\-Calls can book appointments and confirm prices and availability

\-Every call is recorded

\-There's no charge to you or the customer

\-Google limits repeat calls and avoids late nights and early mornings

\-The feature isn't available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana or Nebraska

\-Who gets the call

Google hasn't said how Ask Maps picks which businesses to call. When it added AI calling to Search last year, a Google spokesperson said it picked businesses based on regular local search rankings. If Ask Maps works the same way, your map pack rankings decide who gets the call.

What I'd do:

\-Turn the setting on for every location

\-Tell your CSRs that calls from Google's AI are real customer requests, and to hang up on anyone who wants money or a login to "verify" your listing

\-Give them a real answer for your most common jobs: the next open day and time, plus your service fee or a price range

\-Search your business name and phone number on Google Maps, and delete or report any profile you don't manage
