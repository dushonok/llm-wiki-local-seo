#### You need two things: a service, and a state.

**If you already have microsites, this step is done.** Build the directory around the vertical you own, in the state those sites are in. Pool builders in Orlando, Naples and Miami means a Florida pool builders directory. That is the whole reason to build one.

**Starting from scratch? Pick the way you would pick a microsite, then widen it to a state.**

## Step 1. Talk it through with the Niche Finder GPT

Same tool as the Microsite Playbook. You are looking for a boring, high-ticket local service where nobody has a favorite company.

Put in your service plus the five biggest cities in the state. You are not looking for one winnable city. You are looking for a service that is winnable in most of them.

## Step 3. Look at the state search.

Search "\[service\] in \[state\]" and "\[service\] \[state\]" and look at what ranks. If the results are a mix of national directories and a few local businesses, there is room. If a strong state-wide directory already owns it, pick another service or another state.

## Step 4. Run it through the seven checks below.

A service that passes all seven is a directory. A service that fails two is a microsite.

### ✅ What makes a good directory vertical?

1. **It is an exact Google Maps category.** Step 3 pulls listings by category, so the service has to be one, word for word. "Swimming pool contractor" is a category. "Luxury pool designer" is not. Type your service into Google Maps and look at the label under the business names; that label is the category.
2. **The category name says the trade.** Broad categories let other trades in. "Fence contractor" in one state came back with a third of the rows being landscapers, handymen and roofers who also do fences, and you sort those out by hand later. "Swimming pool contractor," "roofing contractor," "septic system service" come back clean. If the category could describe three trades, expect to sort.
3. **People compare before they buy.** A directory is for a job someone gets three quotes on: a pool, a roof, a fence, a septic system, solar, a remodel. It is not for the job people call the first result on. Nobody browses a directory of locksmiths at midnight.
4. **The ticket is four figures or more, and nobody has a favorite company.** Same rule as a microsite. A $2,000 job makes a $97 listing an easy sale. Brand loyalty is the enemy: a state full of franchise names is a state where the businesses do not need you.
5. **Most businesses have a website.** The Blueprint reads each business's own site to fill in what it does, what it offers and whether it says it is licensed. A trade where half the listings are a phone number and nothing else enriches poorly and gives you thin pages. Handymen and mobile services tend that way. Contractors, installers and builders tend the other way.
6. **The state has the right number of them.** A few hundred to a few thousand reviewed businesses is the band. Under 200 and few cities clear the three-business floor, so the directory is thin. Over 5,000 and the enrichment is a big run on your Claude plan and the build takes days. Florida pool builders was about 2,000. Colorado fence contractors was about 400. Texas roofing contractors is over 8,000, which is a lot of directory for a first one. If DataForSEO is connected to your Claude Code, ask Claude to size the category in your state; it costs about a cent and takes a minute.
7. **There is a real "how to choose" to write.** Licensed trades, permits, warranties, materials, seasons. A directory earns trust with a buyer's guide that says something, and a trade with nothing to explain has nothing to guide. This is also where the calls in Step 8 come from: "your listing does not show your license yet."

## Three things to know here

- **One service, one state, one directory.** Not two services, not the whole country. A second state is a second directory, later.
- **Low search volume is fine.** The state term might show almost nothing in a volume tool. The city terms under it add up, and one listing sale pays for the site.
- **A wrong pick costs an afternoon.** The pull is a few dollars. If the clean report in Step 4 comes back full of other trades or half the businesses have no website, pick again before the enrichment runs. That is the last cheap moment to change your mind.
