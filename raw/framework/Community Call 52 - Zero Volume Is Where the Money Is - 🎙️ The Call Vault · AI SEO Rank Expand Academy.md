52 - Zero Volume Is Where the Money Is

54:39

Roughly the one-year mark for the community, and the call kept coming back to one idea: the keyword tools show everyone the same numbers, so the gaps are the opportunity. We also got a look at where things are heading with directories.

## Things mentioned

- DataForSEO, the data behind the volume tools, which cross-references Google Ads data and still only estimates.
- OpenSEO, an open SEO tool you run off Claude Code or self-host with your own DataForSEO and Claude keys, for around $10 a month instead of a full SEMrush or Ahrefs subscription.
- Google Search Console, to see the real queries your new sites rank for.
- OpenArt AI ([https://openart.ai/](https://openart.ai/ "https://openart.ai/")), for social content built from a client's own photos so it does not look fake.
- Codex and Claude, for building the sites.
- Shopify, for putting a buy button or cart on an EMD.
- Amazon affiliate links, for a cheap way to test whether a product term ranks before you chase distributors.
- Yelp, BBB, Yellow Pages, and Right Local's top-100 list, for local citations, and LinkedIn as a high-authority place to publish.
- A directory build the team is working on, as a complement to microsites.

## What we covered

The through line was search volume. Every keyword tool pulls from the same Google Ads data and then guesses, so a term that reads as "zero volume" often is not, especially in a town of twenty or thirty thousand people. One member has put up zero-volume pages that do three thousand views a month. The takeaway was that microsites are part database and part art: you build with a lot of copy and a lot of service pages, and you let Search Console tell you the queries you never would have guessed. Because everyone else trusts the tools, those gaps are wide open.

That fed into a build-and-learn mindset. A site costs a couple of dollars, so a hunch is a cheap bet that buys you data. Build many, read the data, keep the winners, and prune the rest. A member showed a batch of month-old sites ranking for odd long-tail terms rather than the main keyword, and the answer was that new sites go through a growing phase, so put Search Console on one and give it time, because the real keywords are usually sitting just out of the tool's view.

On niches, the room was honest about the hard ones. E-commerce EMDs work but you are fighting huge ad budgets, and high-ticket dropshipping lives or dies on getting distributors, which is a phone-and-pavement job. Realtor is close to a dead end against Redfin and Zillow. The better plays are the boring local services, and adding more service pages to a site that already ranks.

We also got into the quieter levers. Local links from Yelp, BBB, and a LinkedIn persona that writes home-service articles. Social content built from real photos rather than obvious AI. Buying a GMB first and building the site around it. And a footprint reminder: one good layout at scale is fine, just vary the naming and do not repeat a footer across every site.

Looking ahead, Jesse and Shawn are building a directory course as a complement to microsites, which is also where the GEO and AI-citation upside will come from, since AI tends to cite well-structured sites even when they are not powerful.
