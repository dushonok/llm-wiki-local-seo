**What we covered**  
Tom asked how to build a directory strong enough to pass link value to a microsite. Blair keeps his hyperlocal: realtor outreach hung on the national XYZ month calendar, verified badges shipped as HTML rather than a JPEG so the link comes back to the listing, and the top spot on his best of pages sold from about $100, which pays for a press release whose three links go to the home page, the listing and the best of article. His VAs write hyperlocal articles from realtor YouTube videos, which gives him a reason to go back to those realtors and the Chamber of Commerce for links. Run it like a publisher, with a face and schema behind it.

On rankings, the leading contest entry pairs an exact match domain with a lot of copy high on the page. Jesse's advice: 150 words in the hero or right under the H1 for AI Overviews, then keep going. Brandon has built 5,000 to 10,000 word monster pages for 12 plus years, with a call to action every few sections, because you never know which passage an LLM will cite.

John built a microsite for a clothing alteration client that pulled her leads in its first week indexed and now outranks his own long running business site. He had also asked her to rename her Google Business Profile to match the microsite. Jesse's read: that is a coin flip and Google can crack down, but she changed it over a month ago and the immediate risk has passed.

On images, Shawn uses Ideogram for logos and Midjourney for photorealistic shots, both with a dialed in base prompt. Nadia (sp?) calls the models through Runware's API to run one prompt across all of them, and the ChatGPT image generator won on quality per dollar.

The last stretch was a live AI visibility dashboard one member is building on DataForSEO. It scrapes daily to show where a client appears across Google AI Overviews, Gemini and Perplexity, and which sources get cited. Sixty to seventy prompts cost about $22 per client per month, and it helped him close a $2,500 personal injury client who came with no referral. Other wins: four clients in a week for one member, six sites up two weeks in for Marcus B, 20 New Zealand microsites on one dashboard for Sandra, and first two leads in a day for Nick.
