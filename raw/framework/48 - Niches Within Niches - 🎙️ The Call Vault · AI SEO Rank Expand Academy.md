#48 - Niches Within Niches

1:00:52

No agenda this week, so it turned into a wins and tactics session with a bunch of new members. The thread running through all of it was how to pick a niche and how cheaply you can now build and rank at volume.

## Things mentioned

- GoHighLevel, and its new AI mode that builds the whole pipeline and schedules months of social content. The reference was a 44-minute video, "How to start an agency in 2026 with GHL."
- The Microsite Builder beta, which turns a form and a ChatGPT prompt into a static flat-HTML microsite that is about 80% of the way there.
- Claude Code with DataForSEO, to pull a full competition and backlink audit (fund it with about $25-30 and store credentials in a.env file).
- Claude for Google (the Sci-Bard Chrome extension), to analyze a site and suggest subniches.
- ChatGPT, for content generation and spinning client images for social.
- [tinypng.com](http://tinypng.com/ "http://tinypng.com"), free, to compress oversized images to about a tenth of the size.
- Twilio, to call US businesses from a local number and lift your pickup rate.
- SiteGround, Namecheap, Replit, and Cloudflare, for hosting and masking your footprint.
- Shopify headless with Claude Code, for e-commerce builds.
- Hermes agent with local models, for building microsites (a newer member's setup).
- ZimmWriter, a Claude-based bulk article writer, driven by topical maps (Justin Lofton uses it heavily; Sandra found it too restricting).
- The GHL prospecting lesson in the classroom, [skool.com/rank-expand-academy/prospecting-with-ghl](http://skool.com/rank-expand-academy/prospecting-with-ghl?utm_campaign=skool_link_classroom&utm_content=e0c04038afe74a28a97dccc4b03256df "http://skool.com/rank-expand-academy/prospecting-with-ghl?utm_campaign=skool_link_classroom&utm_content=e0c04038afe74a28a97dccc4b03256df"), and the Microsite Builder at [build.rankexpand.com](http://build.rankexpand.com/ "http://build.rankexpand.com").

## What we covered

Blair opened with the strangest win we have had. Over breakfast at a conference he bought a private Facebook group of 300,000 Filipino virtual assistants, closed it in about seven minutes, and plans to sell them cheap courses on landing American jobs. He will share the link for anyone who needs VAs. Kate added a timely note that ad networks are cracking down on AI content, so she is recording her process by voice so a real person can make it read human.

The connective thread was niche selection. New member Phil asked how to pick one, and the answer that kept coming back was to go a level deeper. Locksmith is a bloodbath, but car locksmith or garage-door locksmith is open, and the same holds for concrete, or wet rooms then wet rooms for the elderly. Mark put a number on it, one city had 30 concrete competitors and the next town over had 3, so he builds around the easy one.

Mark and Tony showed what volume looks like. Mark builds 100 to 300 sites at a time because a $10 domain plus a few dollars of static hosting means that if even 30% rank it is a real business, and he is seeing rankings in two to four weeks. Tony runs about 30 sites and takes a commission on the jobs instead of charging per lead, and he just closed 2,800 euros in commissions plus 4,200 in web design.

Bryan changed his mind on GoHighLevel after watching an agency owner's video. GHL now has an AI mode that builds the whole pipeline and six months of scheduled social content, which is what gets a client cited, so his VAs can run it on autopilot. Blair's counter was worth keeping, that contractors do not care about any of it, only about the phone ringing.

Brandon made the case for feeding the AI. A 100 to 150 word summary at the top of a long page, plus regular refreshes and a few HTML calculators built with Claude, turned into a 32% traffic lift in a month, and his sites that already did this never took the May hit. We also got into cold-call closing (five sites for $1,500 with the contract and card right off the call), the Microsite Builder beta, hosting footprint, and naming the specific dying tree people actually search instead of generic tree removal.
