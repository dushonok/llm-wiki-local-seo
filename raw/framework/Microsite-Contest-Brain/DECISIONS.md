# Decision guides

Each guide is a question a member really asks, the answer the contest supports, and the facts behind it. Fact and rule numbers refer to `BRAIN.md`.

## 1. Should I build in this service and this town?

**Answer:** search the term with the location pinned to the town and read page one before you buy anything.

Build if page one is mostly general directories, franchise location pages from out of town, and thin sites.

Walk away if page one has real local companies with years of reviews, a government page on the subject, or a directory that covers only that trade.

**Why:** the same builders with the same tools scored on mold on 26 of 26 checks and on radon on 2 of 26 (F-04). The winner's own radon site finished at 34 (F-07). The radon page one was a 26 year old company, the state's radon page and a radon only directory (F-08).

**How to do the check:** `knowledge/chapters/results-08-how-to-check-your-real-rankings.md` and `tools/make_search_link.py`.

## 2. New brand or a real business?

**Answer:** if you can build for a real business that already has reviews and a history, do that. If you cannot, build a new brand and expect to do far more work on the site and on listings.

**Why:** the radon number one was a 24 page site for a company operating since 1999 with about 225 Google reviews. It reached number one in 48 days on the harder search (F-09, F-10). The same builder's new brand for the same search never reached the top 40 (F-12). The mold winner had no history. He reached number one with 47 pages, ended with 119, and built 63 listings (F-14, F-19, F-27).

## 3. How big should the site be at launch?

**Answer:** launch a complete site, not a large one. A long home page, a full page for each service, and town pages you can write separately. Do not chase a page count.

**Why:** the winner reached number one with 47 pages (F-19). Page count and town page count did not separate sites that scored from sites that did not (F-24). Home page length did: about 3,750 words on a typical scoring site against about 2,100 (F-23). The winner's home page had about 4,600 words at his first number one and has about 6,800 now (F-15, F-19).

## 4. How many town pages?

**Answer:** as many as you can write so that each could only be about that town. Redirect the ones that cannot pass that test.

**Why:** the winner's 36 town pages share about 29 percent of their text. Templated town pages we audited share 50 to 70 percent (F-17). He redirected 1 or 2 thin ones in week one instead of padding them (F-52). No town page ranked for the contest search, so they are supporting pages, not ranking pages (F-20).

## 5. What do I do in the first week?

**Answer, in order:**

1. Research page one and list your service pages and town pages.
2. Build the site and write each page from notes on what already ranks.
3. Go live.
4. Create the social profiles and build 20 to 25 main listings. Send them to be indexed.
5. On days 4 to 7, check what Google has indexed and which page is ranking for your main keyword.
6. If the wrong page is ranking, fix it now.

**Why:** this is the winner's order (F-21, F-22, F-27, F-51). Full steps: `knowledge/05-winner-own-account.md`.

## 6. The wrong page is ranking for my main keyword. What now?

**Answer:** keep the main keyword in the title and main heading of the home page only. If your brand name contains the keyword, use a shorter form of the name on every other page. Then ask Google to reindex the home page and the pages you changed. Give it 3 to 4 days, the same gap he left before his first check.

**Why:** this is exactly what happened to the winner in days 4 to 7, and what he did (F-51). On his site today the services page is titled "All Mold Services | Gettysburg Mold Pros" and the contact page heading is "Contact Us".

## 7. My site was climbing and now it has dropped. What do I do?

**Answer:** nothing, for at least a week. Do not rewrite it. If you have just added pages, leave them: taking them out is another change. Keep adding listings and posting.

**Why:** the winner touched page one in week three, then sat at 17 to 19 for ten days before going to number one (F-33). After his first number one he dropped to 13, 15 and 12 and was number one again five days after the update (F-33, F-39). Eleven mold sites scored at some stage and only two did it on more than 6 of 26 checks (F-35). Second place was rebuilt once and never touched again, and it scored on every check (F-26).

## 8. My site jumped onto page one. Has it worked?

**Answer:** not yet. A jump is Google trying the site out. Judge it by where it sits across two or three weeks.

**Why:** F-35. One radon site went from 29 to 7 in a day and was out of the top 40 two days after its last page one appearance.

## 9. How many listings, and which ones?

**Answer:** 20 to 25 in the first days, then 5 to 10 a week. Start with social profiles and the well known listing sites. Use the same name and phone everywhere, and list the profiles in your site code.

**Why:** F-27. Five of ten scoring sites named profiles in their code, against one of ten that did not score (F-23). Listings on MapQuest, Facebook, Patch, BBB, Yelp, Houzz and Clutch ranked for the contest searches (F-30). Free publishing sites never did (F-31). On the final check three pages contestants built on other platforms were on page one when only one contest website was (F-29).

**The list:** `data/winner_citations.csv` has all 63 in five groups.

## 10. Should I start a blog?

**Answer:** yes, a small one. You do not need a large blog to reach number one. Start with five or six posts that are truly local, one question each. Add more later, a few at a time.

**Why:** the winner reached number one with six posts (F-37). No blog post ever ranked for the contest search (F-40). The posts did win narrow searches afterwards (F-40). His batch of 53 in one day was an accident and he calls it a mistake (F-38).

**Do not** write a post on the same phrase as one of your service pages (F-42).

## 11. How much should I post on social?

**Answer:** two to four posts a week on each account, on weekdays, starting in the first week. Reuse one post across accounts.

**Why:** F-43, F-44. The winner logged 82 posts over eight weeks, never on a weekend. At the start the same title went out on three accounts on the same day.

**What it did for ranking:** we cannot measure it. Of his four social accounts, only the Facebook page ranked for the contest search, as high as 9 (F-28).

## 12. Should I buy guest posts, a press release or build cloud pages?

**Answer:** know what they are before you decide. The winner used all three. They are the least durable part of what he did, and paid links are against Google's guidelines. The Academy reports them. It does not recommend them.

**Why:** F-45 to F-50. Two of his four guest posts were gone a week after the contest (F-46). Press releases ranked for both searches but came and went within days (F-50). His links went to a site that was already built out and had already reached number one once (F-19).

If the member decides to do a press release anyway, the anatomy of his is in `knowledge/06-winner-off-page-records.md`.

## 13. Do I need an exact match domain? Does the extension matter?

**Answer:** use an exact match domain, as the Blueprint says, and do not expect it to do the work. Take the .com if you can.

**Why:** almost every entry had an exact match domain, so it separated nobody (F-54). All three sites that finished in the points were .com, and a .net and a .org scored on some checks (F-55).

## 14. Should I show a street address or a phone number?

**Answer:** show a phone number for the customer's sake. Show an address only if it is real.

**Why:** second place had no phone number and held page two for a month, so a phone was not what ranked sites (F-57). Six of ten scoring sites showed a street address against one of ten that did not score (F-23), and both number one sites show one. The radon number one's address is a real business. We do not know what is at the winner's. The Blueprint rule on addresses stands.

## 15. How long until I know whether it worked?

**Answer:** give the whole site six to eight weeks before you judge it, and three to six months before you call it. A single jump or drop is a different question: see guides 7 and 8.

**Why:** the winner's first number one came on day 41 and the radon number one's on day 48 (F-33, F-09). The whole contest ran about nine weeks, which is inside the Blueprint's own waiting period.

## 16. What will it earn?

**Answer:** the contest measured rankings, not income. The one figure we have is modest.

**Why:** the winner had over 30 calls and $214 in converted leads by November 25, from a number one ranking in a small town (F-58).
