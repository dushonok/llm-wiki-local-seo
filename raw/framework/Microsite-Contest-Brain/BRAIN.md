# The Microsite Contest Brain: core file

Version: frozen at the end of the contest. Rankings end with the final check on November 17, 2026. The winner's own account and records were received on November 25, 2026 and cover the contest period.

This is the one file to load if you can only load one. Everything in it is backed by a longer file in this pack. `MANIFEST.md` says which.

## 1. What this pack is, and what it is not

The Rank Expand Academy ran a public test: 32 members entered 61 brand new local service websites for two Google searches, and we checked the rankings through the contest: partial checks from late September and 26 full checks in the final month. This pack is the full record of what happened and what worked.

It is the third thing a member reads, not the first:

1. **The Microsite Playbook modules** teach how to build a microsite.
2. **The Microsite Empire module** provides the Microsite Blueprint, the build manual.
3. **This pack** is the evidence: what the contest proved, measured on real sites.

This pack does not repeat the Playbook or the Blueprint and does not replace them. Where it shows a result that goes further than the Blueprint, it says so plainly in section 8. If a question is about how to build something step by step, the answer is in the Blueprint. If the question is "does this actually matter, and what did the sites that won do", the answer is here.

## 2. How to read the claims

Every fact has a number (F-01 and up) and a source tag:

| Tag | Meaning | How firm |
|---|---|---|
| MEASURED | From our own ranking checks | Firm. State it as fact. |
| SITE | Counted from saved copies of the sites | Firm. State it as fact. |
| WINNER | From the winner's own written account and his working sheet | Firm as a record of what he did. It is his word. |
| CHECKED | We opened the page ourselves on the date given | Firm for that date. |
| READING | Our interpretation of the facts | Say it is our reading. |

The rules in section 6 (R-01 and up) are instructions built on those facts. Be firm about them. They are what worked in this contest.

## 4. The facts

### A. How rankings were measured

- **F-01 (MEASURED)** Every check was a Google search in a normal Chrome window with the location pinned to Gettysburg or York and personalization turned off. Only the regular website results were counted. Ads, the map box, the AI answer and the forum box were skipped. Mold was recorded to position 30 and radon to position 40. Each site was counted once, at its best page.
- **F-02 (MEASURED)** There are 26 full checks, from October 19 to November 17. Earlier checks from September 23 to October 18 exist for contest sites only, and the ones before September 30 were not location pinned.
- **F-03 (MEASURED)** A search that is not pinned to the town flatters new sites. On September 30 the pinned check gave contest sites 10 points on mold, from 2 sites. An unpinned check the same day gave 55 points, from 9 sites. Unpinned checks showed contest sites 10 to 15 positions higher than a local searcher saw them.

### B. The market decided more than the build

- **F-04 (MEASURED)** Across the 26 full checks, entered mold sites scored points on all 26. Entered radon sites scored on 2.
- **F-05 (MEASURED)** Twelve entered sites scored at least once in those 26 checks: 11 mold sites and 1 radon site. One site reached position 1.
- **F-06 (MEASURED)** On the final check, 8 entered mold sites were in the top 30 (positions 1, 17, 19, 21, 23, 25, 26, 28). Five entered radon sites were in the top 40 (positions 30, 31, 32, 34, 38). None was in the radon top 20.
- **F-07 (MEASURED)** The winner used the same method on both searches. His mold site finished at 1. His radon site finished at 34.
- **F-08 (MEASURED)** Who holds page one explains the gap. On the final check, page one for radon was a 26 year old local radon company (the radon number one itself, see F-09), Yelp, a radon only directory, the BBB, the state's own radon page, two press releases, a video and another established radon company. Page one for mold was the winner, two franchise location pages, Yelp, Angi, one small directory, a Facebook page for a contest brand at 5, and two listings contestants built on MapQuest and Patch.

### C. Google ranked the business, not the website

- **F-09 (MEASURED)** The radon number one sat on a domain registered on September 17, two days after the contest began. It first appeared in our results at 7 on October 24, reached 2 on October 31 and 1 on November 4, which was day 48. It was number one on 13 of the last 14 checks.
- **F-10 (SITE, CHECKED in October)** The business behind it has operated since December 1999, so it was 26 years old during the contest. It holds a state radon certification, has a real street address and phone, and its Google profile showed about 225 reviews.
- **F-11 (MEASURED)** That company's older listings and press were already ranking before its new site appeared. Its MapQuest listing was in the results on 25 of 26 checks, as high as 3. Two press releases about it sat as high as 7 and 8 from the first full check on October 19.
- **F-12 (MEASURED)** Blair W. built that site. His own entered radon site, a new brand with no history, never appeared in the top 40. Same builder, same weeks, same search.
- **F-60 (SITE)** The radon number one site was small when copied on October 24: 24 pages and about 30,000 words. A home page of about 2,400 words, 6 service pages, 6 blog posts, no town pages, and 9 profiles named in its code.
- **F-13 (MEASURED)** On October 29 the radon number one was the first source cited in Google's AI answer for the search.

### D. What the winning site is made of

- **F-14 (SITE)** The winner's site at the end of the contest: 119 pages and about 220,000 words. One home page, 9 service pages, 36 town pages, 58 blog posts, 4 guide pages (cost, process, state law, FAQ), plus overview, trust and legal pages.
- **F-15 (SITE)** The home page is the page that ranked. It runs to about 6,800 words under 18 main headings, answers 9 questions, has 3 tables and 19 images, and links to 8 outside sources including the borough and county websites and the EPA.
- **F-16 (SITE)** Typical length by page type: service pages about 2,000 words, town pages about 1,700, blog posts about 1,860, guides about 2,650.
- **F-17 (SITE)** His town pages share about 29 percent of their text with each other. His blog posts share about 5 percent. Templated town pages on member sites we audited usually share 50 to 70 percent.
- **F-18 (SITE)** The home page code describes the business in one block: name, phone, street address, map position, hours, towns served, a list of services and links to 16 profiles. FAQ markup is on 108 of the 119 pages.
- **F-19 (MEASURED, SITE)** The site reached number one on October 26 with 47 pages and a home page of about 4,600 words. It did not need 119 pages to get there. The home page grew to about 6,800 words in the October 27 update.
- **F-20 (MEASURED)** Only the home page ranked for the contest search at number one. No blog post and no town page appeared in the top 30 on any of the 26 checks. The services overview page and the contact page showed up between 8 and 22 on some checks.
- **F-21 (WINNER)** He bought the domain on September 15, did his research in about two hours, built the site with Claude Code and had it live within four hours. Town pages went live on day two.
- **F-22 (WINNER)** He did not publish first drafts. For every page he read the headings of the pages already ranking, wrote his own outline, pulled out the terms and phrases those pages share, and only then had Claude write the final copy.

### E. What separated sites that scored from sites that did not

- **F-23 (SITE)** We compared 10 mold sites that scored with 10 live mold sites that never did. Eleven mold sites scored and twelve live ones did not, but one scorer and two non scorers could not be copied, so they are left out. These differed: typical home page length (3,758 words against 2,083), profiles named in the site code (5 of 10 sites against 1 of 10), a street address shown (6 of 10 against 1 of 10), FAQ markup on most pages (7 of 10 against 2 of 10), and having articles or guides at all (4 of 10 against 1 of 10).
- **F-24 (SITE)** These did not differ much: typical total pages (33 against 29), typical number of town pages (8 against 7), and a phone number on the home page (8 of 10 against 6 of 10).
- **F-25 (SITE)** Of the 49 entries that never scored a point: 22 were live radon sites, 13 had no working site at all, 12 were live mold sites and 2 were entered late.
- **F-26 (MEASURED)** Second place had 18 pages. It was rebuilt once on October 15 and never edited again. It scored on all 26 checks, sat between 13 and 17 for a month, and never reached page one. It listed no profiles.

### F. Listings and profiles

- **F-27 (WINNER)** He built 20 to 25 main local listings in the first days, sent each one to an indexing tool the day it was made, and added 5 to 10 new listings every week for the whole contest. His sheet logs 63 profiles and listings. His write up puts the total at 70.
- **F-28 (MEASURED)** Three of his listings ranked for the contest search themselves: MapQuest as high as 6, Facebook as high as 9 and Manta as high as 19. On the final check his MapQuest listing was at 8, on page one beside his site.
- **F-29 (MEASURED)** On the final mold check, three pages that contestants built on other platforms were on page one (a Facebook page at 5, a MapQuest listing at 8 and a Patch listing at 9). Only one contest website was.
- **F-30 (MEASURED)** Listing and profile platforms that had pages in the results during the 26 checks, whoever built them. Mold: Yelp, Facebook, Clutch, Patch, MapQuest, BBB, YouTube, Birdeye, Manta. Radon: Facebook, Yelp, BBB, MapQuest, Houzz, Patch, YouTube, Reddit, Pinterest, Yellow Pages, Crunchbase.
- **F-31 (MEASURED)** Free publishing sites never ranked. Medium, Tumblr, Issuu, Substack, Weebly, WordPress.com, Linktree, Blogspot and Google Sites did not appear once in 26 checks for either search.
- **F-32 (CHECKED, November 25)** Of his 63 listings, a script could read 35. All 35 linked to his site: 23 with a normal link and 12 marked nofollow.

### G. Time, jumps and patience

- **F-33 (MEASURED)** The winner's path: 13 to 22 on the early checks in late September, 10 on October 4 and 5, then back to 17 to 19 from October 15 to 25. Number one on October 26, which was day 41. Then 13, 15 and 12 over the next five days. Number one again on November 1, five days after the update of October 27, and on all 17 checks from then to the end.
- **F-34 (WINNER, SITE)** He says that for the last 25 days he changed nothing on the site and all of his work was off the site: listings, links and social posts. Our copies put the last change on October 27, which is 21 days before the final check. Either way, the site was untouched for the whole of its run at number one.
- **F-35 (MEASURED)** A jump is not a ranking. Eleven mold sites scored at some stage, but only two scored on more than 6 of the 26 checks. One radon site went from 29 to 7 in a single day and was out of the top 40 two days after its last page one appearance.
- **F-36 (MEASURED)** If every one of the 26 checks had counted, the totals would have been 937 points for the winner's mold site, 166 for second place, 33 for the one radon site that scored and 16 for third place.

### H. The blog

- **F-37 (SITE)** The winner had 6 blog posts by October 7, published on September 20, 23, 27 and October 1, 3 and 7. All six named Gettysburg or Pennsylvania in the title. The site reached number one with only those six. One of the six, the cost article, was later moved out of the blog and became a guide page, which is why the blog ends with 58 posts and not 59.
- **F-38 (WINNER, SITE)** On October 27, 53 more posts went live in one day. He calls this "a big mistake". They were meant to be scheduled. Some were indexed the next day, so he left them alone. Our copies show the same update also added 17 town pages and 3 guide pages and lengthened the home page. His account mentions only the blog posts.
- **F-39 (MEASURED)** The site's best position was 13, 15 and 12 over the five days after that batch, then number one again. A press release went out the same day and the site was one day into its first number one, so the batch cannot be proven to be the cause.
- **F-40 (MEASURED)** No blog post ever ranked for the contest search. On November 18 his posts were the first result for three narrower searches: "mold in historic gettysburg homes", "flood insurance and mold adams county pa" and "pennsylvania mold contractor licensing".
- **F-41 (SITE)** A typical post: about 1,860 words, 11 main headings, a short FAQ at the end, one image, links to about 8 other posts and 2 service pages. 54 of the 58 cite the EPA. No two posts cover the same question.
- **F-42 (MEASURED)** When a blog post and a service page covered the same phrase, Google showed the service page and ignored the post.

### I. Links, press and social

- **F-43 (WINNER)** He logged 82 social posts on four accounts (25 on X, 24 on TikTok, 24 on Pinterest, 9 on Facebook), on 29 different days from September 17 to November 10. Never on a weekend. Two to four posts a week on each account at first, then fewer.
- **F-44 (WINNER)** For the first two and a half weeks each post was named after one of his service pages and went out with the same title on X, TikTok and Pinterest. From October 8 each post opened with a problem a homeowner notices.
- **F-45 (WINNER)** He got 4 guest posts placed on other sites, each linking to the home page. No two used the same words for the link. Each was a different way of saying the main keyword. He does not say what they cost. Our reading is that they were paid placements: two sat in the partner content sections of news sites.
- **F-46 (CHECKED, November 25)** Two of the four guest posts returned "page not found" one week after the contest. Both were on local news sites for other regions.
- **F-47 (WINNER, CHECKED)** One press release went out on October 27 through a press release distribution service. It reads like local news: a dated local weather event with figures, a six point checklist, an EPA citation, a link to the home page and one to the FAQ page, and a name, phone and address block.
- **F-48 (MEASURED)** An earlier press release in his business name ranked at 12 and 13 for the contest search on October 24 and 25. His write up does not mention it. His home page reached number one on October 26.
- **F-49 (WINNER, CHECKED)** He built 6 cloud pages, one each on Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare Workers, Firebase, GitHub Pages and Streamlit. The five we could read are homeowner guides of 1,100 to 2,200 words. Each has one link to a service page, not the home page, plus a link to the EPA and links to nine of his profiles. The sixth was asleep on November 25.
- **F-50 (MEASURED)** Press releases ranked for both searches, and they are unstable. A contestant's release was at 6 on the final radon check. Another release went from published to position 8 in two days and was gone on the third.

### J. Problems the winner hit

- **F-51 (WINNER)** He waited 3 to 4 days after launch, then checked what was indexed. In days 4 to 7 the wrong page was ranking. His business name contains his main keyword, so the About page and other pages ranked for it instead of the home page. He removed the brand keyword from every other page, kept it only on the home page and asked Google to reindex. The home page then ranked.
- **F-52 (WINNER)** He redirected 1 or 2 thin town pages to the home page in the first week. He did not pad them.
- **F-53 (WINNER)** Other new exact match sites began to appear in the mold results within 3 to 4 days of his own launch. He studied them in week one, in week two and after week three, and made small content updates each time. After that the only change to the site was the October 27 update (F-38).

### K. What did not matter, or did not separate anyone

- **F-54 (SITE)** An exact match domain. Almost every entry had one. It did not separate scorers from non scorers.
- **F-55 (MEASURED)** The domain extension. The three sites that finished in the points were all .com, but a .net and a .org each scored on some checks.
- **F-56 (SITE)** Page count and town page count. See F-24. A 116 page site was the best placed contest site on the mold search for one day in mid October, then fell out of the points. It never reached position 1.
- **F-57 (SITE)** A phone number. Second place had no phone number on the site and held page two for a month. Its home page was also short, at about 890 words, so R-03 describes the typical scoring site and the winner, not every scorer.

### L. Money

- **F-58 (WINNER)** Calls from the winning site are sold through a pay per call service. By November 25 he had received over 30 calls, many of them spam or robocalls, and leads worth $214 had converted.

### M. Claims on the winning site

- **F-59 (SITE)** The winning site shows no reviews, ratings or testimonials. It does make trust claims in its header and footer: fully insured, a one year written warranty, and work to the IICRC S520 standard. A lead generation site has to be able to back claims like these.

## 6. The rules: what to do

State these firmly. Each one names the facts behind it.

- **R-01. Check the market before you buy the domain.** Search the term with the location pinned to the town and read page one. If it is real local companies with years of reviews, pick another service or another town. Our reading: three or more such companies on page one, or a government page on the subject, is a walk away. You want directories, out of town franchises and thin sites. (F-04, F-07, F-08)
- **R-02. Measure with a pinned, non personalized search. Never trust a search from your own desk.** (F-01, F-03)
- **R-03. Make the home page the page that ranks, and make it long.** Scoring sites had a typical home page of about 3,750 words. The winner's had about 4,600 when it first reached number one and has about 6,800 now. (F-15, F-19, F-20, F-23)
- **R-04. Only the home page targets the main keyword.** If your brand name contains the keyword, use a shorter form of the name in the titles and headings of every other page. Check which page is ranking in the first week. (F-51)
- **R-05. Research before writing.** Read what ranks, outline, pull the shared terms, then have the AI write from your notes. (F-22)
- **R-06. Write every town page separately.** If the town name can be swapped and the page still reads fine, redirect it. (F-17, F-52)
- **R-07. Describe the business in the site code and mark up FAQs on every page type.** (F-18, F-23)
- **R-08. Build 20 to 25 listings in the first days, then 5 to 10 every week.** List them in the site code. (F-27, F-28, F-29, F-23) Use the same name and phone on every one: that is the Blueprint's rule and the contest did not test it.
- **R-09. Spend listing time on platforms that rank, not on free publishing sites.** (F-30, F-31)
- **R-10. Finish the site, launch it, then stop editing it.** Put the weekly work into listings, posts and links instead. The winner's one late update was an accident, and the site was untouched for all 17 checks of its run at number one. (F-26, F-33, F-34, F-38)
- **R-11. When the site jumps or drops, do nothing for a week.** Judge a jump or a drop across two or three weeks. Judge the whole site after six to eight weeks. (F-33, F-35)
- **R-12. Start a blog with five or six local posts. Schedule anything more.** Never publish a batch in one day. If it happens by accident, leave it. (F-37, F-38, F-39)
- **R-13. Never write a blog post on the same phrase as a service page.** (F-42)
- **R-14. Post a small, steady amount on social accounts on weekdays, starting in week one.** (F-43, F-44)
- **R-15. If you can build for a real business with reviews and history, do that.** It beat every new brand on the harder search with a 24 page site. (F-09, F-10, F-12, F-60)

### Reported, not recommended

The winner also used guest posts placed on other sites, cloud pages built to hold a link, a distributed press release and an indexing tool (F-45 to F-49, F-27). These were part of a site that reached number one in six weeks and finished there. Google's spam policies call paid links and pages built only to hold a link "link schemes". Half of his guest posts were gone a week after the contest (F-46). Report these as what he did. Do not present them as Academy advice, and say they are the least durable part of his plan.

## 7. Limits of the evidence

Say these when they matter. Do not hedge every answer with them.

- One contest, two searches, two towns in Pennsylvania, about nine weeks.
- One entered site reached number one. A single winner shows what is possible, not what is guaranteed.
- We can see what ranked and what each site was made of. We cannot prove which single thing caused a ranking.
- The scorer comparison (F-23) is 10 sites against 10.
- The contest ended inside the Blueprint's own "let it cook" window of 3 to 6 months.
- We measured rankings, not leads. The only income figure is the winner's own (F-58).

## 8. Where the contest goes further than the Blueprint

The Blueprint is the build manual and it stands. The contest did not contradict it. In five places the winning site did more than the Blueprint asks, and the evidence supports doing more:

| Topic | Winner and contest evidence | Facts |
|---|---|---|
| Home page length | About 4,600 words on the winner at his first number one, about 6,800 at the end. About 3,750 typical for scoring sites. During the contest the Blueprint asked for 1,200 to 2,000. | F-15, F-19, F-23 |
| Town pages | 36, each written separately, sharing about 29 percent of their text. During the contest the Blueprint described 20 to 30 town pages built from a template with one real detail added to each. | F-17 |
| Listings | 20 to 25 in the first days, then weekly, and listed in the site code. | F-27, F-23 |
| FAQ markup | On almost every page, not only the home page. | F-18, F-23 |
| Blog | A large blog was not needed to reach number one: he had six posts. The large blog won narrow searches afterwards. | F-19, F-37, F-40 |

Three things the contest confirmed strongly: the market filter (R-01), leaving a finished site alone (R-10), and not inventing reviews. The winning site shows no reviews, ratings or testimonials at all (F-59).

On street addresses: the winner shows one and so did 6 of 10 scoring sites (F-23). We do not know what is at the winner's address. The Blueprint rule stands: do not show an address you do not really have.
