**TLDR:** Fifty days into the contest, five things have shown up in the rankings over and over that most SEOs never think about. None of them are about keywords or word counts.

**Five things the contest taught us that most SEOs don't think about**

Full rules, queries and the point table: https://www.skool.com/rank-expand-academy/microsite-1000-seo-contest-official-rules

**1. Your profiles rank before your site does, and they tell you when it's coming.**
Facebook pages, Patch listings, BBB profiles, Clutch, MapQuest and press release pages hit page one weeks before the sites they belong to. The never entered radon company's press release sat at 7 for a week before its site showed up at all. If your profiles are climbing and your site isn't, you're early, not failing. If your profiles aren't ranking either, Google hasn't found the business yet, and no page edit fixes that.

**2. You cannot measure your own rankings from your desk.**
A search from your own laptop, logged in, in your own town, shows your site 10 to 15 spots higher than a Gettysburg searcher sees it. Half the contest thought they were on page two when they were on page four. The only reading that counts is a search pinned to the target town with personalization off, and until you do that you're optimizing a number that doesn't exist.

**3. A visit is not a ranking.**
Every site that jumped into the top 15 in this contest left within a week, except the ones with an entity behind them. Google puts a new site in, watches what happens, and takes it out. The mistake everyone made was reacting to the pop: editing, adding pages, celebrating. The site at number one today sat through its test window without touching anything.

**4. Ship in batches, then leave it alone.**
Nobody who added pages one at a time moved a single spot. Every move into the top 15 came within 48 hours of a large republish. The leader shipped 73 pages in one day, dropped from 1 to 13, and was back at number one six days later with more of the site ranking than before. One big batch, one reset, one recovery. Trickling gives Google nothing to re read.

**5. Domain age doesn't matter. Business age does.**
A six week old domain for a 26 year old York company went to page one five weeks after registration and has held position 2 for a week. Six week old domains for six week old businesses are at 30. Google isn't ranking the website. It's ranking the business the website describes, and it decides whether that business is real by finding it in ten other places that agree on the name, the phone and the town. That's the whole game, and almost nobody in the contest did it.

**Three bonus things every site at the top is doing**

These aren't surprises. They're the base the five lessons sit on, and every site that has scored in this contest has all three.

**1. One service, and the homepage is the page that ranks.**
The number one mold site, the hyphen .com at 14 and the never entered radon company all rank on their homepage. Inner pages flicker in and out, the homepage holds. Every one of them is a single service site where every page links back to the home and service pages. Nobody with two services on one domain has scored.

**2. Something on the site that could only be about this town.**
The hyphen .com's township pages describe the actual housing stock in Cumberland Township and Hanover. The mold leader has a post on historic Gettysburg homes and one on Adams County flood insurance. The radon company has a post on York split levels and additions, and cites the Pennsylvania regulation by name. Every site at the top has pages a competitor from another state could not have written. Every site in the block at 25 to 30 could be moved to any town by changing the name.

**3. They stopped touching it.**
The hyphen .com hasn't changed since Oct 15 and has held 14 or 15 for ten crawls. The leader hasn't changed since Oct 27 and has been number one for three days. The radon company went 18 days between its two main updates and climbed the whole time. Build it right, build the profiles, then give Google a month of silence to read it. The sites that edited every few days are the ones still on page three.

**How we know**

Every number above comes from the same measurement, run the same way since Sept 30: a Chrome search pinned to Gettysburg or York, personalization off, organic results only, footer checked for the town name before anything is counted. Fourteen days left.
