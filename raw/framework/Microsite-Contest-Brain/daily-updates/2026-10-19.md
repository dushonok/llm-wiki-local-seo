**Day 35: Seventeen Pages on a Wednesday**

Today's crawl, pinned to Gettysburg and York. Two days since the last one.

First, I owe the board a correction. On Day 33 I called the new mold leader a one pager with 110 words on it. That was true for a month. It stopped being true on Oct 15, and I wrote the post without looking again. Here's what I missed, because it's the best story this contest has produced.

**The site that gave up, then didn't**

For the first four weeks, this site was one page. One hundred and ten words, no phone, no address, basic schema. It sat in the 40s for three weeks. Then it fell out of the top 50 entirely. By every measure we had, it was done. I'd been calling it the control group.

On Wednesday Oct 15, it shipped 17 new pages. By Friday it was at 12. Today it's 14, seven points, leading mold.

Forty eight hours from rebuild to page 2. In a field where the other 40 sites have been grinding on page 3 for a month.

**What went in**

Not volume. Eighteen pages total is mid pack for this contest. What went in is specificity.

Seven township pages, and each one is about that township. Fairfield talks about spring lines and well houses. Carroll Valley talks about seasonal homes that sit closed all winter. New Oxford talks about main street doubles. The Gettysburg page has a section on the Historic District rulebook and another on the August student rental turnover. A cost page with Adams County ranges broken out by room and by square footage. Separate pages for basement mold and attic mold, because fieldstone foundations and vented bath fans are different problems. Every page has its own FAQ with markup and breadcrumbs.

Read any page and you'd believe the writer has stood in a Gettysburg basement.

**What didn't go in**

A phone number. There still isn't one anywhere on the site. No address past the town name. No profiles, no sameAs, no outbound links. The one photo per page has alt text that says it's AI generated. And the site serves four live copies of every page because http and www never redirect.

So the mold leader has the most specific content in the contest and the least off site presence of any site in the top 20. Zero.

**The site it passed**

The Day 31 leader is the opposite build. 116 pages. Neighborhood pages. Prices on the homepage. 27 profiles pointing at it. It led mold on Wednesday at 14. Today it's not in the top 30.

I've said for a month that off site is what separates scorers from non scorers, and the block at 21 through 30 still looks exactly like that: nine contest domains with no entity behind them, stacked in a row. But the top of the board just got taken by a site with no entity at all, on the strength of pages that read like they were written about this town instead of generated for it. That's a different lesson than the one I've been teaching, and I'm not going to pretend it isn't.

**Radon**

The stacker's radon site, the only radon scorer for a week, is out of the top 40 for the second crawl running. Its Houzz, Pinterest and Facebook pages all held within two spots of where they were. The press releases profiling its brand hold four of the top 30. Everything on someone else's domain stayed. The one thing on the contestant's own domain left.

One new radon contest site at 23. First one in a week to get within striking distance of a point.

**Thirty days left**

Here's the question that matters now. The rebuild got to page 2 in two days. The Day 31 leader got there in a week and was gone in five days. Every site that has touched the top 15 in this contest has fallen back within a week. If the rebuild is still at 14 next Sunday, the township pages did it. If it's back in the 40s, it was another test window and we learned something else.

Radon is zero. Mold is seven points held by a site with no phone number. Thirty days.
