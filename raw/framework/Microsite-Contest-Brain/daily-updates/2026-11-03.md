**The site that was never entered: where it is and where it's going**

This is the most useful case in the contest, so here is its full record. A York radon company that has been installing systems since 1999, with a Google Business Profile carrying about 225 reviews and a BBB record going back decades, got a brand new domain on Sept 17, two days after the contest opened. It was never submitted, so it can't score, and under the two domain rule it couldn't have been. But it was built like a contest site, on contest timing, and it's the only radon site in the market that behaves the way we all hoped ours would.

- Oct 19, the first pinned crawl we have: the site is nowhere in the top 40. Its press release is already on page one at 7. Its MapQuest listing is at 17 and its BBB profile at 31.
- Oct 22: still not in the top 40. The MapQuest listing spikes to 3 and the press release holds at 8.
- Oct 24: the site appears for the first time, at 7. It takes the exact spot the contest guide site had held for the two crawls before.
- Oct 25 through Oct 27: 5, then 6, then 5.
- Oct 29: 4. First time it's cited in the AI Overview, and it's cited first.
- Oct 31: 2.
- Nov 1, Nov 2, Nov 3: 2, 2, 2. Today its services page also shows at 30.

Read that in order. The profiles ranked first, for a full week, before the site existed in the results. A press release on a regional news domain sat on page one from the first crawl we have. MapQuest, BBB, two more syndicated press pages all held. Then the site appeared at 7, and it never went backward. Seven, five, six, five, four, two, and now four crawls at 2. Every contest site that reached the top 15 was gone within a week. This one has moved in one direction for eleven days.

The only thing above it is a multi city radon chain that has owned position 1 since the first pinned crawl. Position 2 is the ceiling for a single location company unless Google decides the chain's York page is weaker than a York company's homepage. That's the thing to watch for the next 15 days. If it takes 1, a six week old domain will have beaten an established regional site, and the lesson is that domain age means nothing when the business behind the domain is old.

What it didn't do is just as important. It didn't rebuild. It didn't trickle pages. It got a press release, listings on MapQuest, BBB, Yelp and Houzz, and a site that matched the profile Google already trusted. Then it waited five weeks for Google to connect the new domain to the old business. The day that happened it went to page one, and it has climbed every crawl since.

After Nov 18 this is the site to keep checking, because it answers the question every one of us will face with a real client: how fast does a new microsite rank when it's attached to a business Google already knows? The answer so far is five weeks to page one, two weeks more to position 2, with no edits in between.

**How these rankings are measured**

The process hasn't changed since Sept 30. Chrome, English, US, personalization switched off in the URL, and a location code that puts the searcher in Gettysburg or York. Three result pages on mold, four on radon, ten seconds between loads, seven loads total. Before anything is written down I check the footer for the town name and "Results are not personalized." Only organic blue links count. Ads, the map pack, the AI Overview and the forum block are ignored. One position per domain, its best URL, and positions 1 through 20 score. "Number one" in this post means the first organic website on page one for the exact contest query from a Gettysburg pin. Rank tools and the built in browser preview show new sites ten to fifteen spots higher than this, so I don't use them.

**What to take away**
