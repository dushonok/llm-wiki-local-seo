# The winner's own account

Source: after the contest, Zeeshan Y. wrote up his launch process and shared the working sheet he used to log every listing, social post and link. He told the Academy that all of it could be used. This file sets out his account in the order he gave it. It is his record of what he did. Where a line starts "We saw" or "We checked", that part is the Academy's own measurement.

Background he gave: he has been building rank and rent sites for two years. This was not his first.

## Day 1: September 15

### Research (about two hours)

1. Bought the domain.
2. Searched the main keyword and looked at which sites were ranking.
3. Did keyword research and studied the competitors. Pulled out every secondary keyword.
4. Found the inner service topics. These became separate service pages.
5. Listed the neighborhoods and local areas to target. These became the neighborhood pages.

### Build

1. Created a GitHub repository for the site.
2. Connected Claude Code to VS Code.
3. Gave Claude Code the main keyword and had it build every page: the home page, all the service pages and all the trust pages such as About and Contact. He calls these E-E-A-T pages.

### Content

1. Studied competitor headings for each keyword, then wrote his own outlines.
2. Read competitor content in detail and pulled out entities, n-grams, skip-grams and semantic keywords. He credits "Korey Frameworks" for this step. Our reading is that this means the semantic SEO method taught by Koray Tugberk Gubur.
3. Wrote content intended to be better than the pages already ranking, using what he found.
4. Did the same for every service page.
5. Had Claude write the final content for all pages, then added it to the site.
6. Created and added the trust pages.

### Going live

Within four hours of starting, he connected the domain to Cloudflare and put the site live on Vercel.

## Day 2

1. Built the neighborhood pages from the day one list. He describes this as vibe coding.
2. Had Claude write semantic content for each neighborhood page, then published them.

## Off the site

### 1. Citations and profiles

- Built 20 to 25 main local citations at the start.
- Indexed them straight away with a tool called Rapid Indexer.
- Added new citations every week. The weekly target was 5 to 10.
- Total by the time he wrote: 70 citations and profiles. His sheet lists 64 addresses. We checked: one of them, a Birdeye page, belongs to a different contest entry, which leaves 63.

### 2. Social media

- Indexed the social accounts and main profiles with the same tool.
- Kept Facebook, X, Pinterest and TikTok active.
- Posted every week. His sheet logs 82 posts.

### 3. Guest posts

- Got guest posts that link back to the website. His sheet lists four.

### 4. Press release

- Published one press release on October 27, 2026, with a Gettysburg dateline.
- We saw: an earlier press release in his business name ranked at 12 and 13 for the contest search on October 24 and 25. He does not mention it.

### 5. Backlinks and cloud stacking

- Kept building backlinks.
- Did cloud stacking "to add more authority signals". His sheet lists six cloud pages.

## Days 4 to 7: indexing and first fixes

1. Waited 3 to 4 days, then checked which pages were indexed.
2. Looked at how every page was doing.
3. Found a problem. The main keyword was also the brand name. Because of this, the About page and other content pages started ranking for the main keyword instead of the home page.
4. Fixed it. Removed the brand keyword from all other pages and kept it only on the home page. Asked Google to index the site again. After that, the home page started ranking.
5. Some neighborhood pages were too thin, so he redirected 1 or 2 of them to the home page.

## Week 2: competitors and blog

1. New competitors appeared. After 3 to 4 days, other exact match domain sites began to show up. He studied their content.
2. Made small content updates based on what they were doing.
3. Started a blog for authority. Claude wrote posts on problem based keywords and he published them.

## "A big mistake"

His words for the heading. Claude wrote more than 50 blog posts. They were not scheduled, and they all went live at the same time. The next day some of them were being indexed anyway, so he left them as they were and did nothing more.

We saw: the site went from 47 pages to 119 on October 27. Besides 53 blog posts, that update added 17 town pages and 3 guide pages and lengthened the home page from about 4,600 words to about 6,800. His account mentions only the blog posts. The site had reached number one the day before. Its best position was 13, 15 and 12 over the next five days. It was number one again on November 1 and stayed there.

## After three weeks: freshness updates

1. Studied competitors again.
2. Added fresh updates to some pages so that Google would come back and crawl the site again.

We saw: the site's own sitemap shows pages updated on September 27, October 3, October 7 and October 16.

## The last 25 days: off-page work only

For the last 25 days he did not change anything on the website. All work was off the site: backlinks, cloud stacking and other off-page work.

We saw: he was number one on all 17 checks from November 1 to November 17. Our copies put the last change to the site on October 27, which is 21 days before the final check, not 25.

## Money

He sells the calls through a pay per call service called Lead Smart. He had received over 30 calls. Many were spam or robocalls. Leads worth $214 had converted by the time he wrote.
