**The Pre-Launch Checklist**

This last page is not about the contest sites. It is about yours.

We audited more than 30 member sites this fall. The same problems came up again and again, and most of them are invisible until someone looks at the code. A site can look finished and still be blocked from Google, or have a form that sends nowhere.

The attached PDF turns those audits into 30 tick box checks in seven groups. Go through it before you launch, and again any time you wonder why a site is not ranking. Here is what each group covers.

**1. Can Google get in?**

Make sure nothing is telling Google to stay out. One member site had a "noindex" tag on every page and its owner could not work out why nothing was indexed. Check the robots file and the sitemap too, and make sure a missing page shows a real "not found" page.

**2. Does every address lead to one place?**

Your site has four addresses: with and without www, and with and without https. All four should end up on one version. This was the most common technical miss we found. On one site the www address did not load at all.

**3. Does each page stand on its own?**

This group is about depth. The home page needs about 1,200 words. Service pages need 800 to 1,200, and each one should cover a different job. Town pages need about 800 words and should read like they could only be about that town.

Two mistakes showed up on site after site. The first is dozens of thin town pages where eight to ten strong ones would do better. The second is a home page and a main town page that share the same main heading, so they compete with each other.

**4. Is it honest?**

AI builders invent things. We found made up owners with made up hobbies, and draft notes left on live pages that said the profiles should be replaced before publishing.

Search your own site for the words "placeholder," "proposed" and "illustrative." If you pass leads to a contractor, say so in the main copy. Only promise free estimates or 24/7 service if the contractor has agreed to it.

**5. Will a lead reach you?**

Send a test through every form and make sure it arrives. One site had forms that looked fine and sent nowhere. Call your own phone number. One site showed three different numbers depending on how the page was read.

**6. Does the code describe the business?**

Tell Google what the business is with LocalBusiness schema that includes the name, the phone number and the area served. Give every image a description of what it shows, and give every page its own title.

**7. After launch**

Build your profiles in the first week with the exact name and number from the site. Check your position the right way, from the town. Then stop editing and give Google a few weeks.

The contest winner did not touch his site for the last three weeks, and he spent all of them at number one.

**How to use it**

Print the PDF and tick the boxes, or hand it to your AI assistant along with your site address and ask it to check each item. Fix anything that fails in groups one, two and five first. Those are the ones that stop a site from working at all.
