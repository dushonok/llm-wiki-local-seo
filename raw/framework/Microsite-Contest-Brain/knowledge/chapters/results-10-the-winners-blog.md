**The Winner's Blog**

The number one site has a 58 post blog. A lot of members have asked the same questions about it. Did the blog win the contest? When were the posts written? What are they about? Should I build one?

This page answers those from the site itself. The attached PDF has the full study, including a list of all 58 posts.

**The short answer**

The site reached number one with six blog posts. The other 53 were published the next day.

So the big blog did not win the contest search. But it did something else that matters, and we will get to that.

**When the posts were published**

The first post went up on September 20, five days after the domain was registered. Five more followed, about one every three or four days, with the last on October 7.

Then nothing new was added for almost three weeks.

On October 26 the site hit number one. On October 27, 53 new posts were published in a single day. The site dipped to 13, came back to number one five days later, and stayed there.

**The local posts came first**

All six of the early posts named Gettysburg or Pennsylvania in the title. They covered things like basement mold in old Gettysburg homes and what to do in the first 48 hours after water damage.

Across all 58 posts, 15 are about the town, the county or the state. The other 43 are general questions that would apply anywhere.

**What they are about**

Each post answers one question a homeowner would type into Google. They fall into five groups.

The biggest group, 22 posts, is mold in a specific place: under the kitchen sink, behind drywall, on window sills, in attic insulation after a roof leak.

Eleven posts cover testing and how the job works, such as how long remediation takes and whether you can stay home during it.

Ten cover leaks, damp and prevention. Nine cover money, law and hiring, such as insurance, selling a house with mold, and tenant rights in Pennsylvania. Six cover spotting mold and health.

**How each post is built**

The posts follow one recipe. A typical one has about 1,860 words under 11 main headings, with a short FAQ at the end and one image.

Each post links to about eight other posts and two service pages, so every article points readers back toward the pages that sell. Almost every post cites official sources. The EPA is cited in 54 of the 58.

No two posts cover the same question.

**Did the posts rank?**

For the contest search, no. Across 26 checks, no blog post ever appeared in the top 30 for "mold remediation gettysburg pa." The page that ranked was the home page.

For their own questions, yes. On November 18 we searched three topics the posts cover, with the location set to Gettysburg. The winner's post was the first result for "mold in historic gettysburg homes," for "flood insurance and mold adams county pa," and for "pennsylvania mold contractor licensing."

That is what the blog did. A site with only a home page can rank for one thing. This one now shows up first for questions its customers ask before they call anyone.

**One warning**

Do not write a blog post on the same phrase as one of your service pages. When we searched "attic mold gettysburg pa," Google showed the winner's attic service page at number one and ignored his blog post on the same subject.

**If you are planning a blog**

Start with five or six posts that are truly local. Give each post one question and put it in the title. Cite official sources. End with a short FAQ and links to related posts and to the service it supports.

If you add a large set later, schedule it over a few weeks. The winner has since told us his batch of 53 went live in one day by mistake. He left it alone, and the dip lasted five days.

**What we can and cannot say**

The posts did not hurt the site, and they may have helped it hold number one. We cannot prove that from one site. What we can show is that the site was already number one before the big blog existed.
