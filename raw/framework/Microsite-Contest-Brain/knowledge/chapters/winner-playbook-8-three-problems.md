**Three Problems and How He Fixed Them**

**TLDR:** Three things went wrong on the number one site in its first six weeks. Each time he checked early, made one small fix and left it alone. Part 8 of the playbook PDF turns each problem into a check you can run on your own site.

**1. The wrong page was ranking**

His business name is Gettysburg Mold Remediation Pros. His main keyword is mold remediation Gettysburg PA. The name contains the keyword.

So every page that used the name also repeated the keyword. When he checked on days four to seven, the About page and other pages were ranking for the main keyword instead of the home page.

He removed the brand keyword from every other page and kept it only on the home page. Then he asked Google to index the site again. After that, the home page started ranking.

You can see the fix on his site today. The services page is titled "All Mold Services | Gettysburg Mold Pros," with the name shortened. The main heading on the contact page is just "Contact Us."

If you have an exact match domain, you probably have this problem. Search your main keyword and see which of your pages shows up.

**2. Some neighborhood pages were too thin**

A few of the town pages built on day two did not have enough to say.

He did not pad them out. He redirected one or two of them to the home page in the first week.

**3. More than 50 blog posts went live at once**

He started the blog in week two with a handful of posts. Later, Claude wrote more than 50 more. They were meant to be scheduled. They all went live together on October 27.

He calls it "a big mistake." Some of the posts were indexed the next day, so he left them all as they were.

Our checks show the site had reached number one the day before. For the next five days its best position was 13, then 15, then 12. On November 1 it was back at number one and stayed there.

The timing fits. But a press release went out the same day, and the site was one day into its first number one, so we cannot say the batch caused the dip.

What we can say is this. Schedule your posts. Count the pages before you deploy. And if a batch goes live by accident, leave it. He did, and he recovered in five days.

**One thing he did on purpose**

He watched the competition. New exact match sites appeared within days of his launch. He studied them in the first week, in week two and again after week three, and made small updates each time.

Then he stopped. The last 25 days were all off-page work.

**The pattern in all three**

Check early. Make one small fix. Leave it alone. He never rebuilt the site, including when it sat outside the top ten for two weeks in October.
