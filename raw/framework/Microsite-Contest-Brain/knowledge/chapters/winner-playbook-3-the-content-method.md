**The Content Method**

**TLDR:** He did not ask Claude for a page and publish it. He studied the pages that were already ranking, took notes, and had Claude write from those notes. Part 3 of the playbook PDF has a worksheet and five prompts so you can do the same.

**The five steps**

**Study the headings.** For each keyword, open the pages that rank and read only their headings. Then write your own outline.

**Read the pages in detail.** Pull out the terms and phrases they share.

**Write to beat them.** Use what you found to write a page that covers more than the pages already ranking.

**Repeat for every service page.** The home page is not the only one that gets this treatment.

**Let Claude write the final copy.** From your outline and your notes, not from a one line request.

**The jargon, in plain English**

In step two he lists entities, n-grams, skip-grams and semantic keywords. That sounds harder than it is.

Entities are the real things a topic is made of. For mold in Gettysburg that means things like HEPA filters, containment, the EPA, crawl spaces and Adams County.

N-grams are short runs of words that keep showing up together, like "mold remediation cost."

Skip-grams are word pairs that keep appearing near each other with other words in between, like "moisture" and "basement."

Semantic keywords are the related words Google expects to find around a topic, even though nobody searches for them directly.

You do not need a special tool for any of this. Paste five competitor pages into Claude and ask for the four lists. The prompt is in the PDF.

**A note on "Korey Frameworks"**

He credits "Korey Frameworks" for this step. We read that as the semantic SEO method taught by Koray Tugberk Gubur. That is our reading, not his words.

**What it produced**

His home page runs to about 6,800 words, one of the two longest in the contest. It gives price ranges, names local housing and local rules, and the site has its own page on Pennsylvania mold laws.

In our study of sites that scored against sites that did not, home page length was one of the clearest gaps: a typical scoring site had about 3,750 words on its home page and a typical non scoring site about 2,100.
