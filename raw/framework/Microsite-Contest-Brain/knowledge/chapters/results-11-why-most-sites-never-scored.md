**Why Most Sites Never Scored**

Sixty one websites were entered. Twelve scored at least once in the final month. Forty nine did not.

It is easy to assume those 49 were just worse websites. Most of them were not. When we went through them one by one, most had missed for reasons that had little to do with how well they were built.

This page does not name any site or anyone who entered it. It is here because the misses teach as much as the win.

**Where the 49 went**

Twenty two were live sites built for radon. Only one entered radon site scored all month, and it lasted two checks. That search was harder, and a better build did not change it.

Thirteen had no working website. When we checked every entered domain on November 18, eight were not connected to any website, two showed a "coming soon" page, and three were parked, broken or offline. More than one entry in five was never in the race.

Two were entered after the deadline. One of them ranked well enough to score on five checks and earned nothing.

That leaves twelve. Twelve live, eligible mold sites that did not score. This is the only group where the build made the difference.

**The fair comparison**

Same search, same weeks, same rules. We compared ten mold sites that scored with ten live mold sites that never did.

The home page was the clearest gap. Sites that scored had a home page of about 3,760 words. Sites that missed had about 2,080. In this contest the home page was the page that ranked, so that is where depth counted.

Five of the ten scoring sites listed their profiles in the site's code. Only one of the ten that missed did.

Six of the ten scoring sites showed a street address. Only one of the ten that missed did.

Seven of the ten scoring sites had FAQs marked up on most of their pages. Only two of the ten that missed did.

Four of the ten scoring sites had articles or guides. Only one of the ten that missed did.

**What did not matter**

The number of pages did not separate them. The typical site had about 30 pages in both groups.

The number of town pages did not either. The three non scoring sites with the most town pages had 35, 28 and 16 of them, and none of the three ever reached the top 20.

So the sites that scored were not bigger. They were deeper on the home page, and they looked more like a real business.

**How close they came**

Missing the points did not mean being invisible. Seven of the twelve live mold sites that never scored showed up on page three at some point, with best positions between 22 and 30. Several sat there for most of the month. They were ranked. They were a few places short.

**Radon: good sites, wrong search**

The radon sites were not weak. One had 126 pages. Another listed 54 profiles in its code. The strongest of them matched the mold sites that scored on almost every measure.

It did not matter. Nine of the 22 reached the top 40 at some point and none reached the top 20. Page one was held by an established local company, the state's own radon page and a directory that covers only radon.

The contest winner entered a radon site too, built the same way as his mold site. It finished at 34.

**What to take from it**

Launch something. A plain site that is live beats a perfect one that is not.

Choose the search before you build, and look at who already holds page one.

Put your depth into the home page.

Make the business look real outside its own website, with profiles that match the site.

Do not count pages. And enter on time.

The two groups are small, about ten sites each. Read the comparison as a pattern from one contest, not as proof.
