**Five Lessons from the Contest**

If you only read one page in this module, make it this one. These are the five things the rankings showed again and again over the contest. None of them are about keywords or word counts.

Each lesson below has the short version. The attached PDF has the numbers behind each one and a "what to do" box you can act on.

**1. Your listings rank before your website does**

Facebook pages, MapQuest listings and Patch listings reached page one while the websites they belonged to were still on page three.

On the final check, two listings built by contestants were on page one for mold. Only one contest website was there with them. The winner built 20 to 25 profiles and listings in his first days and added more every week. The second place site listed none and never reached page one.

Build your profiles first. If they are climbing and your site is not, you are early, not failing.

**2. The market matters more than the build**

The same people built both sets of sites with the same tools. Across the last 26 checks, mold paid out points every single time. Radon paid out twice.

The winner's mold site finished at number one. His radon site finished at 34. The difference was who already held page one before anyone started.

Read page one before you buy the domain. If real local companies with years of reviews already fill it, pick another service or another town.

**3. Google ranks the business, not the website**

The number one radon result was a website on a domain registered two days after the contest began. It reached number one in about seven weeks.

The reason was the business behind it: a York company that has operated since 1999 and has about 225 Google reviews. Blair W. built that site. His own contest entry for the same search, a new brand with no history, never appeared in the top 40.

A new domain is not the problem. A business that Google cannot find anywhere else is.

**4. Build it deep, ship it in one go, then leave it alone**

The winning site reached number one with 47 pages. The next day it grew to 119 pages in a single update, which he has since told us was an accident. It dropped to 13 and came back to number one five days later. It then held the top spot for 17 checks without a single edit to the site.

Second place did the same thing on a smaller scale. One rebuild, no edits after it, and a month in the same place.

Finish the site before you launch. If you add more, schedule it a few pages at a time, and then stop touching it.

**5. A jump is a test, not a ranking**

Eleven mold sites scored points at some stage in the final month. Only two of them scored on more than six of the 26 checks.

One radon site jumped from 29 to 7 in a single day and was out of the top 40 two days after it left page one. A press release reached position 8 in two days and was gone on the third.

When your site jumps, do nothing. Judge it by where it sits across two or three weeks, not by its best day.

**How we know**

Every number here comes from the same kind of check: a search in a normal Chrome window with the location set to Gettysburg or York and personalization turned off, counting only the regular website results.
