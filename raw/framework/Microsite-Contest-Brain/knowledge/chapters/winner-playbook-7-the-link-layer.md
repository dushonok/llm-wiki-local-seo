**The Link Layer**

**TLDR:** He built four guest posts, one press release and six cloud pages. A week after the contest, two of the four guest posts were already gone. This page shows what he built and is honest about how long it lasts.

**Read this first**

The links came after the site, not instead of it. He built a 6,800 word home page, nine service pages and 36 area pages first.

Paid guest posts and pages built only to hold a link are what Google's spam policies call link schemes. They were part of a site that reached number one in six weeks and held it. We have no data on how long that lasts.

This page reports what he did. It is not a recommendation to copy every part.

**Guest posts**

Four articles on other sites, each with a link to his home page. No two use the same words for the link. Each is a different way of saying the main keyword, such as "Mold Remediation Gettysburg, PA" and "mold remediation company in Gettysburg, PA."

When we opened them on November 25, two were live with normal links. The other two returned "page not found." Those two were on local news sites for other regions, in their partner content sections.

**The press release**

One release went out on October 27 through a press release distribution service. It does not read like an advert. It reads like local news.

It opens with a real local hook, heavy rain in Adams County two weeks earlier, with rainfall figures and a source. It gives readers a six point checklist. It cites the EPA. It mentions the old fieldstone foundations and the review board for the historic district, which only a local would know. It links to the home page and the FAQ page, and it ends with the business name, phone and address.

Our checks also caught an earlier release in his business name, at positions 12 and 13 for the contest search on October 24 and 25. His write up mentions only the October 27 one. His home page reached number one on October 26. We cannot say one caused the other.

**Cloud pages**

He calls this cloud stacking. It means publishing small free pages on the big hosting platforms, each with a link back to your site. He built six, on Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare Workers, Firebase, GitHub Pages and Streamlit.

The five we could read are full homeowner guides of 1,100 to 2,200 words. Each answers one narrow problem. Each has one link to a service page, not the home page. Each cites the EPA. And each links to nine of his profiles, so the cloud pages hold up his listings as well as his site.

The sixth, on Streamlit, had gone to sleep when we looked.

**Where each kind of link points**

Guest posts and the press release point at the home page. Cloud pages point at service pages. Nothing in his sheet points at the blog.
