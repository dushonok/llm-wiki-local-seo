**How to Check Your Real Rankings**

Most people check their rankings by searching from their own laptop. That number is usually wrong.

Google changes its results based on where you are and what you have searched before. A search from your own desk can show your site well above where a customer in the town sees it. Early in the contest that gap was 10 to 15 spots. Some contestants thought they were on page two when a local searcher saw them on page four.

This page is the method we used for every contest check. It takes about two minutes.

**Step 1: Build a search link that pins the location**

Take a normal Google search address and add three settings to it: your town, personalization off, and the country. The attached PDF shows the exact link we used for Gettysburg, so you can copy its shape.

**Step 2: Open the link in Chrome**

Use a normal window. You do not need a VPN or a paid tool.

**Step 3: Scroll to the very bottom before you count anything**

This is the step people skip. The footer of the results page must show your town and the words "Results are not personalized."

If it shows another place, or says the results are based on your activity, throw that check away. It is not what a local customer sees.

**Step 4: Count only the regular website results**

Start at the top and count down. Skip the sponsored results, the map box with three businesses, the AI answer, the "People also ask" box and the forums box.

Do count directory pages, Facebook pages, YouTube videos and press releases when they appear as normal results. Each page holds about ten.

**Step 5: Check pages two and three**

Add &start=10 to the end of the link for page two, and &start=20 for page three. Wait about ten seconds between pages so Google does not block you.

**Step 6: Write down one position per site**

Use its best page. If your home page is at 14 and a service page is at 22, your position is 14.

**Step 7: Check once a day at most, and judge by weeks**

One good day is Google trying your site out. Two or three weeks in the same place is a ranking.

**Making the link for your own town**

The town is set by a short code in the link. The PDF explains how the code is built in three pieces.

The easy way is to give those three rules and your town to your AI assistant and ask it for the finished link. Then do step three. If the footer shows your town, the link is right.

**Track your listings too**

Keep a simple log with the date, the search, whether the footer showed your town, and the position of your site and your main listings. In the contest, listings usually moved before the site did, so they are your early signal.
