**The Weekly Off-Page Routine**

**TLDR:** For the last 25 days of the contest he changed nothing on his site. He was number one on every check in that stretch. What he did instead fits on one page, and Part 5 of the playbook PDF has a tracker so you can do it too.

**The routine**

**Add 5 to 10 new listings.** Work down the Citation List. Same name, phone and address every time.

**Send the new listing pages to an indexing tool.** He did this the day each one was made.

**Post on each active social account.** He kept X, TikTok, Pinterest and Facebook going. Two to four posts a week on each, Monday to Friday.

**Build links.** Guest posts, cloud pages and a press release. The Link Layer page covers these.

**Look at the competition.** He studied new competitors in the first week, in week two and again after week three.

**Leave the site alone unless something is broken.**

**What his own log shows**

His sheet has dates for every social post, so we counted them by week. In the first month each account got three or four posts a week. After that it dropped to two, and then one.

The site work is front loaded. Week one was the build, the town pages and one fix. Week two was a content refresh and the start of the blog. After week three there were some freshness updates. Then the accidental blog batch in week seven. Then nothing.

**Why this matters more than it looks**

Our audits keep finding the same thing. A site slides in week four or five, and the builder starts rewriting it. That usually makes it worse.

His routine gave him something useful to do while the site settled. The work went into listings, posts and links, and the site was left to rank.
