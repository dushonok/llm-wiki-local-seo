# The radon number one: digest

Site: absoluteradonmitigationllc.com. Search: "radon mitigation york pa". Built by Blair W. for a real radon company in York, Pennsylvania.

**Status in the contest:** never on the entry sheet, so it could not score. In the daily updates it was called "the mystery site" and "the company that was never entered". Blair W. received the honorable mention.

**What this file is:** a description and analysis. Because the site was not entered, this pack contains no copy of it: no saved pages, no page text and no screenshots. Do not reproduce its pages. Learn from how it is put together.

## Why it matters

It is the only site other than the winner to reach position 1, and it did it on the search where no entered site got inside the top 20. It is the clearest evidence in the contest for one idea: Google ranked the business, not the website.

## The business

- A radon mitigation company operating in York since December 1999.
- Holds a Pennsylvania state radon mitigation certification, and the site code states the certification number and links it to the state department that issues it.
- A real street address and a local phone number.
- A Google Business Profile with about 225 reviews. The site code links to it.
- A member listing with the local builders association.

None of that was created for the contest. It existed for 26 years before the domain did.

Built on WordPress with the Kadence theme.

### What the pages cover

- **Six services, each a separate job:** testing, mitigation systems, fan replacement and repair, sump pit sealing, crawl space systems, and radon help for a house sale.
- **Two of the six service pages carry the town in the page address.** The rest do not. The home page carries the town in its main heading.
- **No town pages at all.** The area served is listed in the site code as a city and the surrounding townships, several of them tied to their Wikipedia entries.
- **Six blog posts, each answering one question** a homeowner with an existing system would ask, such as what the gauge on the pipe shows or why the reading is still high. Each opens with a short direct answer.
- **No prices** on the home page.
- **The home page repeats the proof:** "since 1999" appears 11 times and the certification is mentioned about 10 times.

### What is in the code

- The business is described twice, once as a company and once as a home and construction business, with name, phone, street address and founding date.
- The state certification is marked up as a credential, with its number and the issuing department.
- The owner is marked up as a person holding the same certification.
- FAQ markup on the home page.
- Nine profile links: Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, Medium, ProvenExpert, Issuu, Substack, the Google Business Profile and the builders association listing.

## The comparison that makes the point

Blair W. also entered the contest with a new brand for the same radon search. That site never appeared in the top 40 on any check. His entered mold site finished at 21.

Same builder. Same weeks. Same search. One site had a 26 year old business behind it and one did not.

## What to take from it

1. **A real business needs far less site.** 24 pages reached number one on the harder search. The mold winner, with no history, built 119.
2. **Put the proof in the code, not only on the page.** The certification, the founding date, the address and the Google profile are all stated in the site's structured data.
3. **The footprint came first.** Listings and press about the business were ranking before the new domain appeared.
4. **A new domain is not a handicap.** This one was two days younger than the contest.
5. **It never needed town pages.** It named the towns it serves in the code and on the page.

Our reading: if a member can build for a real company with reviews, a licence and a history, that is the strongest position available. The Blueprint's market filter says the same thing from the other side: do not go up against a company like this with a new brand.
