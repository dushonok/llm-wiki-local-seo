THE WINNER'S PLAYBOOK: CONTENT PROMPTS
Rank Expand Academy

These prompts follow the content steps the contest winner described. They are ours, written to match his method. Replace the parts in square brackets.

======================================================================
1. BUILD THE OUTLINE
Run this once per page, starting with the home page.

I am writing a page to rank for "[main keyword]" in [town, state].
Below are the headings from the top five pages that rank for it today.

[paste the headings from each page, labelled Page 1 to Page 5]

List the topics that at least three of them cover. List the topics only one covers. List the questions a customer would ask that none of them answer.
Then write one outline for my page that covers all three lists, in the order a worried customer would want them.

======================================================================
2. PULL OUT THE SHARED LANGUAGE
This is the step he calls entities, n-grams, skip-grams and semantic keywords.

Below is the full text of the top five pages for "[main keyword]".

[paste the text of each page]

Give me four lists:
1. The people, places, tools, materials, organizations and rules these pages name.
2. The two to four word phrases that appear on three or more of the pages.
3. Word pairs that keep appearing near each other, even with other words between them.
4. Related terms a specialist would expect to see on a page about this.
For each item say how many of the five pages use it.

======================================================================
3. WRITE THE PAGE
Give Claude the outline and the four lists.

Write the page from this outline: [paste outline]
Work these terms in where they belong, and only where they are true for my business: [paste the four lists]
Write for a homeowner in [town] who has this problem today. Use plain words. Include real price ranges with a source. Do not invent reviews, awards, staff or licences.
The finished page must answer more of the customer questions than any of the five pages I gave you.

======================================================================
4. WRITE A NEIGHBORHOOD PAGE
One at a time. If the answer comes back generic, the page is not worth publishing.

Write a page for "[service] in [neighborhood or town]".
Use facts that are only true of this place: the age and type of the housing, the ground and the water, the local council or rules that apply, the landmarks and roads, and the distance from [main town].
If you cannot find at least five facts that would be wrong for the next town over, tell me, and do not write the page.

======================================================================
5. FIND PROBLEM BASED BLOG TOPICS
His blog targets problems, not services.

My site offers [service] in [town]. List 30 questions a homeowner types into Google when they first notice this problem and do not yet know they need a company.
Start each with the symptom, such as a smell, a stain or a sound. Group them by the room or part of the house. Mark the ones that are specific to [town] or to older homes there.
