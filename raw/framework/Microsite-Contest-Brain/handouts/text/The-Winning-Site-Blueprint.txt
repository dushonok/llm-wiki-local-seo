"Typical words" is the middle value for that page type. "Sections" is the number of main headings on a typical page.
The home page
The home page is the page that ranked. It runs to 6,805 words under 18 main headings, with 9 questions answered, 3 tables and 19 images. It links to 8 outside sources, including the borough and county websites, the EPA, the state attorney general and FEMA flood maps. The quote form at the top asks for six things: name, phone, email, town, service and a message.

How different the pages are from each other
We compared pages of the same type to see how much text they share. The town pages share about 29 percent. The articles share about 5 percent. In our audits, town pages built from a template usually share
50 to 70 percent.
What is in the code
The home page describes the business to Google in one block: name, phone, address, map position, opening hours, the towns served, a list of services, and links to 16 profiles. Most pages also mark up their questions and answers as FAQs.
How it was shipped
The site reached number one on October 26 with 47 pages. The next day it grew to 119 in a single update:
53 articles, 17 town pages and 3 guides. It dropped to 13, returned to number one five days later, and was not edited again.
Where it goes further than the Microsite Blueprint
The winner built more than the Blueprint asks for: a much longer home page, nine service pages, 36 town pages each written separately, a 58 article blog, and a street address shown on the site. One site is one result. The second place site had 18 pages and held page two for a month, so size alone does not explain it.
HOW WE KNOW
Figures come from a full copy of the site saved on October 27, which matched the live site through the end of the contest. Word counts cover the main content of each page and leave out menus and footers.
