Five Lessons from the Contest
What 61 brand new websites taught us about ranking locally
Thirty two members built 61 brand new websites for two Google searches: mold removal in Gettysburg and radon systems in York. We checked the rankings almost every day, always with the search location set to the town. The numbers here come from the 26 full checks between October 19 and November 17.
These are the five things the results showed again and again.

1
Your listings rank before your website does
WHAT WE SAW
On the final check, two listings built by contestants were on page one for the mold search: a MapQuest listing at 8 and a Patch listing at 9. Only one contest website was on that page with them.
Radon showed the same thing. A contestant's press release was at 6 and a MapQuest listing was at 13, while the best contest website was at 30.
The winner, Zeeshan Y., built 20 to 25 profiles and listings in his first days and added more every week.
The second place site listed none. It held page two for a month and never reached page one.
WHAT TO DO
Build your profiles in the first week, before you worry about where the site ranks. Use the exact same name, phone number and town on every one. If your listings are climbing and your site is not, you are early, not failing.
2
The market matters more than the build
WHAT WE SAW
The same people built both sets of sites with the same tools. Across those 26 checks, mold paid out points every single time. Radon paid out twice.
The winner's mold site finished at number one. His radon site finished at 34.
The difference was who already held page one. Mold in Gettysburg had room on it. Radon in York had a long established local company, the state's own radon page and trusted directories.
WHAT TO DO
Before you buy a domain, search the term with your location set to the town and read page one. If it is full of real local companies with years of reviews, pick another service or another town. You want a page one made of directories, out of town franchises and thin sites.
3
Google ranks the business, not the website
WHAT WE SAW
The number one radon result was a website on a domain registered on September 17, two days after the contest began. It first appeared at 7 on October 24 and was number one by November 4.
The reason is the business behind it: a York radon company that has operated since 1999 and has about
225 Google reviews.
Blair W. built that site. His own contest entry for the same search, a new brand with no history, never appeared in the top 40. Same builder, same weeks, same search.
WHAT TO DO
A new domain is not a handicap. A business that Google cannot find anywhere else is. Put the same name, phone number and town in many places so the business looks real, and where you can, build for a company that already has reviews and a history.
4
Build it deep, ship it in one go, then leave it alone
WHAT WE SAW
The winning site reached number one on October 26 with 47 pages. The next day it grew to 119 pages in a single batch, which its builder has since told us was an accident.
It dropped to 13, came back to number one on November 1, and stayed there for 17 checks in a row without a single edit to the site. In the last week Google was showing three of its pages in the top 20 at once.
Second place followed the same pattern. Cyril G. rebuilt his site once on October 15, never touched it again, and it sat between 13 and 17 for a month.
WHAT TO DO
Finish the site before you launch it, with a full page for each service and each town you serve. If you add more later, schedule it a few pages at a time. Then stop editing and give Google a few weeks to read it.
5
A jump is a test, not a ranking
WHAT WE SAW
Eleven mold sites scored points at some stage in the final month. Only two of them scored on more than six of the 26 checks.
One radon site jumped from 29 to 7 in a single day in October. Two days after its last page one appearance it was out of the top 40. In the final week the same site went 31, 23, 30, then 31, 25, 32.
Press does it too. A press release went from published to position 8 in two days and was gone on the third.
WHAT TO DO
When your site jumps, do nothing. Do not edit it, add pages or change the plan. Google is trying it out.
Judge a site by where it sits across two or three weeks, not by its best day.
HOW WE KNOW
Every number here comes from the same check: a search in a normal Chrome window with the location set to Gettysburg or
York and personalization turned off, counting only the regular website results. Early in the contest, searches made from a desk in another town showed contest sites 10 to 15 spots higher than a local searcher saw them. Check your own rankings the same way we did.
