The Content Method
How he studied the competition before writing a word
Zeeshan Y. did not ask Claude for a page and publish what came back. For every page, he first read what was already ranking and took notes. Claude wrote the final copy from those notes. This sheet walks through the method and gives you prompts to run it yourself.
1
Study the headings. For each keyword, open the pages that rank and read only their headings.
Then write your own outline.
2
Read the pages in detail. Pull out the terms and phrases they share. He lists entities, NLP, n-grams, skip-grams and semantic keywords.
3
Write to beat them. Use everything you found to write a page that covers more than the pages already ranking.
4
Repeat for every service page. The home page is not the only page that gets this treatment.
5
Let Claude write the final copy. From your outline and your notes, not from a one line request.
The jargon, in plain English
Entities
The real things a topic is made of: people, places, tools, materials, organizations and rules. For mold in Gettysburg that means things like HEPA filters, containment, the EPA, crawl spaces and
Adams County.
N-grams
Short runs of words that keep showing up together, such as "mold remediation cost" or "black mold removal".
Skip-grams
Word pairs that keep appearing near each other with other words in between, such as "moisture"
and "basement".
Semantic keywords
Related words and phrases Google expects to find around a topic, even though nobody searches for them directly.
NLP
Natural language processing. Here it just means using a tool, or Claude, to pull these lists out of competitor pages for you.
A NOTE ON "KOREY FRAMEWORKS"
He credits "Korey Frameworks" for this step. We read that as the semantic SEO method taught by Koray Tugberk Gubur, which is built around covering a topic and its entities completely. That is our reading, not his words.

How each cloud page is put together
It is a full homeowner guide, not a stub.
The five we could read run from about 1,100 to 2,200 words.
It answers one narrow problem.
The same kind of problem based topic his blog and his later social posts use.
It has one link to a service page, not the home page.
The link text is the name of that service page. Guest posts go to the home page. Cloud pages go deeper.
It links out to an official source.
The EPA on every page, plus the CDC or the borough website on some.
It links to his profiles as well.
Every page we read links to his Facebook, Pinterest, TikTok, YouTube, X, Houzz, Manta, Yelp and Quora profiles. The cloud pages hold up his listings as well as his site.

Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare Workers, Firebase, GitHub Pages and Streamlit.

Three Problems and How He Fixed Them
What went wrong on the number one site
Zeeshan Y. was open about what did not go to plan. Three things went wrong in the first six weeks. Each one is a problem we have also found on member sites, so each comes with a check you can run on your own.
1. The wrong page was ranking
What happened. His business name is Gettysburg Mold Remediation Pros. His main keyword is mold remediation Gettysburg PA. The name contains the keyword, so every page that used the name also repeated the keyword. When he checked on days 4 to 7, the About page and other pages were ranking for the main keyword instead of the home page.
What he did. He removed the brand keyword from every other page and kept it only on the home page.
Then he asked Google to index the site again. After that, the home page started ranking.
What we can see today. Only the home page leads its title and main heading with the keyword. The services page is titled "All Mold Services | Gettysburg Mold Pros", with the name shortened. The main heading on the contact page is just "Contact Us".
Check yours
Search your main keyword with the location pinned. Is the page that shows up your home page?
In Search Console: Performance, click the keyword, then open the Pages tab.
Does your business name contain your keyword?
If yes, use a shorter form of the name in the titles and headings of every page except the home page.
Does any other page share the home page main heading?
Home page and main town page with one shared heading is the most common fault in our member audits.
After you change it, ask Google to reindex the home page and the pages you changed.
Search Console: URL Inspection, then Request Indexing. Then wait 3 to 4 days.
2. Some neighborhood pages were too thin
What happened. A few of the neighborhood pages built on day two did not have enough to say.
What he did. He redirected 1 or 2 of them to the home page in the first week. He did not try to pad them out. The site has 36 area pages today.
Check yours
Read each town page and ask whether it could only be about that town.
If you can swap the town name and it still reads fine, it is too thin.
Redirect the ones you cannot fix to the home page or your service area page.
A permanent redirect. Do not leave the thin page live and do not just delete it.
3. More than 50 blog posts went live at once
What happened. He started the blog in week two with a handful of posts on problem based keywords.
Later, Claude wrote more than 50 more. They were meant to be scheduled. They were not, and they all went live together. He calls it "a big mistake".
What he did. Nothing. Some of the new posts were indexed the next day, so he left them all as they were.
What our checks show. The site went from 47 pages to 119 on October 27. It had reached number one the day before. For the next five days its best position was 13, 15 and 12. On November 1 it was back at number one and it stayed there. The timing fits, but a press release went out the same day and the site was only one day into its first number one, so we cannot say the batch caused the dip.
Check yours
Schedule new posts. Do not publish a batch in one go.
His first six posts went out over two and a half weeks, and the site climbed to number one with only those six.
Before you deploy, count the pages that are about to go live.
With Claude writing, it is easy to ship 50 pages when you meant to ship 5.
If a batch does go live by accident, leave it.
He did not delete or unpublish anything, and the site recovered in five days.
Do not judge the result for a week.
Position 13 on day two said nothing about where the site would be on day six.
One thing he did on purpose: he watched the competition
New sites showed up within days. Three to four days after launch, other exact match domain sites began to appear for the same keyword. In a contest that was always going to happen. In a normal market it happens more slowly, but it happens.
He studied them three times. In the first week, in week two and again after week three. Each time he made small updates based on what the new pages covered that his did not.
Then he stopped. After the freshness updates he made no more changes to the site. The last 25 days were all off-page work.
THE PATTERN IN ALL THREE
He checked early, made one small fix, and then left it alone. He did not rebuild the site at any point, including when it dropped out of the top ten for two weeks in October.
