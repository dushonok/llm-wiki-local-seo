When they were published
The first post went up on Sep 20, five days after the domain was registered. Five more followed, one every three or four days, with the last on Oct 7. Three of those were updated on October 16. Then nothing was added for almost three weeks.
On October 26 the site reached number one with those six posts. The next day, 53 new posts were published at once. He has since told us that was a mistake: they were meant to be scheduled. One of the original six, the cost article, was moved out of the blog and became its own page in the main menu.

How each post is built
The posts are close to a single recipe. A typical one runs to 1,859 words (the shortest is 1,549, the longest
2,493) under about 11 main headings.
Every post has a short FAQ section, marked up for Google. Most have 4 questions.
Every post has one image, and 56 of the 58 have at least one table.
58 of the 58 end with a "related guides" section and a call to request an estimate.
Each post links to about 8 other posts and 2 service pages.
So every article feeds readers, and Google, back toward the pages that sell.
Almost every post cites official sources. The EPA is cited in 54 of the 58, the IICRC in 20, the CDC in 19 and the state attorney general in 9.
Local posts also cite the borough, the county, FEMA flood maps and the National Park Service.
No two posts cover the same question. When we compared them, they shared only about 5 percent of their text.
Did the posts rank?
For the contest search, no. Across the 26 full checks, no blog post ever appeared in the top 30 for "mold remediation gettysburg pa". The pages Google showed from this site were the home page, the services page, the contact page and one service page.
For their own questions, yes. On November 18 we searched three topics the posts cover, with the location set to Gettysburg. The winner's post was the first result for all three:

What we can and cannot say
The big blog did not win the contest search. The site was already number one with six posts. After the 53 were added it dropped to 13 for five days, came back, and then held number one for 17 checks. So the posts did not hurt, and they may have helped it hold, but we cannot prove that from one site.
What the posts clearly did is give the site more searches to show up for. A site with only a home page can rank for one thing. This one now ranks first for questions its customers ask before they call anyone.
If you are planning a blog
Start with five or six posts that are truly local.
The winner's first six all named the town or the state.
Give each post one question, and put that question in the title.
Do not write a post on the same phrase as one of your service pages.
For "attic mold gettysburg pa" Google chose the service page and ignored the post.
Cite official sources in every post.
A federal agency, a trade standard, and a state or local source where one exists.
End every post with a short FAQ, links to related posts, and a link to the service it supports.
Add state and county posts on money and law.
The two we checked, on flood insurance and on contractor licensing, ranked first for their own searches.
If you add a large set, schedule it over a few weeks.
The winner has since told us his big batch went live at once by mistake. He left it alone and the dip lasted five days.
