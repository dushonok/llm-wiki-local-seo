Why Most Sites Never Scored
What happened to the 49 entries that finished the month without a point
Sixty one websites were entered. Twelve of them scored at least once in the last 26 checks. Forty nine did not. It is tempting to assume those 49 were simply worse websites. Most of them were not. They missed for reasons that had little to do with how well they were built.

Where the 49 went
22 were built for radon. These were live, eligible sites. Only one entered radon site scored all month, and it lasted two checks. Radon was a harder search, and a better build did not change that. More on this below.
13 had no working website. When we checked every entered domain on November 18, eight were not connected to any website, two showed a "coming soon" page, and three were parked, broken or offline.
More than one entry in five was never in the race.
12 were live mold sites that missed. This is the group where the build made the difference. The next page compares them with the mold sites that scored.
2 were entered late. One contestant registered both domains after the deadline. The mold site ranked in the scoring zone on five checks, but it could not earn points.

What separated them
The home page. Sites that scored had a home page almost twice as long. In this contest the home page was the page that ranked, so this is where depth counted.
Signs of a real business. Sites that scored were far more likely to list their profiles in the code and to show a street address. Those are the things that tell Google a business exists somewhere other than its own website.
Something to read. Sites that scored were more likely to have articles or guides as well as service pages.
Questions answered across the site. Most sites that scored marked up FAQs on the majority of their pages. Most that missed had them on only a few.
What did not separate them
The number of pages. The typical site had about 30 pages in both groups (33 for those that scored, 29 for those that did not). Building more pages did not get a site into the points by itself.
The number of town pages. Both groups had about the same. The three non scoring sites with the most town pages had 35, 28 and 16 of them. None of the three ever reached the top 20.
A phone number on the home page. Most sites in both groups had one. It is worth having, but it was not the difference.
How close the mold sites came
Missing the points did not mean being invisible. Of the 12 live mold sites that never scored, 7 showed up on page three at some point, with best positions from 22 to 30. Several of them sat there for most of the month. They were ranked. They were just a few places short.
Radon: good sites, wrong search
The 22 live radon sites were not weaker builds. One had 126 pages. Another listed 54 profiles in its code.
The typical one had 34 pages, about the same as the mold sites that scored. Their home pages were shorter, at about 1,900 words, but the strongest radon builds matched the mold sites that scored on almost every measure in the table.
It did not matter. Of the 22, 9 reached the top 40 at some point and none reached the top 20. Page one for radon was held by an established local company, the state's own radon page and a directory that covers only radon. What to take from it
Launch something.
13 of 61 entries never had a working site. A plain site that is live beats a perfect one that is not.
Choose the search before you build.
22 live sites were built for a search where no entry held a score. Look at page one first.
Put your depth into the home page.
It was the page that ranked, and it was almost twice as long on the sites that scored.
Make the business look real outside its own website.
Build profiles, list them in the site's code, and be consistent about who and where the business is.
Do not count pages.
More pages and more town pages did not separate the sites that scored from the ones that did not.
Enter on time.
One site ranked well enough to score on five checks and earned nothing.
HOW WE KNOW, AND WHAT TO BE CAREFUL ABOUT
Scoring is from the 26 full checks between October 19 and November 17. Site details come from copies of each site saved during the contest; one scoring site could not be captured and is left out of the comparison table, as are two small live sites we did not save. Whether a domain had a working site was checked on November 18 and confirmed from outside our own network.
The groups are small, about ten sites each, so read the table as a pattern from one contest and not as proof. No non scoring site or owner is named here on purpose.
