56 - Directory Blueprint as Force Multiplier

56:33

This week centered on Shawn's new Directory Blueprint build: how it works, what it costs, how to monetize it, and why it doubles as a force multiplier for the community's microsite game. We also covered member wins and keeping Claude's SEO advice grounded.

## Things mentioned

- Claude Code: builds the Directory Blueprint site end to end, including schema and service tagging
- DataForSEO: pulls the Google Maps listing data; chosen over Outscraper
- Formspree: current form handler routing "claim this listing" submissions to an inbox
- Stripe: discussed as the likely payment layer for claimed or premium listings, not yet built
- A Notion or Airtable to Cloudflare pipeline: seen in a video, used to host and update directory images
- [KIE.AI](http://kie.ai/ "http://KIE.AI"): a cheaper aggregator for AI ad graphics, mentioned as an alternative to Higgsfield
- Vapi and Twilio: the stack behind one member's "Alex" voice assistant running across his microsites
- A Wiki LLM referenced through a [CLAUDE.md](http://claude.md/ "http://CLAUDE.md") file: keeps Claude's SEO answers grounded in vetted call recordings and pulled transcripts

## What we covered

Shawn showed the raw output of the Directory Blueprint: around 1,500 Google Maps listings, enriched and schema tagged automatically, built in roughly 45 minutes to an hour and a half for under $100 all in, domain included. The site runs 28 megabytes and scores near 98 on page speed. Blair broke down monetization from his own agency work: selling featured or top three listings (he runs his at $95 a year), writing "best XYZ" articles that catch citation searches, and calling other SEO companies whose clients get submitted into your directory as a sales opener.

The group didn't think putting your own name or agency down as publisher creates a PBN style footprint risk the way running multiple microsites does. A directory is normal for a publisher to run, and it lets you build authority in one place, citations, press, a featured article, then distribute that down to your own microsites without the exposure of running microsites alone.

Images are the open question. Because Google restricts server side storage of things like Street View imagery, the current lean is AI generated images (about 1,700 unique images for roughly $200). Blair's counterpoint: for higher effort outreach directories, VAs pull real images off a business's Facebook page or Google Business Profile, since real photos read as more credible when trying to get someone on the phone. On data sourcing, the team tested Outscraper against DataForSEO on the same query, found results within about a percentage point of each other, and went with DataForSEO since it's roughly 17 times cheaper and keeps new members in the same tool they already use for the research assistant.

Wins were strong: Bryan audited a PR firm's directory (no schema, broken meta tags, a paywall conflicting with public URLs) and sent a $5,000 proposal. Blair lost a longtime client to a required national SEO vendor, scraped that client's competitors with Claude, and landed all three in one day at $5,000 a month each. Robert joined the day before the call and had already built six microsites with Claude for his weather triggered service business. On grounding, Shawn showed how the community's Wiki LLM only pulls from vetted sources through a [CLAUDE.md](http://claude.md/ "http://CLAUDE.md") file, so answers come from that material rather than the open internet.
