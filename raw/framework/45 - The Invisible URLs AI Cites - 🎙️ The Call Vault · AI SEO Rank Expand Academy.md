#45 - The Invisible URLs AI Cites

58:38

This one came straight out of Blair's post: how do you actually get AI to cite you? We went deep on the tactics working right now in local SEO, and Jesse shared a method for finding the exact pages AI trusts that no backlink tool can see.

## Things mentioned

- Google Search Console and Google Analytics, for the query and traffic data everything is built on.
- DataForSEO, to check which pages Gemini and ChatGPT cite for a query (pulling AI results costs money, so run it once or monthly, not daily).
- Claude Code and Fable, to build the citation dashboard. Jesse turned it into a build prompt and posted it in the classroom.
- The query fan-out bookmarklet, the manual version that shows what ChatGPT cites for a single search.
- Splashdash, for keyword and data aggregation across multiple searches.
- Semrush, for backlink and ranking data.
- Bradley Benner (Semantic Mastery), for cheap backlink analysis (about $50-60), full local audits (about $200), and a mastermind. A great way to learn links on someone else's dime.
- Semantic Links ([semanticlinks.io](http://semanticlinks.io/ "http://semanticlinks.io")), for local SEO audits. [https://semanticlinks.io/](https://semanticlinks.io/ "https://semanticlinks.io/")
- WhitePress ([whitepress.com](http://whitepress.com/ "http://whitepress.com")), a marketplace of sites that will take payment for a link. [https://www.whitepress.com/en/](https://www.whitepress.com/en/ "https://www.whitepress.com/en/")
- SiteWise ([site-wise.io/seo-and-geo](http://site-wise.io/seo-and-geo "http://site-wise.io/seo-and-geo")), one of the "get found in AI" tools to keep an eye on. [https://site-wise.io/seo-and-geo](https://site-wise.io/seo-and-geo "https://site-wise.io/seo-and-geo")
- Replit and Flowcode, for fast static site builds and hosting under the $100 credit umbrella.
- Pexels API with Claude Code, so the AI can pull and vet stock images with vision.
- HeyGen and Julie McCoy's approach, for affiliate YouTube videos that earn citations.
- Lasso and Amazon, for affiliate product sites.
- Upwork and Fiverr, for client work and outsourcing (skip the cheap Fiverr backlink packages).

## What we covered

The big question was how to trigger AI citations and overviews, because clients are walking into meetings asking about it. The foundation everyone agreed on is content clustering plus real FAQ pages, fed by press releases and honest local citations. One member's client is already getting calls off ChatGPT just from clustering the content the way it is taught and keeping the blog fresh, which itself started getting cited.

Then Jesse walked through his method. Take your top Search Console queries, turn them into questions, and use DataForSEO to see which pages Gemini and ChatGPT cite for each one. Do it across 50 queries and a pattern appears: the same handful of URLs get cited over and over, and most of them are invisible to Ahrefs. Those are the pages to get listed on, sometimes for as little as $50. He built it in Claude Code and posted the Fable build prompt in the classroom.

On the local side, Blair walked through citations that do not need expensive links. He buys expired domains from old charity 5Ks and fun runs for $20 to $50 and lists his clients as sponsors. He also flagged that AI can tell a self-bought press release, so the move is a listicle on an unrelated site under a different author, which reads as third-party and gets trusted. In a low-competition niche you may not need backlinks at all.

A big stretch was about getting off the solo treadmill. Hire and train VAs early, make Loom videos, and become the person who manages the work instead of doing it. Shlomi showed what momentum looks like: two clients in one day plus a referral, after he re-engineered his Upwork profile to match the exact clients he wanted. And the reminder that keeps returning is that clients stay for the relationship, not because nothing ever breaks.

We closed on the practical stack. Pricing ranged from a $650 a month automated GMB program up past $5,000 for full builds, and the group agreed backlinks are the cherry on top once the technical foundation is solid. Bradley Benner's cheap audits are the place to learn links. On builds, Blair reverses the flow (build in Claude Code, drop the zip into Replit, host in minutes), and Jesse showed how to add images with the Pexels API and how to mash the top 10 competitor designs into a layout that does not look templated.
