53 - Scaling Microsites and SEO Strategies

1:00:37

The tools have gotten good enough that building a microsite is nearly free and nearly instant. So this call was about everything that happens after the site is live: keeping the client, pricing the work, and managing a portfolio once you have a lot of them.

## Things mentioned

- GPT-6 Astra, OpenAI's new model, mentioned as fast ([https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/ "https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/")).
- Astro, the static site framework several members build client sites on ([https://astro.build/](https://astro.build/ "https://astro.build/")).
- Majestic, for checking a domain's link profile and trust before you buy it ([https://majestic.com/](https://majestic.com/ "https://majestic.com/")).
- DataForSEO, for keyword and history data, tied into Claude Code ([https://dataforseo.com/](https://dataforseo.com/ "https://dataforseo.com/")).
- Grasshopper, for phone numbers with custom caller ID ([https://grasshopper.com/](https://grasshopper.com/ "https://grasshopper.com/")).
- Bluio, an iOS-emulator texting app for outreach that slips past spam filters ([https://blooio.com/](https://blooio.com/ "https://blooio.com/")).
- Also named: Claude Code and Codex for building, GoHighLevel for the retention and reporting layer, HL Pro Tools for agency client support, SpamZilla for expired domains, Hermes for a self-built tool stack, ZimmWriter for content, Cloudflare and Twilio, and a Claude SEO-writer skill the team has coming.

## What we covered

The first half was about the money model. Jesse shared a test he had not talked about before: a low-cost site sold on a simple monthly plan, which pencils out beautifully until churn eats it. That led to a longer talk about retention. A cheap site is easy to cancel, so the members keeping clients are the ones showing a simple number every month, more reviews, more leads, better rankings, plus small ongoing touches like monthly blog posts and rewritten Google review responses. GoHighLevel does most of that automatically.

On pricing, the room landed on the same idea from two directions: commoditize and go low with a dead-simple offer, or go premium with real management. Med spa and plastic surgery clients will pay five to six hundred a month per site and expect hands-on work, sold in groups that circle their location. The middle is the trap.

We also got into the two hard parts of doing this at volume. Replication is basically solved, you build a few style templates per niche and let Claude swap in each client's details, but managing a hundred or a thousand sites needs a real pipeline and a layer that tracks them all, which is what we are building toward. And the niche you pick decides a lot: some clients own the relationship and will fire you once they get busy, some niches need a license before you can even generate the lead, and some are getting real regulatory heat right now.

We closed on outreach tactics for the members who still have to get businesses on the phone, and on the tools people use to research expired domains before they buy.
