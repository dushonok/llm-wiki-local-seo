#46 - Build It First, Then Get Paid

56:49

A newcomer-heavy call that turned into a working session on how to actually start. Three first-timers laid out where they are stuck, and the same answer kept coming back: build the thing before you ask for the work.

### Things mentioned

- GoHighLevel, as the comparison point for an all-in-one lead gen offer (typically around $297 a month).
- ChatGPT self-serve ads, now open. Still bedding in, no impressions in our test yet.
- Claude Code, for site builds, client dashboards, and automations.
- Google Search Console API, to give Claude your client's real data.
- The Knowledge Wiki LLM setup, so Claude keeps durable memory of a client and follows your rules.
- Cloudflare Pages, for hosting and server-side environment variables.
- Replit, for the agent plus a Secrets tab (the equivalent of your.env file).
- DataForSEO and Cloudflare Turnstile, as examples of keys that belong in secrets.
- Mailgun, so Claude can email the client every update it makes.
- Stripe, for client subscriptions. Wise, for cross-border currency conversion.
- Upwork, for landing a first client.
- n8n, for workflow automation.

### What we covered

We opened on Sandra's question: how do you package an all-in-one growth system for tradies, one price to set up and one monthly, covering everything? The reference point is the GoHighLevel offer at around $297 a month. The advice was to sell low or sell high and avoid the middle, and there is a good example of a community charging $1,500 a month just to teach owners to do it themselves, which is more than the done-for-you version costs. Adding email marketing works well if you also teach the client how to tag, segment, and warm up their list.

The heart of the call was three first-timers. Razi is a Dallas commercial real estate broker sitting on 20 years of domains, and a broker in his co-working space wants to buy leads after his old supplier went under. The commercial close cycle is six months to two years, so a commission-only deal could mean a year without income. Jesse's answer was to have the broker prepay about a thousand dollars for three microsites, hand him every lead for year one, and keep ownership of the sites while learning the build on his dime. Shawn added a hybrid: get cash flow first, then lease the sites and add a rev share later.

John is technical, already builds automations, and is stuck on client acquisition. Same answer. Pick a business you already know, build the finished site before you ask for anything, and show it to them. Almost nobody does that, and it is a few hours of work now. He also worried about working the US from Ontario, and the room was unanimous that face to face is optional. Shawn has run US clients from the Netherlands for years without meeting one in person.

Then the money. Lakshmi landed her first Upwork client and is converting them to $1,500 a month direct. The advice was to charge upfront, always, put it on a Stripe subscription, and price in US dollars because the currency swing is not your problem. From there Jesse pushed the scaling frame: decide you will spend a maximum of three hours a month on a client, then build the system that makes it true. Give Claude access to Search Console, FTP, and a private repo, let it write and revise and run on crons, and add Mailgun so it emails the client its own updates. Three hours on a $1,500 client is $500 an hour.

Two more worth catching. Shlomi watched 400 Google reviews vanish from the web interface and nearly blamed his GMB tool, before discovering they were all still visible on the Maps interface. It is a known display bug, not a deletion. And on security: never put an API key in the browser, because anyone can read it in the inspector. Keep it server side in Cloudflare Pages variables or a Replit secrets tab.
