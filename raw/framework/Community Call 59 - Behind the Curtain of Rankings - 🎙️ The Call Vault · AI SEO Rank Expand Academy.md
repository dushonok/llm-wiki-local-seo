## What we covered

New-member intros ran from a member already at 307 microsites breaking down his costs to a 10-year lead gen owner layering microsites onto his brands, a former tattoo artist turned directory builder, and a member asking if the US playbook works in Europe (yes, with adaptation).

A big chunk went to GBP verification, prompted by a member whose brand-new EMD ranked #1 in the map pack and AI Overview but got few organic clicks. The verification routine working for one member: no street number, a brand-new SIM in a factory-reset phone, three to four weeks after indexing before requesting verification, run as a service-area business. Twilio-forwarded numbers work too, and verified GBPs sell for roughly $300-500.

On NAP and EMD, the group agreed NAP matters a lot, and EMD helps but isn't everything: the member currently leading the group's microsite contest is winning without a perfect EMD. Jesse was candid about how much real-world local SEO runs on tactics nobody advertises: PBNs, cloaked pages, and his own network of sites feeding "best of" articles. No-follow social links don't hurt, and leads come straight from social.

When a heat pump client asked for his own site instead of buying leads, the advice was to keep the asset: charge for the build, lock in a minimum term, add a buyout clause. One member shared his actual contract structure (he owns the domains, six-month minimum, a 14-day buyout window after cancellation, about $1,500 per site across seven sites).

To filter a scraped Outscraper list down to businesses that actually take personal injury cases, run it through Claude Code or the cheaper DataForSEO. For directories, the group favors state, county, or named-region level over hyper-local.
