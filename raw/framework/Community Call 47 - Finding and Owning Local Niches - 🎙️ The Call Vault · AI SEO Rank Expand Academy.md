#47 - Finding and Owning Local Niches

1:02:52

Shawn hosted this one solo while Jesse was away, and it turned into a practical run through how to spot a niche worth building and how much of the ranking machine you can now build yourself for pocket change.

## Things mentioned

- The Niche Validator tool, as a starting point for checking a niche.
- Local Services Ads, as a demand signal (Google charges roofers $100 to $250 a call, and it never shows in CPC data).
- Radaar ([radaar.io](http://radaar.io/ "http://radaar.io")), for scheduling and auto-posting social content.
- HeyGen, for avatar video, with the tutorial at [youtube.com/watch?v=KC9G5\_XfYlY](http://youtube.com/watch?v=KC9G5_XfYlY "http://youtube.com/watch?v=KC9G5_XfYlY").
- ChatGPT, for image edits and ad creative, and its self-serve ads (~$3 a click).
- vidIQ, CapCut, and Camtasia, for placement research and cutting shorts.
- GoHighLevel, Aweber, and AdSense, for the funnel, follow-up, and blog revenue.
- Omega Indexer, to force-index your profile and citation pages.
- Replit, Cloudflare Pages, and Hostinger VPS (~$24 to $29 a month), for building and hosting static sites and running Claude Code off a server.
- DataForSEO (now pay as you go), the engine behind a self-built rank tracker for about $10 a client.
- Ubersuggest MCP, for content ideas and quick wins.
- Semantic Mastery on YouTube, for the profile-page citation tactic.
- NFC and QR review tap cards ([abcrfid.com](http://abcrfid.com/ "http://abcrfid.com")), for review velocity.
- ProvenExpert, Yelp, Facebook, and LinkedIn, for review and trust signals.
- Fiverr, for cheap citations and real-person videos.

## What we covered

We opened on a good question from Sarah: what are the first three things you check before building a niche? The room's shortlist was demand, a high enough ticket price, and whether companies are already paying for the leads, which you can see in the map pack. Blair added the ones no tool shows you, regional oddities like butted-glass corner windows on old ocean condos or shed removal, found by sitting in local Facebook groups and noticing the same unanswered question. Fred pointed out Local Services Ads as a tell, since Google charges roofers $100 to $250 a call, so if the ads are running the money is there.

Bryan gave the walkthrough of the call. He built an authority site on one of his domains, 754 pages indexed, with AI avatar videos, six months of social posts auto-scheduled through Radaar, and an upsell ladder behind a quiz funnel. He wrote around 300 articles for about $15 and got his citations from a Fiverr seller. His line was that the website is not the point anymore, the system is.

Shlomi went the other direction, on cost. Tired of paying for SEMrush and Accuranker, he fed a seven-month-old DataForSEO tutorial to Claude Code and had it build his own rank tracker and AI-citation dashboard, the hidden-links technique baked in, for about $10 a client a month. The room told him to sell it, since DataForSEO is now pay as you go.

There was a real thread on private equity too. Justin is packaging up a water treatment business, and the group talked through helping owners prep for sale, and even negotiating a slice of equity for driving the valuation, with Seth's owned rank-and-rent model as the example. Underneath it all was a reminder that the boring stuff still prints, SEO plus city and web design plus city, and half the competitors never build the local pages.

We closed on reviews and trust. A verified-review showcase page reads as case studies you can push around the web, review velocity matters more than count, and NFC tap cards get field techs collecting reviews on the spot. On microsites, real content and real videos now beat AI avatars, because Google is deranking AI video, and new members Andrew and Yoav shared where they are starting, including a niche affiliate site already ranking on the first page in Israel.
