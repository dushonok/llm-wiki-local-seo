#50 - Winter Is Coming, Build Now

59:00

We tried a new format this week: drop your questions in the chat and we work through them. It turned into a strong Q&A on structuring sites, filling your funnel, pricing your work, and turning a pile of domains into something that actually pays.

- Facebook Ads Library ([https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/ "https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/")), which Jesse called the way to "get 100% clarity on anyone's Facebook ads."

## What we covered

Steven asked how to structure microsites for a complex medical practice. The answer was to skip the general term, since the practice already has a Google Business Profile you won't beat, and go after the specific services people actually type. For managing a lot of sites, Jesse's stack is Cloudflare Pages with Claude Code and a GitHub repo, so he can point at any one site and change it without touching the rest.

Tom asked what fills the top of the funnel. Tony Hill, who built Pin Clicks, shared how he grew by being useful in someone else's community first, then Twitter, then a newsletter. Mark walked through his own play: cheap Facebook video-view ads on pain-point content, building a custom audience of the people who actually watch, then waterfalling them toward an offer, so people come to him instead of him cold calling.

Nadya asked whether it's ethical to bill hourly when the AI is doing the work. The room was clear that you're paid for value, not time. Someone told the old FedEx story, a dollar to turn the screw and nine thousand nine hundred ninety nine for knowing which screw, and the takeaway was to stop billing hourly and charge per job.

Someone sitting on a domain portfolio and asked how to sell it. The honest answer was that a raw domain is worth what you paid for it, and the value is in the leads. Build the site, get it ranking, put a voice agent on it, and then you have an asset a contractor will pay for, sold with a no-brainer offer rather than a pitch about exact-match domains.

We closed on the practical stack. Mark researches the schema the top-ranking sites use and takes the best of all of them. The group agreed GHL is a great CRM but not the way to build, that you should always keep revision control so an AI can't nuke your site for good, and that the way to track leads at scale is to have Claude Code build you a dashboard, the way Mark wired Twilio and Retell together to log every call. Jesse's parting line was the theme of the whole call: winter is coming, it's dirt cheap to build right now, so build.
